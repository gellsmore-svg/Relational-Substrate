# Preface

A discipline can be exact in its own domain and still leave the deepest questions about reality open. Physics is the clearest case. It predicts with astonishing precision, and nothing in this book disputes that. But a theory can predict well without telling us why its entities are compatible with one another at all, why the same grammar of interaction holds everywhere we look, why the world is mathematically describable, or how living things and persons belong to the same order as stones. These are not questions physics has failed at. They are questions its success leaves open.

This book asks them together. Its question is not whether science works. It is what kind of created reality can make everything we know, physical, living, personal, moral and revealed, belong coherently to one world.

Its answer is that created physical reality is **relational** from its foundation. Things are not first made as sealed objects and then connected. They are constituted within an order of relation that God made and upholds, which the project calls the **Relational Substrate**. Matter is fully real but not foundational. *That* matter derives from a foundation that is not itself material is held as a theological premise; *how* materiality arises within such an order is the book's central question. Two further proposals give the answer shape: what actually happens is drawn, with real chance, from what created order makes possible and favours; and a difference anywhere becomes consequential elsewhere through one recurring grammar, **transmit, carry, receive**.

Scripture governs the whole. The biblical distinctions between Creator and creature, dust and breath, body and person, corruption and restoration set boundaries the ontology must respect.

## How the argument is judged

The standard is **global coherence**: a proposal about what exists must hold together across every domain it touches, without changing the meaning of its terms, without unexplained interfaces, and without breaking one domain to fix another. The standard runs both ways. The book must not contradict observation, measurement or successful mathematics. Equally, the picture usually carried along with physics, independent objects in a container of space, is itself a proposal, tested the same way.

The book labels its claims: Scriptural or theological constraint; empirical observation; mathematical or physical constraint; ontological inference; Relational Substrate (RS) working hypothesis; mechanism candidate; open question; rejected. A **model result** comes from the project's computer models; it can show an idea is possible and workable, not that nature works that way. A status table at the end of this edition gathers the main claims.

No measurement could display the deepest structure of created reality directly, so the warrant offered is that of a global coherence argument, not of experiment. The book states its hypothesis clearly enough to be wrong, says why it is believed, what supports it and what would count against it, and follows its consequences.

## About this edition

This edition is for readers comfortable with structured argument, such as students of philosophy, religious studies or physics at about A-level. Technical terms are defined at first use. One scene recurs as a reference point: seeing a stone on a path in morning light.

Earlier editions relied on specific mechanisms that later work did not support, including an "ontological stack", a "transformation tier" and a knotted field structure called the vorton presented as settled. This edition does not rely on them. The vorton and related structures remain **mechanism candidates**, named and not relied on.

The research behind the book is the work of one author, carried out with extensive computational and AI-assisted exploration, and its records are public. A free electronic edition is available at <https://relational-substrate.blogspot.com/>, with the research repository at <https://github.com/gellsmore-svg/Relational-Substrate>.

# Chapter 1: Scripture and the World That Is There

It is a clear morning, and a stone lies on the path. You see its grey surface and a pale vein of mineral. You could pick it up and find it in your pocket tomorrow, still the same stone. What is it, at the deepest level we can reach? How does the light bring it to you? Why is it the same stone tomorrow? And who is the one who sees it? The scene proves nothing. It was chosen because every idea the book needs is present in it. First, though, a prior decision: where does inquiry begin?

## Scripture speaks first

> `All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works.`
> *2 Timothy 3:16-17*

The book receives Scripture's claim to authority, in the form Scripture actually takes. Genesis is narrative, theological and liturgical; it is neither a laboratory report nor a hidden vocabulary for physics. Scripture does not overstep its own truthfulness, but human interpretation can, on either side. Apparent collisions between the Bible and science usually lie in a human extension: a text pressed beyond what it says, or a metaphysical conclusion drawn beyond what was measured.

"Scripture first" therefore means that Scripture sets the **boundaries** within which an account of reality may be built. A proposal that contradicts Scripture's teaching about creation is excluded however elegant; a proposal Scripture neither teaches nor forbids may be explored, but never presented as biblical teaching. Because the Relational Substrate is a modern proposal, four relations are kept apart:

1. **Scripture teaches** something (the world is created, held, good, and distinct from God).
2. The proposal **is consistent with** what Scripture teaches.
3. The proposal offers **a possible mechanism compatible with** a scriptural claim, without Scripture teaching the mechanism.
4. Scripture **provokes a question** the proposal explores.

Only the first sets binding boundaries. The others never borrow its authority.

## A world that is really there

> `In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth. And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.`
> *Genesis 1:1-3*

The sequence matters. The heaven and the earth are created in verse 1; in verse 2 the earth, the deep and the waters already exist, unformed; only then is light spoken. Genesis 1:1 is best read as a real beginning, and what follows as formation, separation and filling *within* an existing world.

That bears on the darkness of verse 2. Two things are plain in the text: it is not nothing, since the earth and waters are there with it, and it is not presented as evil. From the sequence the book infers, and labels the inference, that this darkness is a real condition of a real world not yet manifest in the visible order. If darkness were nothing, light would be the arrival of existence itself, and the unseen would seem less real than the seen. Genesis forbids that inference. **Visibility is not the measure of reality.**

> `Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear.`
> *Hebrews 11:3*

This text will matter again when the book asks what the stone is made of. Whatever is deepest in created order is likely to be known indirectly, through what it does.

## The Creator and the creature

> `For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible... And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.`
> *Colossians 1:16-17*

The Creator–creature distinction is the most important boundary in the book, and it cuts both ways. Created things are **not divine**: nothing in creation is God or part of God. And created things are **not self-explaining**: nothing exists or coheres by its own power. "By him all things consist": creation is coherent because it is held.

This boundary will be tested, because the Relational Substrate is proposed as the deepest created order of the physical world, and such an order is easily mistaken for something more than created: a cosmic mind, a world-soul, a substitute for God. Scripture closes that door before the proposal is stated. The substrate, if it exists, is a **creature**: not divine essence, not intelligent, not a rival principle and not the source of its own order.

## Kinds of reality

The Hebrew verb *bara*, used only with God as subject, marks three thresholds in Genesis 1: the beginning of everything (1:1), animate life (1:21) and humanity (1:27). The book does not rest its argument on one word, but the pattern is clear: creation is not one flat category. Genesis 2:7 shows the inner structure of the last threshold:

> `And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.`
> *Genesis 2:7*

The book calls kinds of reality **categories**: the material, the living, the personal, the spiritual. God is not a category within creation but its Creator. Categories matter because the book will claim that relation runs through every category, and a careless reader or author could slide from "relation appears everywhere" to "everything is the same kind of thing". That slide would be a serious error.

## The same word is not the same thing

Genesis 1:3 speaks of created light; Christ says "I am the light of the world" (John 8:12); John writes "God is light" (1 John 1:5). It would be absurd to conclude that God is made of the light of Genesis 1:3. Yet the shared word is not accidental: created light discloses and makes visible, and so, in another order, does Christ. They share a *form* of relation, not a *nature*.

The book distinguishes four ways categories may be related, and says which it means each time:

- **Structural analogy**: a likeness of form between different kinds, as between created light and Christ's making-manifest.
- **Representation**: a created form that, by design, shows something of the spiritual order. Creation was made to disclose: "the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made" (Romans 1:20). It instructs without being conscious of doing so.
- **Causal connection**: within one human person, between an embodied act and its moral reality. A lie is spoken with a tongue and is also a wrong; the two are connected, not identical.
- **Ontological identity**: sameness of kind across categories. The book rejects it.

## What Scripture leaves open

Scripture's boundaries exclude a good deal: a world that is eternal or self-made, a world divine in whole or part, matter as evil or illusory, darkness as a co-eternal rival to light. (The full edition's Appendix A sets out the historical positions these exclude.) But Scripture leaves much open, including the deepest structure of matter, and it commends the search: "It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter" (Proverbs 25:2).

Some of its language provokes questions without answering them. God "stretcheth out the heavens as a curtain" (Isaiah 40:22). Such language sits uneasily with space as an empty container, and invites the question of what extension is. It does not supply a geometry, and the book does not extract one. Scripture constrains and provokes.

## What this chapter establishes

- Scripture sets binding boundaries; the RS proposal is kept distinct from Scripture's teaching by four explicit relations.
- Creation is real from the beginning; visibility is not the measure of reality.
- The Creator–creature distinction is absolute: the substrate, if it exists, is a creature.
- Categories are distinct; four relations across categories are distinguished, and ontological identity is rejected.

# Chapter 2: How We Come to Know What Is There

Physics knows how gravity *behaves* with extraordinary precision, from a falling stone to the relativistic corrections in satellite navigation. What gravity *is*, a force, the curvature of a substantival spacetime, or a pattern of relations among events, remains disputed among people who accept every measurement, and how gravity fits with quantum theory is unknown. That gap between describing behaviour and knowing what something is shapes everything in this book. This chapter assembles the tools the rest of the book uses, in order: the layers of knowing; what behaviour can and cannot reveal; the rule against elastic explanation; coherence as the standard of judgment; comparison without numbers; and a bounded account of logic, inference and proof. Each later chapter draws on them, so it is worth returning here when one is invoked.

## Six layers of knowing

1. **Observation**: the stone falls.
2. **Measurement**: numbers by a defined procedure (mass, time).
3. **Mathematical relation**: distance fallen grows with the square of the time.
4. **Physical model**: a force between masses, or a geodesic in curved spacetime.
5. **Ontological interpretation**: what must actually be there for the behaviour to occur.
6. **Worldview**: the background in which that interpretation feels natural.

Each layer depends on those before it, but is not guaranteed by them. Newtonian gravitation predicted superbly for two centuries before general relativity replaced its picture of instantaneous action across empty space, while preserving most of its predictions. Quantum theory sharpens the point: one formalism, confirmed with great precision, is read as fundamentally indeterministic on some interpretations and as deterministic (with hidden variables or branching worlds) on others, all agreeing on every measurement so far. **A successful description can be joined to more than one ontology.**

## Behaviour and intrinsic nature

A programmer can call a *private function* without reading its code, and by systematic testing learn a great deal about it. But different code could produce the same outputs for every input tried. Knowledge of **consequential behaviour** is genuine; it is not, by itself, knowledge of **intrinsic ontology**. Mathematics offers a precise case: in 1992 Gordon, Webb and Wolpert constructed two differently shaped drums that sound exactly the same notes. The sound tells you much about the drum, and not everything.

Observability and understanding answer different questions. A question may stand in four **respects of accessibility**:

1. **Mechanistic explanation**: we can account for how specified processes produce an outcome.
2. **Relational understanding without a complete mechanism**: we genuinely understand what trust is, or why a promise binds, without an exhaustive account of how it operates.
3. **Present investigative limits**: beyond current instruments or methods, but possibly accessible later.
4. **Possible limits of exhaustive creaturely comprehension**: never fully comprehended by a creature, though partial understanding remains possible.

These are respects in which questions are approached, not kinds of reality. Observability cuts across them: atoms were inferred long before they were imaged, and quantum theory limits what can be learned from a single unknown system, yet unobservability does not forbid inference from consequences. Two errors are avoided: declaring something permanently inaccessible without reason, and assuming that every underlying reality will eventually be observed as one more object. The governing principle: **a successful description of observable consequences does not, by itself, establish the ultimate ontological nature of their cause.**

## Every account starts somewhere

Every ontology contains assumptions beyond measurement, whether materialist, religious or this book's. **Methodological naturalism**, the working rule of explaining natural phenomena by natural causes, is sound practice. **Metaphysical naturalism**, the claim that nothing exists except the natural world, does not follow from it; a metal detector cannot discover that there is no wood. The same discipline binds theology: quoting Scripture does not make an argument rigorous if the step from text to conclusion is unexplained.

Assumptions fail characteristically through **metaphysical elasticity**: assigning additional, insufficiently justified properties to an explanatory entity whenever needed to preserve an interpretation, without acknowledging the added assumptions or the evidence they would require. The author's informal name is the *Harry Potter problem*: in a world of spells, an unseen cause can be produced for anything. It occurs when a successful mathematical description is taken, without separate argument, as evidence that its entities are fundamental substances, and when a word is widened to rescue a thesis: if "matter" covers whatever physics finds, materialism cannot be wrong and has stopped saying anything. It could occur in this book. The rule binds the Relational Substrate as strictly as its rivals; hence fixed commitments, stated defeaters (Chapter 9) and priced additions.

## Coherence: local, regional, global

Without direct measurement of ontology, accounts are judged by **coherence**: not mere consistency, but connection with real distinctions preserved.

- **Local coherence**: a model explains a bounded phenomenon.
- **Regional coherence**: neighbouring domains integrate (chemistry with atomic physics, molecular biology with chemistry).
- **Global coherence**: the whole account remains mutually compatible: physics, life, consciousness and personhood, morality, history and, for a Christian account, Scripture.

Global coherence adds tests beyond non-contradiction:

- **real connection** between domains;
- **valid interfaces** where one kind of reality meets another;
- **meaning preserved** through explanatory levels;
- **stability under repair**;
- the **round trip**, in which a claim taken through other domains and back returns substantially unchanged;
- **accountable kinds of knowing**, so that observation, mathematics, philosophical inference, personal encounter and revelation each contribute without silently overruling the others.
 Throughout, real distinctions must survive: Creator and creature, analogy and identity, probability and arbitrariness, a person and a configuration. Connection is not reduction.

The book's central methodological thesis: **local explanatory success is not sufficient evidence that an ontology is true if that ontology produces contradiction elsewhere.** Chapter 10 argues for it.

**Coherence runs both ways.** The RS proposal is finished if it contradicts robust observation. But the conventional metaphysics of physics, space as container, independent objects, laws as rules things obey, belongs to layers 4 to 6. It gains no evidential weight from familiarity or consensus, and it is held to the same standard.

## Comparing without numbers

Three judgments are distinct: **logical possibility** (no identified contradiction), **qualitative plausibility** (more or less plausible given observations, assumptions and alternatives), and **quantitative probability** (a number under a specified model). The absence of the third does not invalidate the second. Passing a hand along an electric fence at one centimetre is more likely to end in contact than at one metre; other factors bear on both passes alike, so the ordering holds across every reasonable variation of them. The mathematics of comparative probability shows that "more probable than" can be reasoned about rigorously without numbers. Comparisons are therefore **robust** (surviving all reasonable variation), **conditional** (holding under stated assumptions) or **unavailable**. An account is not improbable merely for being complicated, and global coherence concerns how naturally an account explains observed relationships without unnecessary independent assumptions.

## Logic, inference and proof

Human understanding normally assembles the most coherent account available from incomplete evidence, as a doctor diagnoses or a historian reconstructs. The book needs further distinctions here.

**Logic** is the necessary structure of consistency, implication, exclusion and possibility; **reasoning** is its application. The complete coherence of all that is, perfectly comprehended and perfectly applicable, the author calls **true logic**; it belongs inseparably to God's nature, and no creature possesses it exhaustively. **Status:** theological premise. Finite minds move towards it, and the author's definition is exact: "Inferential logic is the recursive refinement of partial understanding towards true logic through the discovery and application of relational coherence within the boundaries of created runtime order." **Status:** working definition. *Created runtime order*, the operating created world, is defined in Chapter 3. *Partial* means genuine but incomplete; *recursive* means a later finding can revise a conclusion, its assumptions and even the question. Revising an account of coherence is never revising the coherence of what God has made.

**Proof**, in the book's bounded sense, is the achievement of sufficient warrant, under stated premises, scope and constraints, for an inferred relation to be relied on in further reasoning: to become a **coherence factor**. Formal proof is conclusive relative to its axioms and rules; experimental, philosophical and theological warrant differ in kind. A coherence factor keeps its scope, source, uncertainties and dependencies visible so it can be revised. The aim of inquiry is **inferential precision**, not possession of the whole truth.

## What observation decides

**Coherence is not truth**: a consistent flat-Earth story is still false. Observation rules out accounts that contradict it, reveals regularities any account must respect, and tests specific proposals; several of the project's have failed, and the failures are recorded. It narrows the field without displaying the deepest structure of reality. The book's warrant is therefore a global coherence argument, held with **disciplined confidence**: confidence must not outrun warrant, and justified understanding should not be discounted for falling short of exhaustive knowledge.

## What this chapter establishes

- Description underdetermines ontology; behavioural knowledge is genuine but not knowledge of intrinsic nature.
- Metaphysical elasticity is excluded for every account, including RS.
- Global coherence, tested both ways, is the standard; plausibility can be compared rigorously without numbers.
- Proof is bounded warrant yielding a coherence factor; coherence is not truth, and disciplined confidence is the posture.

# Chapter 3: Relation Before Things

List the stone's properties: grey, hard, heavy, cold, shaped. Each is a way the stone stands to something else. Its greyness is how it answers daylight, and in the dark it has no visible colour though it remains disposed to answer light that way. Its hardness is resistance to other things; its weight the pull between it and the Earth (a sixth as much on the Moon); its coldness the direction of heat flow with your hand; its shape the boundary at which it resists other things. Strip away the relations and it is hard to say what is left. This chapter asks whether that difficulty tells us what the physical world fundamentally is.

## Two pictures

On the **object picture**, reality is a collection of self-contained objects, each with its own properties, in an independent space and time; relations are real but secondary, added to things that would be the same without them. On the **relational picture**, things are real and distinct but constituted *within* relations: what a thing is cannot be separated from how it stands to others, space is an order of relations, and change is the reorganisation of relations. The object picture is practically indispensable. But it carries an unstated assumption: that the basic furniture of the world is mutually independent, and relation an afterthought.

## The working definition of "material"

Much turns on *material*, a word easily stretched, so the book fixes it. **Working definition:** a form of reality is **material** when it exhibits persistent, spatially individuated identity that can, in principle, be mechanically manipulated or relocated while preserving its continuity as an identifiable entity. Hands are the reference point and instruments extend them: an atom nudged by a microscope tip has been handled. The definition is narrower than "physical" (a sound is physical but cannot be carried elsewhere; Chapter 6). It is open to refinement but not to be widened whenever a case is awkward, since a word that covers everything decides nothing.

## Why the object picture strains

First, physics has found no truly isolated thing; properties are specified by how things interact, and an electron's charge is its capacity to interact in a certain way. Quantum field theory treats particles as excitations of fields rather than self-standing pellets. Second, the deepest theories describe relations naturally: general relativity describes gravity as the geometry relating events, and quantum **entanglement**, confirmed many times, gives joint states that cannot be decomposed into states of the parts. Leibniz, Carlo Rovelli and the structural realists have drawn relational conclusions; the argument rests on none of them, but they show that the same mathematics admits relation-first readings. Third, and most important for this book, if the basic furniture is self-contained, then causation, communication, belonging and responsibility must each be added to a world that does not contain them (Chapter 10).

## Universal interoperability

Granite from Hampshire and from Western Australia, formed with no shared history anyone arranged, are *mutually intelligible*: they press, scratch, warm and cool each other by the same relations, and light from either is received by the same eye. Newly encountered matter (a meteorite) and newly made matter (a new alloy) join the same causal grammar at once. Physical quantities are commensurable everywhere. The book calls this **universal interoperability**. **Status:** empirical observation, requiring ontological explanation.

Part of it is unsurprising: in any compositional ontology, composites inherit their parts' compatibility. The deep fact lies at the base: every fundamental kind shares one finely matched grammar. The proton and electron charges are equal in magnitude to at least one part in 10²¹, which is why ordinary matter is neutral. Physicists do not treat this as luck; they seek a common structure that requires it.

An ontology of self-contained objects cannot take this for free. Something common must supply the interface: common laws, fields, symmetries, forms, universal dispositions or a common history. Each does real work, and some come close to this book's view. But each is a common structure doing what self-contained objects could not. The question for every ontology, this one included, is what that structure is and what it costs. The comparison is qualitative and **conditional**: *if* each kind's capacities were specified independently, their exact matching would be a coincidence, and an account needing such coincidences is less plausible than one that does not. No number is defensible, and none is needed for the ordering.

## Three propositions

- **A. Material fundamentality.** Material entities are fundamental within creation; their relational capacities belong intrinsically to them; material individuality does not arise from a more fundamental non-material created order.
- **B. A non-material foundation, with matter emergent.** A more fundamental created relational reality, not itself material, constitutes and sustains material individuality. Matter is real; its materiality is emergent rather than self-standing.
- **C. Two fundamental orders.** Material entities and a distinct non-material relational order are both fundamental; the order supplies the capacities through which matter interacts, but matter is not constituted by it.

All three are compatible with creation, and design alone favours none. The author's **theological premise** goes further: *that* material existence derives from a non-material foundation is held as a premise, read with Hebrews 11:3. On that premise A and C are excluded. The comparison is retained because it reaches the same conclusion by an independent route from observation, and because it shows what B still owes.

**A**, pressed to explain the exact matching, has three routes. It may hold that capacities are intrinsically directed at partners, which is faithful to A but makes relation essential and narrows the disagreement with B. It may add a governing order of laws or fields, which is C unacknowledged. Or it may treat the matching as brute, leaving it unexplained. None shows A incoherent; each shows A relying on something unstated. A also meets pressure from physics: material individuals are created and annihilated (radiation near a nucleus produces an electron and positron), and identical particles carry no individual labels. Retreating to fields as the fundamental "material" changes the proposition, which is elasticity. **C** explains the matching by positing the relational order openly, at the cost of two fundamental orders, and must still explain why independently existing matter fits it so exactly. **B** posits one order, within which interoperability is what common constitution means. Its cost is real: B owes an account of how stable material individuals arise within a non-material order, and none has been demonstrated. The premise says *that*; *how* is unpaid (Chapter 9).

The comparison is clear without being final. B does best on economy and integration, conditional on paying its debt; on the comparison alone C is not excluded; plain A carries the heaviest burden. And B is a family, including a realist reading of quantum fields. Physics favours the family, not its relational member, which must be argued for separately.

## The central thesis

**What is most fundamental about created physical reality is relation.** Whatever appears as a stable unit, an atom, a stone, a star, appears as a configuration within relation, never as an item that first exists independently and then relates. This is Proposition B in relational form. **Status:** RS working hypothesis, the book's central thesis. It is neither an observation nor a theorem. It is supported by the strains in the object picture, by underdetermination and by interoperability, and would be weakened by an object-first account explaining interoperability, life and personhood with equal economy.

Three clarifications. Relation-first does not deny that things are real and distinct; distinctness is real but *derivative*, carried by a stable pattern of relations. (A knot in a rope is real and countable, yet a configuration of the rope; the picture fails in that rope fibres pre-exist the knot, while the proposal imagines no pre-existing material.) Relation here is not a mental association but a real standing-between. And relation-first is stronger than "everything interacts": *interactional* relation leaves identities unchanged, while the proposal holds that physical things are *constituted* within relation.

## The relational substrate

The **relational substrate** is the created relational order within which physical things are constituted and through which they act. It is not matter (matter is a stable form within it), not a field of physics under another name, not an ether, and not information detached from anything physical. A field is a mathematical representation; the substrate is a proposal about the order such representations describe. Whether it is spatial is **open**. It is not a mind. Above all it is not God and not a soul.

If relation is foundational, nothing acts across a gap of non-relation: influence is **mediated**. The claim concerns dependence, not travel or distance.

**Created runtime order.** Creation may be considered in its origin or in its ongoing operation. **Working definition:** the *created runtime order* is the operating, non-conscious created order that expresses itself materially. It is not all that exists: God is not part of it, and souls and spiritual creatures, though created, are not members of it. The computing borrowing has a sharp limit: creation is not a computer and God is not a programmer who stepped away. A better image is music, its composition distinguished from its performance, except that the Composer upholds every note as it sounds.

## What Scripture requires, and the question of grounding

> `Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power...`
> *Hebrews 1:3*

These texts teach that created order coheres because Christ upholds it, not how. They exclude a self-sufficient or divine deep order. If there is a substrate, it is a creature whose coherence is received. The ontology is **consistent with** Scripture's teaching on dependence, and bounded by it; Scripture does not teach it.

Could the deepest order be grounded in something beneath it, and that in something else, without end? Each step might be intelligible. But offered as the *complete* ground, such a chain hands its task on at every step, so the foundation it requires is never supplied. The author regards a grounding made only of deferral as internally contradictory, given what a ground must do. This is a claim about grounding, not about mathematics: an infinite sequence of numbers is consistent. The proposal therefore posits a primitive created order, specified and upheld by its Creator, beneath which there is no further created ground. **Status:** ontological inference, within the requirement of foundational grounding and from the scriptural premise.

## What this chapter establishes

- "Material" has a fixed working definition; universal interoperability is an observation every ontology must pay to explain.
- The author's premise excludes A and C; the comparative argument independently favours B, which carries an unpaid debt of mechanism.
- Central thesis (RS hypothesis): relation is foundational; the substrate is a creature, not mind, God or soul.
- Created runtime order is defined and bounded; an endlessly deferred chain cannot be an ultimate ground.

# Chapter 4: What Can Be and What Comes to Be

Water freezes into one characteristic lattice, and every snowflake, however individual, is six-sided. The possible is wide and yet firmly bounded. If physical things are configurations within relation, what bounds the configurations created order can bear, and how does a particular one become actual?

## T0: whatever conditions actualisation

The possible is structured before anything happens: conservation laws, symmetries and quantised energy levels bound what can occur. The project uses the label **T0** for *whatever conditions actualisation*: the constraints, potential or relational organisation according to which an actual state can come to be at all. That minimal commitment, that something is logically prior to any happening and bounds it, the book holds with confidence. **Status:** ontological inference.

What *kind* of thing T0 is remains **open**. The book's working reading is **constrained possibility**: the shape of what created order can bear, meaning which configurations and transitions can occur and how they are weighted. Live alternatives include a created potential order, a generative architecture of constraint, or the relational state of the substrate prior to a given actualisation. "Possibility space" is a way of speaking, not a realm or store.

## Admissibility and tendency

A rule may **forbid** a transition, making it impossible, or **favour** one, weighting it while leaving every option open. The project's **constraint laboratory** made this exact. In the smallest relational worlds, a few points between which links form and dissolve, it enumerated every rule of these kinds and every combination of up to three, and computed exactly, by calculation rather than simulation, each rule set's effect on long-run behaviour.

The result was clean: **only forbidding rules changed which patterns could be reached at all.** Only they created dead ends, altered the basic rhythm of movement or divided the space of possibilities; favouring rules shifted occupancy, sometimes greatly, but left every reachable pattern reachable. Later work located the difference more finely: in aggregate statistics a ban behaves like the limit of ever-stronger disfavouring, but only a ban changes the *structure of possible histories*, making some changes one-way and some states recoverable only by first undoing another relation. A persisting absence is therefore either **rate-limited** (return possible but unlikely) or **gate-limited** (return impossible until something else changes). These are **model results**, not discoveries about nature. They fix two terms:

- **Admissibility**: whether a transition is possible at all.
- **Tendency**: how likely an admissible transition is relative to others.

Tendency is not a probability anyone assigns; it names how created order is disposed among admissible options. The book proposes that created order has both. **Status:** mathematical result in models; RS working hypothesis for created order. *Admissible* means physically possible within created order, never morally permitted (Chapter 13).

## Constraint as generative

Constraint, in all its forms, makes things possible. A river is a river because of its banks; without them the water becomes a marsh. Coupling between transitions and relations among more than two things also generate structure where nothing new is forbidden (Chapter 9). Scripture presents creation as division and bounding:

> `Thou hast set a bound that they may not pass over; that they turn not again to cover the earth.`
> *Psalm 104:9*

> `He hath also stablished them for ever and ever: he hath made a decree which shall not pass.`
> *Psalm 148:6*

## T1, reachability and two kinds of constraint

**Actualisation** is the coming-to-be of one configuration from among the possible; **T1** is actual order, the configurations and transitions that have actually come to be. T1 is not a bare snapshot: it carries its history (Chapter 6). So the T0–T1 relation is not a one-way pipeline. Distinguish **total admissibility**, everything created order could ever bear, from what is **currently reachable** given the present state and its history. A stone that has cracked presents a fracture path that was not there before, changing how later forces act on it; a germinated seed can no longer stay dormant. Actual history changes what is reachable without changing whatever T0 ultimately is. **Status:** RS working hypothesis, with model support.

Constraints differ in level. **Explicitly intrinsic** constraints belong to the primitive configuration of created order, the conditions under which anything can happen; total admissibility rests on them. **Implicitly intrinsic** constraints arise once a configuration is actual and follow from it necessarily: once a crack exists, it necessarily affects the conditions of subsequent fracture, perhaps favouring propagation along it, without determining whether or exactly how the stone breaks. **Status:** working definitions. The point that matters next: **a configuration that arose by chance can carry necessary constraints.**

## Opportunity

Possibility, opportunity, admissibility and actualisation are four different things. **Working definition:** an **opportunity** is a distinguishable occasion at which some admissible actualisation is actually available. An opportunity can pass unused, like the seed that could have germinated this spring and did not. How such an unused occasion is to be individuated is **open**.

## Chess without players

The rules of chess are admissibility; commonly preferred moves are something like tendency; the game played tonight is actualisation. The analogy captures that possibility is structured but not determining, and that the actual is drawn from the admissible. It fails at one point, and the failure is its most important lesson: chess has *players* who choose. Created physical order has none. Nothing within it selects a transition or wants anything. An analogy pressed to supply a player turns the substrate into a mind. Chapter 5 asks how one transition becomes actual without a chooser.

## Purpose and non-agentic runtime

> `While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.`
> *Genesis 8:22*

Scripture calls the order of the heavens *ordinances* (Jeremiah 31:35-36) and binds God's faithfulness to it. Creation is purposed through and through. The question is where purpose sits. The book's theological inference:

1. **Purpose shapes what is possible**: God's intention is expressed in the shape of constrained possibility.
2. **Possibility shapes what can be actualised.**
3. **Actualisation proceeds without the substrate intending anything.**

This protects purpose (the world's possibilities were given with intent) and creatureliness (physical order is lawful in its own right, neither puppet nor mind in disguise). The book calls this **non-agentic runtime**: purposed without being purposeful in itself. It avoids occasionalism, on which created things have no real operations, and deism, on which God withdrew, in favour of the mainstream Christian position of lawful created operation under continual upholding (Colossians 1:17; Hebrews 1:3).

One caution, developed in Chapter 15: God is not one more cause among the world's causes. The primitive order is fixed for every creature operating within it; it is not fixed for its Creator. The book describes the ordinary operation of creation, and makes no claim about the manner of God's action in miracles, providence or redemption.

## What this chapter establishes

- T0, whatever conditions actualisation, is a confident minimal commitment; its nature is open.
- Admissibility and tendency are distinct (model result; RS hypothesis for created order); only forbidding changes reachability.
- Actual history alters what is currently reachable; explicit and implicit constraints differ in level; chance configurations can carry necessary constraints.
- Non-agentic runtime: purpose belongs to the Creator, the substrate intends nothing, and God is not confined by the primitive order.

# Chapter 5: Constrained Chance

A single atom of carbon-14 has, on the standard understanding of quantum physics, no internal clock: it may decay in the next second or in fifty thousand years, and not for lack of information. (Some interpretations dispute this.) Yet half of a large sample decays in about 5,730 years, precisely enough to date an ancient campfire. Open in the particular, exact in the aggregate: this pairing is among the most important facts in the book.

## The meaning of "stochastic"

"Random" can mean **lawless** (anything could happen), **unpredictable to us** (a cause we could not foresee), or **probabilistic** (outcomes open, frequencies fixed). The book uses **stochastic** for the third: a process whose outcomes are governed by probabilities over defined possibilities. A fair die is stochastic, not lawless; it never shows seven. Stochastic is also distinct from **chaotic**: a chaotic system is fully deterministic but so sensitive to initial conditions that it is practically unpredictable.

Calling a process stochastic says how it is *described*, not why. The probabilities may be **epistemic**, expressing ignorance of hidden details in a deterministic process, or **ontic**, expressing genuine openness in the world. The same description fits both.

## Chance in physics

Repeated quantum measurements yield stable, exact frequencies (observation), and quantum theory gives its predictions as probabilities (mathematical constraint). Whether nature is *fundamentally* indeterministic is a matter of interpretation: some readings take the probabilities as ontic, others as epistemic, all agreeing on every measurement so far. **The probabilities are established; fundamental indeterminism is not.**

Jacob Barandes has argued that any quantum system can be described as an *indivisible* stochastic process among ordinary configurations, one whose transition probabilities from an earlier to a later time need not compose through intermediate times, which take on a role only when something, typically the environment, registers the system's state. This shows a stochastic reading of quantum theory can be worked out in detail. It does not show the world is chancy, since it reproduces standard predictions, and it supplies no probability for complete histories. **Status:** an illuminating mathematical connection, not adopted as support.

## The proposal: constrained stochastic actualisation

Chapter 4 left the question of how one admissible transition becomes actual without a chooser. The book proposes that **actualisation is fundamentally stochastic** in the ontic sense: which currently reachable transition occurs is *not uniquely determined* by the preceding created configuration together with its governing relations; tendency weights outcomes without selecting one in advance. This openness is the book's claim, not part of the meaning of "stochastic".

The weight falls on **constrained**. *Unconstrained* stochasticity, every conceivable outcome equally open, would yield a world without stable form. *Constrained* stochasticity is chance operating within the shape of the possible: admissibility excludes whole regions, tendency weights what remains, and history alters what is reachable. Accordingly the book rejects **true randomness**, occurrence governed by no admissibility relation or pattern, as incompatible with a coherent created order.

Necessity is not **determinism**. **Working definition:** determinism holds where a complete preceding configuration, under its applicable governing relations, admits exactly one successor; and the governing relations are not properties hidden inside the state. Absolutely binding constraints can leave several outcomes open, as the rules of chess never select the move. Created order is **necessary in its constraints and open in its actualisations.**

**Status:** RS working hypothesis. It fits the probabilistic character of quantum theory without following from it. A demonstration that the probabilities are wholly epistemic would count against it. Its positive support is coherence: it explains how a non-agentic creation actualises without a hidden chooser, and it fits openness in the particular with exactness in the aggregate.

## An illustration: exact answers from chance

One of the project's models adds two numbers by building populations of positively and negatively oriented relations and letting them change at random under local rules (re-attachment, cancellation of opposite pairs, creation and annihilation of neutral pairs). Thousands of runs from one starting arrangement followed thousands of different paths, and every one reached the correct sum. **An exact outcome can coexist with great diversity of chance-driven paths.** The exactness came from rules conserving the total; chance supplied the variety. A fixed sequence would also be exact, and ordinary computer science explains the model fully. It illustrates how chance and exactness relate, not that nature computes this way.

Weakening the rules, by occasionally permitting a forbidden deletion, degraded accuracy smoothly and progressively. Stability is always stability **of something, against some disturbance, over some timescale**.

## Chance and providence

> `The lot is cast into the lap; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD.`
> *Proverbs 16:33*

The lot was the ancient paradigm of chance, used even for dividing the land. Scripture does not deny that it falls by chance as far as anything within the lap can tell; it says the whole disposing belongs to the Lord. "Time and chance happeneth to them all" (Ecclesiastes 9:11) is stated without alarm.

The book's theological inference: if created actualisation is constrained-stochastic, chance is a **created mode of actualisation**, not a rival power and not God's uncertainty. The openness is openness *with respect to created determination*; it is **not a claim that God lacks knowledge** of the actual outcome. How God's exhaustive knowledge and action relate to that openness is not a mechanism the book claims to explain, and the long Christian discussion of sovereignty and contingency is left where it stands.

Four ideas must be kept apart: **mathematical randomness** (a property of a description), **ontological stochasticity** (actualisation is open), **purposelessness** (nothing is for anything) and **atheism** (there is no God). None follows from the one before. Proverbs 16:33 accepts the first, perhaps the second, and denies the third and fourth together.

## What this chapter establishes

- "Stochastic" describes probabilistic governance; whether probabilities are epistemic or ontic is a separate question, unsettled in physics.
- RS hypothesis: actualisation is constrained-stochastic, not uniquely determined by prior created configuration; true randomness is rejected.
- Necessary constraint is not determinism, which is defined precisely.
- Chance is a created mode of actualisation; its openness concerns created determination, not divine knowledge.

# Chapter 6: Identity Through Change

Heraclitus's river, a candle flame and your own body all persist while their material is replaced. So sameness through change cannot in general rest on an unchanging core of stuff. Chapter 5 sharpened the puzzle: if the history beneath every thing is open at each step, what keeps the stone a stone?

## Invariants

In the calculator of Chapter 5, relations changed constantly but the total never moved; it lived in what every allowed change preserved. Such a preserved quantity is an **invariant**: something a set of transformations leaves unchanged. Physics is built on invariants. Noether's theorem ties each conservation law (energy, momentum, charge) to a symmetry, and particles are classified by invariants rather than by being particular bits of stuff. A knot's type is invariant under every deformation that does not cut the string, measured by integers that can only stay the same or jump. Identity can be carried by organisation. The lesson is sound but incomplete.

## Projection and equivalence

Two coins look identical, and for shopping they are; a bank's test reveals one as counterfeit. Two keys cut by eye look alike; only one turns the lock. **Whether two things are "the same" depends on what could tell them apart.** Exact analysis of the project's simplest rule systems found the same: systems sorted into one family by coarse behaviour proved to be many distinct systems, some sharing every recorded summary while differing in how unpredictably they moved.

This is the problem of **projection** or coarse-graining: every description summarises and may hide distinctions. The principle: **a distinction can safely be ignored only where no reachable future consequence can draw on it.**

Hence the account of identity: **a thing continues to be the same kind of thing, in the relevant respect, when its changes preserve its relevant equivalence class: the set of states no admissible future interaction of the relevant kind could tell apart from it.** An invariant is one way of *certifying* such a class, not necessarily the whole of identity. The criterion is not what a human notices but what admissible interactions within created order could discriminate; the counterfeit differed whether or not it was ever tested. Sameness so defined is relational without being arbitrary.

The criterion has an established relative in **computational mechanics**, where two histories of a stochastic process are equivalent exactly when they imply the same probabilities for everything that could follow; the resulting *causal states* are provably the minimal maximally predictive description. Two limits come with it: predictive equivalence is relative to a chosen description of the future, and it is not individual sameness. **Status:** ontological inference.

## Two questions about sameness

Sameness of *kind* differs from sameness of *individual*. A thing destroyed and later rebuilt indistinguishably belongs to the same equivalence class without thereby being the same thing; two coins from one die can be indistinguishable and still two. What answers the second question is continuity: an unbroken, history-bearing lineage. Four achievements are distinct: restoring **function**, restoring **organisation**, restoring the **equivalence class**, and restoring the **same individual**. The first three can each occur without the fourth, as the project's exploratory models found. What physically carries individual continuity is an **open question**.

## Provenance and memory

Sometimes present states differ only in how they came to be: a straightened paper clip is weaker, a healed bone carries its remodelling. **Provenance** is past distinction surviving in present relational structure. It is not a separate ledger but embodied in residual stresses, correlations and accessibility, and it belongs to identity where it remains consequential. Where nothing can draw on it, it may be set aside for identity without becoming unreal. **The changed relations are the record**, and consequence can persist where provenance is unrecoverable. **Status:** RS working hypothesis.

T1 is therefore not a sequence of memoryless snapshots. From this the book proposes: **memory, at the physical level, is past distinctions embodied in present relations so that they alter what is currently reachable, how likely it is, or how a thing responds.** Hysteresis is mainstream physics. Supporting evidence differs in strength. Published metal-fatigue data show an order effect in the predicted direction, though the project's model predicted its size badly, so this is a directional finding only. In small relational models, hidden present structure produced apparent memory. And one visible arrangement faced different futures depending on its history, until that history was counted as part of the state. **Status:** RS working hypothesis; model results, not a law of nature.

## Persistence, and its limits

**Persistence** is continued re-actualisation within a relevant equivalence class and, for a continuing individual, along an unbroken history. It always has three qualifiers: stability *of* something, *against* some disturbance, *over* some time. The later models added discipline: *what is likely* and *what lasts* differ (systems agreeing in occupancy, density and activity differed more than twentyfold in pattern lifetime), so persistence claims must name their comparison and separate reaching a state from surviving in it. And **preserving is not repairing**: when a model relation was destroyed, the system settled confidently on a different total. Recovery requires that the needed distinctions survive somewhere, in redundancy, a template, a wider order or an agent.

## Handling, perturbation and material organisation

To **handle** something is to relocate or manipulate an identifiable entity while preserving its identity; to **perturb** is to change a condition or configuration without relocating a separately identifiable entity. Sound is the paradigm of the second: it crosses a room at about 340 m/s while no molecule makes the journey. **Material organisation** is a real configuration or process instantiated through material reality whose organisational identity need not be an additional material entity. Four things are distinct: the material carrier (air), the organised activity (travelling pressure variation), the identity of the organisation (this note) and the mathematical description (a wave equation).

Hard cases refine the definition without dissolving it. Russell's solitary wave on a canal (1834) and magnetic skyrmions persist, are individuated and can be moved; where such individuality belongs to an organisation of a distinct material carrier, the book counts it as *material organisation with entity-like individuality*, a clarification rather than a widening. Phonons in a crystal have particle-like mathematics, yet nobody counts them as additional material entities. And materiality can depend on regime: a trapped electron is individuated and movable, electrons in a metal's collective state are not. **Materiality belongs first to individuated configurations, and derivatively to kinds.**

## Matter as stable form; two emergences

The book's proposal: **matter is stable form within the relational substrate**, configurations whose equivalence classes are preserved by the changes they ordinarily undergo. Atoms are very stable relational forms, not self-existing pellets. Physics already undermines mass as the sum of lumps: most of ordinary matter's mass arises from the energy binding quarks. **Status:** ontological interpretation.

Two emergences must be kept apart. **Emergence within matter**, of higher-order identities from organised matter, is observed everywhere. **The emergence of matter**, of material individuality within a created order not itself material, is Proposition B: *that* it is so is the author's premise, *how* is the book's hypothesis. The first supplies analogies, constraints and questions for the second, not demonstrations. The author's remark that "matter begets matter" names the observed regularity of material continuity, not a claim that matter must ultimately come from matter; pair production shows material individuals arising from radiation, consistent with B but equally described by field ontologies. The central ontological question: **how does a configuration of something not itself material acquire persistent, manipulable individuality?** It has not been answered.

## Succession, cadence and time

On the relational proposal, time is the **ordering of actualisation**, not an independent container. That must fit measurement: clocks run at different rates with height and speed, as general relativity predicts; whether spacetime is a substance or an ordering is interpretation, not measurement.

The order is directed by consequence: the bell sounds because it was struck. **Succession** is the directed order of actual consequence, not produced by anything more basic and prior to its measurement. **Status:** theological and philosophical premise. It does not make every logical implication an event in time, and it does not conflict with time-symmetric fundamental equations, which describe which histories are admissible, whereas succession concerns which occurred.

Three things are distinguished from it. **Cadence** is the ordered pattern of occasions or events: a ferry calling twelve times a day gives an **opportunity cadence**; a passenger crossing four times gives an **actualisation cadence**; they can differ. (The passenger chooses; in physical order nothing chooses, but the two patterns differ similarly.) A **rhythm** is a recurrence with a cadence, possibly irregular or compound. **Measurement** compares one cadence with another taken as reference; clocks are physical processes, seconds a convention. And a **reference cadence** intrinsic to created order would make comparison possible; the author's favoured candidate is oscillation (Chapter 8), which would measure succession, not produce it. **Status:** mechanism candidate. The models confirm that direction does not come free: it requires a non-gradient tendency or a one-way rule, a fact about models.

> `But thou art the same, and thy years shall have no end.`
> *Psalm 102:27*

Created things persist within equivalence classes and wear out like a garment; God's sameness is underived, since he is not a configuration. Whether a *person's* identity is an equivalence class of bodily configuration is a further question, answered no in Chapters 12 and 15.

## What this chapter establishes

- Identity of kind is the relevant equivalence class judged by reachable futures; invariants certify it; individual continuity requires lineage and is open physically.
- Provenance and memory: history is carried in present relations, with no separate ledger (RS hypothesis).
- Material organisation, regime-dependence and two emergences are distinguished; how matter emerges is unanswered.
- Succession (premise), cadence, measurement and reference cadence are distinct levels of an account of time.

# Chapter 7: Transmit, Carry, Receive

Sunlight falls on the stone; its grey body and pale vein return light differently; some reaches your eye; molecules, cells and nerves change; you see the stone. Nothing moved from stone to head, yet a distinction in the stone became a distinction in you. The book names what that requires **Transmit, Carry, Receive** (T-C-R), and proposes it as the grammar of consequential manifestation: the logical structure by which any distinction in created order becomes consequential to another state.

## The minimal grammar

```text
distinction → preserved dependency → consequence
```

- **Transmit**: a distinction in one relational condition is capable of constraining something other than itself.
- **Carry**: the dependency between distinction and consequence preserves enough of the distinction for the consequence to depend on it. The carrier need not be an object; at the most primitive level the relation itself may carry.
- **Receive**: another state becomes actual, or its range of actualisation is constrained, because of the source distinction. Reception is consequence, not arrival.

**Test:** if two different source states could never make any difference to any consequence, the distinction has not been carried.

The minimal grammar presupposes little: no travel, no measurable space to travel through, no intermediate object, no encoding and decoding, no layers. The source need not be depleted (telling your name; a lighthouse), and reception need not occur exactly once. Each of these occurs in particular cases; none belongs to the grammar.

## Transmission and carriage

What reaches you is not "light" in general but the **source distinction** the stone makes in it; were the stone identical to its surroundings in every way light registers, it would be invisible. To transmit is to be capable of constraining something beyond oneself, by emission, copying, exposure, modulation or simply standing in a relation on which something depends. Transmission is governed by admissibility: an atom emits only at energies its structure allows, hence each element's spectral fingerprint.

Carriage need not be travel. A pebble's ripples reach the bank though no water crosses the pond; signal fires relay news though no fire travels, each lit *in dependence on* the last; a telephone call survives conversion through current, radio, glass and current again. What persists is **the relevant distinction, preserved by a chain of dependency**. The *carrier* is whatever bears that dependency at a stage, and carriers are substitutable: a carrier can preserve consequence without preserving its implementation. At substrate depth, where two conditions are constitutively related, the relation is the dependency and nothing further crosses.

Chapter 6 does work here. A carrier must preserve the source distinction's **relevant equivalence class**: whatever the receiver's possible responses can tell apart. A telephone line can drop vocal texture but not the difference between "yes" and "no". **Provenance** must be carried where the receiver's response depends on it, as with signatures or chains of custody. Carriage is constrained, degrading under noise, and is persistence of something, against some disturbance, over some time.

## Reception and compatibility

Light passing through a car window leaves the window unchanged in any way dependent on the stone; the light arrived but the distinction was not received. At the retina, a molecule changes shape on absorption, starting a cascade: reception. A letter unopened on the mat has arrived; read and acted on, it has been received.

**Receiver compatibility** has two sides. Every receiver has a range of registrable distinctions (the eye admits visible light, not radio), so reception too is governed by admissibility. And receivers differ in their response to repetition: a thermostat receives continuously, an ear integrates, a switch already on is idempotent, a choir is changed more by each rehearsal, and a bank ledger requires exactly-once reception. Exactly-once is a property of transactional receivers, not a law of reception. An FM broadcast shows how much compatibility involves: the programme is carried in the *modulation* of a carrier frequency, and recovery requires both tuning and a matched demodulator. Five things are distinct: carrier frequency, modulation, preserved organisation, the receiver's compatible arrangement, and the programme reconstructed. Hence: **consequential reception depends on compatibility between the organisation in which a distinction is preserved and the organisation of the receiver.** **Status:** ontological inference.

## Identity organiser and identity coherer

When what passes is an organised identity, two functions appear. An **identity organiser** is a relational organisation or process that establishes, structures or constrains distinctions so that they can participate in a coherent, consequential identity (a modulator; a cello's strings and body). An **identity coherer** is a relational organisation capable of preserving or reconstructing consequentially relevant identity from variations received through a compatible interface (a demodulator; an ear and brain). Three cautions: neither is a substance, field or vibration by definition; cohering is not confined to receivers (frequency modulation itself confers robustness); and one process can do both. Neither belongs to the minimal grammar. The working reading is that they are T-C-R's transmitting and receiving roles specialised to identities. **Status:** RS working hypothesis; developing research concepts.

A distinction may be transmitted and carried without consequential reception: starlight falling on nothing able to register it. The propagation is real; the chain is incomplete.

![Figure 2. Transmit, Carry, Receive, as it appears in manifest cases. A distinction at the source becomes capable of constraining something beyond itself; a chain of dependency preserves the relevant distinction across substitutable carriers; a compatible receiver is changed in a way that depends on it. The dependency persists while the vehicles change. At substrate depth the carrier may simply be the relation.](v2/figures/fig-tcr.png)

## Why the grammar recurs

T-C-R nests (the nerve impulse is its own chain), composes (one retinal image from many rays) and is coordinated (depth from two eyes' related images). The three-part form recurs in light, DNA replication, nerves, speech, law and liturgy. Coincidence is one explanation. The book proposes another: if A is genuinely consequential for B, something about A must be able to make a difference to B, that difference must be preserved, and B must differ because of it, which already is T-C-R. **T-C-R is the operational form of consequential relation itself.** It is also **recursive**: every receiver is a source for a further relation, so consequential relation is a network in which every present state is the reception of earlier distinctions and a source of later ones. That is how actuality carries history and shapes reachability.

**Status:** RS working hypothesis (stronger form). Supported by recurrence and economy; weakened by a genuine consequence not describable as a preserved distinction, or by showing the recurrence is better explained as convergent design. A shared grammar is a structural analogy, not an identity of nature.

## Cause as consequential relation

In a deterministic picture a cause produces its effect. In a stochastic world a distinction can still be causal by excluding next states, enabling others, changing likelihoods, constraining correlations, preserving an invariant or altering later accessibility. **Working formulation:** *a causal relation exists where a distinction in one relational condition modifies the admissible actualisation, or the consequential state, of another through a distinction-preserving dependency.* It was checked against the simplest models, where a three-way condition weights the next two-way event without determining it, while that condition's initial value soon ceases to matter because no dependency preserves it. **Status:** working hypothesis.

The account also disciplines "enabled", which can mean **precedence** (came first), **competitive clearance** (removed competitors; the project's model reports call this "opportunity", a word the book reserves for Chapter 4's sense), **rate** (changed the other's tendency) or **support** (made it possible at all). Only rate and support change admissibility or tendency, and only support is the work of a forbidding rule. In the later models most soft "enabling" proved to be competitive clearance.

## Manifestation, and its status

For the substrate to be manifest as material and experienced reality, there must be a **consequence-preserving relationship** between distinctions in the underlying order and the world we meet. That minimal claim is all the book makes. It does not assume a hidden object transformed into a visible one through layers; the two might be descriptions of one fact, resolutions of one order, or related by a mapping not yet understood.

**Status overall.** Emission, propagation and absorption are established physics, which T-C-R organises without replacing; it has not been shown to predict anything the physics does not. Open: whether created order generates its own carriers from minimal relations (in every model so far the carriers were designed), and the architecture of manifestation.

## T-C-R across categories

> `So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper in the thing whereto I sent it.`
> *Isaiah 55:11*

Isaiah's image is physical rain; its subject is God's word. Paul traces sending, preaching, hearing and believing (Romans 10:14-17). The form is that of something made available, carried and received. On Chapter 1's four relations, this is at least a **structural analogy**; whether created consequence was also designed to **represent** how God's word works is a question about creation's purpose (Chapter 16). It is not identity: God's word is not a physical signal, and faith is not the absorption of light.

## What this chapter establishes

- T-C-R's minimal grammar presupposes no travel, encoding or layers; reception is consequence.
- Carriage preserves the relevant equivalence class; reception depends on receiver compatibility.
- Identity organiser and coherer are functional roles (RS hypothesis); T-C-R as the form of consequential relation and an account of cause are working hypotheses.
- Cross-category use is structural analogy, possibly representation, never identity.

# Chapter 8: Organised Identity

The opening of Bach's first Cello Suite, G, D, B, A, B, D, B, D, is recognisable on a different cello, a guitar, a marimba, a phone speaker, or from a file that contains no sound until played. Something persists that is not material: an organised identity. This chapter asks what such identities are, how they survive changes of carrier, and how relations become organisations and organisations new wholes.

## Identity in relations

Instruments shape overtones differently (timbre), rooms add reflections, and phone speakers scarcely produce the cello's lowest notes. What survives is relational: **intervals** (ratios of frequency: an octave 2:1, a fifth very nearly 3:2), **rhythm** (relations among durations), contour and harmonic relations. A melody transposed or played slightly faster is the same melody. **Observation:** the continuity of an organised identity need not depend on the continuity of one particular material carrier.

Variation has limits. A badly placed loudspeaker produces standing waves that boom some bass notes and cancel others, yet the line remains recognisable. A phone reproduces almost nothing near the cello's 65 Hz C, yet listeners hear the right pitch, because the auditory system reconstructs the missing fundamental from its overtones: an identity coherer at work. Change the intervals or scramble the rhythm and the identity is lost. Identity under variation is Chapter 6's equivalence class seen from the receiver's side. Lossy audio compression is legitimate only relative to a particular receiver: it discards what human hearing would not register, which another receiver could draw on.

Two questions must be separated: whether the identity is **preserved in the signal** (the interval ratios are objectively present whether or not anyone listens), and whether a given **receiver can recover it** (which depends on its organisation; a trained musician hears a modulation an untrained listener misses).

## Does the receiver help constitute the identity?

The pitch heard at 65 Hz is not a component of the sound; it is what a compatible receiver makes of relations that are present. A rainbow is cleaner still: each observer sees a bow formed by different drops at an angle fixed by position. Tuning forks near a chord each respond to their matching component, without consciousness. Four things are therefore distinct: the **source identity**, the **carried distinctions**, the **received or reconstructed identity**, and the **consequential manifestation**.

The book's proposal, which the author regards as a credible candidate: **a receiver's compatible organisation may be part of what makes a particular identity possible *as a consequentially manifested identity*, rather than merely recognising one fully constituted at transmission.** This is not idealism: the cello vibrates in an empty hall, and created things are real before anything receives them. **Status:** RS working hypothesis; scope open.

The working principle, **relational identity compatibility**: *a distinguishable identity can remain coherent across changes of instantiation when the relationships necessary for its consequential recognition or operation are preserved through compatible T-C-R processes.* **Status:** RS working hypothesis.

## Identity without its first carrier

The dodo was extinct by the late seventeenth century. It is known through sailors' accounts, variable paintings, bones from a Mauritian marsh, the only surviving soft tissue (a head and foot in Oxford), and genetic material showing in 2002 that its closest living relative is the Nicobar pigeon. In 2018 scans revealed lead shot in the Oxford head: a distinction carried for centuries awaited a compatible receiver. **Preservation of identity-relevant information is distinguishable from the continued material existence of its original instantiation.** What is preserved must be sorted: the *species* (a kind and lineage), the Oxford bird's *historical identity* (one individual), and any *reconstruction*, which would at most fall within a reconstructed equivalence class and would not be any dodo that lived. The same line holds for persons (Chapter 15): a person is not the information that survives about them.

## History as received distinction

Historical inquiry is T-C-R across time: past events leave distinctions in records and artefacts whose value depends on provenance, fidelity, corroboration and preservation. Darwin was a master of this reasoning, reconstructing history from fossils, biogeography and shared structure, and acutely aware of his carriers' limits: following Lyell, he called the geological record "a history of the world imperfectly kept, and written in a changing dialect". The anatomists' distinction between resemblance of structure and of function, which Darwin read as evidence of descent, parallels the book's own: a shared form is not by itself a shared lineage. Readers differ on the scope of Darwin's theory, and the book does not adjudicate it; its point concerns method. *Quantum Darwinism* (Zurek) borrows the name for a physical theory in which a system leaves many redundant records in its environment, so that observers reading fragments agree; its kinship with this chapter is structural.

## The grammar of emergence

Follow the music upward: pressure varies, so notes are distinguishable; notes stand in relations (intervals, durations); relations are organised by harmony and counterpoint; the organisation acquires an identity (this phrase, this movement). Stated as a process, the book adopts a **working formulation**:

```text
distinction → relationship → organisation → higher-order identity
```

The order is one of articulation, not priority in being: a distinction is already relational, and at the lowest order the first two stages may be given together. Not every pattern is a new order. A **higher-order identity** must (1) **persist beyond its own constituents**, against a matched comparison; (2) be **consequential as a whole**, so that some reachable future responds to it as a unit; and (3) be **multiply realisable** at the level below. These absorb the first edition's criteria for crossing "from aggregate to body". **Status:** RS working hypothesis; not a ladder of substances and not a new layer of reality.

## Between orders

A note is a higher-order identity of vibration; in a melody it functions as a single distinction; a phrase functions as a distinction in a movement. How the step from one order to the next should be classified (a fifth stage, an interface, the first stage of the next order, or a recursive boundary condition) is **open**. The working reading: one identity plays two roles, a distinction *upward* and a constraint *downward* on the relations that sustain it.

Established physics shows the downward role. In Haken's **synergetics**, a laser above threshold is dominated by one light wave whose strength, an *order parameter*, is produced by the atoms' emission and then governs it (the *slaving principle*; *circular causality*). A musical key, established by a piece's notes, then governs how each later note is heard. Synergetics is well-tested physics within matter; it does not establish RS. It does clarify a distinction: a **constraint** confers *potential* consequentiality, affecting what may become actual; a **consequence** occurs when a difference is actually made; T-C-R names the link.

## One pier at a time

None of this derives gravity or quantum mechanics from minimal relations, and the book does not ask it to. In the author's image, we need not bridge the Atlantic; we can investigate one pier and establish what it supports. The project's models are such piers, and negative results count: the hoped-for step from dissolution to new organisation did not appear in the simplest systems.

One candidate for the transition to material individuality comes from **oscillation**: phase-locking (pendulum clocks on a shared beam), mode coupling (a cello's wolf tone), and persistent localised patterns in nonlinear media (solitary waves; the *oscillons* observed in 1996 in vibrated granular layers). The question is whether interacting activity can produce persistent, consequential distinctions, and whether identity might reside in *relations among modes*. The technical volume's knotted structures remain candidates too. **Status:** mechanism candidate, favoured by the author, and not assumed primitive. Oscillation has a second, separate candidacy as an intrinsic reference cadence (Chapter 6); neither role makes it the source of succession.

Music shows emergence *within* matter, not *of* matter, and a soul is not a melody played on a body.

## What this chapter establishes

- Organised identity lives in relations and survives carrier change; preservation in signal differs from recovery by a receiver.
- A compatible receiver may help constitute an identity as manifested (RS hypothesis), without idealism.
- Information about an individual is not the individual; reconstruction yields at most the same class.
- The grammar of emergence, with three tests for higher-order identity, is a working formulation; inter-order transition is open; oscillation is a mechanism candidate.

# Chapter 9: Regularity, Coordination, and the Place of Mathematics

A bicycle tyre's steady pressure is the combined effect of countless erratic molecular impacts, and it obeys a one-line law. If created order is constrained-stochastic at depth, how is the world so reliably regular, and what is the mathematics that describes it so well?

Four problems are distinct. (1) Probabilistically described events can combine into **stable aggregates** without settling whether the underlying processes are chancy. (2) How events are **related** can change the behaviour of the whole while each event's probability is unchanged. (3) A simple law can describe a whole at one scale **without describing what lies beneath**. (4) Such a description may **omit lower-level distinctions** irrelevant to its question but relevant to others.

## Aggregation

The **law of large numbers** guarantees that ten million coin flips almost certainly yield heads within a tenth of a per cent of half; versions hold for dependent events whose dependence fades sufficiently. Physics uses such statistical regularities widely: the gas laws, Brownian motion (traced by Einstein in 1905 to irregular molecular impacts), radioactive half-lives. These establish **reliable aggregate behaviour**, not by themselves a fundamentally stochastic microscopic ontology: classical statistical mechanics may rest on deterministic dynamics, while quantum decay is predicted probabilistically and its interpretation remains contested.

Physicists call large-scale laws **effective**: precise at their scale without describing what lies beneath. **Effective determinism** is behaviour regular and predictable to observable precision, whatever the underlying process: a fact about regularity, not a metaphysical claim. **Mathematical determinism** is a property of equations, where the state at one time fixes all later states; a deterministic large-scale description, such as the fluid equations, does not decide whether underlying processes are deterministic or ontologically stochastic. And an **attractor**, precisely, is a state or set toward which a system tends from many starting points and returns when slightly disturbed; a reliable outcome is not by itself evidence of one (the Chapter 5 calculator succeeded by conservation, not attraction).

## Same odds, different behaviour

Two rooms of a hundred people toss fair coins repeatedly. In one, tosses are independent; in the other, people are paired and each pair reports opposite results. Every individual's chance of heads is one half in both rooms, so the **marginal** probabilities are identical. But the **joint process** differs: the first room's total wanders, the second's is exactly fifty every time.

```text
the same individual odds
  are not the same joint process,
  which is not the same pattern of co-variation,
  which is not the same behaviour of the whole
```

The book calls such relations among chance events **coordination**. It is not a controller above the events; it is a relation among them. And it is not what stabilises a mean; aggregation does that. Coordination governs everything else: the size and shape of fluctuations, whether a quantity is held exactly, the path of the whole, its response to disturbance, and which futures remain open. Exploratory work on rings of locally coupled chance units, not yet a formal record, found that correlation as such was not what mattered. A strong dependence can be irrelevant to a property, and a modest one decisive if aligned with it. Stability also comes in layers: a mean can be stable while fluctuations are not, and some quantities can be exactly conserved while others vary.

None of this mathematics belongs to a relation-first ontology. What the relational proposal changes is the reading: on an object-first reading, dependence structure is an extra fact about how complete things happen to be related; on the relational reading, it belongs to *what the physical whole is*. **Status:** RS working hypothesis (dependence structure is constitutive). The book does not claim that local relations alone account for the regularity of created order.

## What mathematics is doing

Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics" invites the question. The Navier–Stokes equations treat a fluid as a smooth continuum, though it consists of molecules in chaotic motion; they are approximately derivable from that statistical behaviour, fail in very thin gases, and whether they always have smooth solutions is a celebrated open problem. Two lessons: deterministic mathematics at one scale does not imply determinism at every scale, and a smooth field description need not be the ontology of what it describes. The project draws a research question rather than a result: *what kinds of relational constraint and coordination preserve the invariants, correlations and continuity needed for stable effective laws to emerge?*

**Hidden structure.** In the simplest models, adding a genuinely three-way relation and observing only two-way links makes the visible process *appear* to have memory, though nothing in the rules records the past: hidden present structure masquerades as history. Hence **mathematical equivalence under one description does not settle sameness**, and **a mathematical structure that describes behaviour is not, by that fact, what reality fundamentally is.** Mathematics works because created order is structured by constraint, preserved by equivalence and invariance, and regular through coordination, exactly what mathematics describes best.

> `The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork. Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge.`
> *Psalm 19:1-2*

Creation is intelligible, "understood by the things that are made" (Romans 1:20). Scripture does not supply the equations; it explains why a world in which they hold, and in which minds find them, is what a creation made through the Word should be.

## Measurement, model and interpretation

Where measurement and prediction stand, the relational reading must agree. What a field is, whether spacetime is a substance, whether quantum chance is fundamental, and what a particle is between measurements are interpretive and open. In quantum field theory five things are distinct: the formalism; the measurable phenomena it predicts; interpretations of its states and operators; the claim that fields are fundamental; and the claim that whatever is fundamental is material. Predictive success bears directly on the second, not by itself on the others. The same formalism of quantised excitations describes crystal phonons, where no new material entity is inferred. Nothing in the formalism proves the substrate either; what is required is compatibility.

## Gravity and time in the simplest models

The Gravity/Time programme obtained exact results on systems of at most seven points, adopted at that scope. A favouring rule defines a **relational potential**, a weighted count of a link pattern; "attraction" toward a structure is then participation in the favoured pattern, with no centre and no force across. **Status:** mechanism candidate, model-scoped. **Gathering**, a lasting preference for relation to a reference structure, is distinct from **succession**: as systems grew from six to seven points the directed handover kept its direction in every case, while the only accompanying gathering fell to the level of the null comparison. Apparent gathering must survive adjustment for density, and a relational reference is not a neutral frame.

A negative result: the rules do **not** tie activity rates to the potential, so a relational account of gravitational time dilation would need a principle they lack. Every model claim must name its clock, since step counts and continuous time can give opposite signs. None of this is gravity or a theory of time.

## What would count against these proposals

- **Contradicting precise measurement.** Any specific mechanism contradicting measurement fails. One has: a 2026 model predicted the direction of a metal-fatigue order effect but its magnitude many times too small, and was recorded as a failure.
- **Intrinsic properties at the base.** Evidence that fundamental entities have fully interaction-independent properties would seriously weaken relation-first.
- **Entanglement and mediation.** Bell-violating correlations, experimentally confirmed, rule out a class of local explanations; they cannot signal, and several interpretations (relational ones included) account for them. They remain the hardest test for mediated relation, unsolved here.
- **Gravity's clocks.** No relational account of time dilation exists in the project, and the models show one needs a new principle.
- **Stochasticity is hard to test.** Standard interpretations agree on all measurements (collapse theories, testable in principle, aside), which protects the hypothesis and limits the confidence it warrants.
- **Matter's emergence.** B's mechanism would be weakened by persistent failure to obtain individuated organisation from simpler relations; the comparative argument by A or C explaining interoperability as economically. The premise of non-material origin rests on other grounds. The debt is unpaid, not defaulted.
- **The models do not single out RS.** Ordinary computer science and probability explain every model result, and a hoped-for pattern of "dissolution leading to new organisation" did not appear.

The value of the proposals lies in coherence: whether a relation-first account helps the whole of what we know hang together better than its alternatives. Chapter 10 makes that case.

## What this chapter establishes

- Aggregation stabilises means, but statistical regularity does not establish ontic stochasticity.
- Coordination, distinct from correlation, shapes fluctuations, exactness and accessible futures; its constitutive reading is an RS hypothesis.
- Mathematics describes regularity without being the reality; hidden structure can mimic history.
- Model results on gravity and time are scoped and partly negative; explicit defeaters and pressure points are stated.

# Chapter 10: Why Relation Matters Beyond Physics

On Monday you promise to help a friend move house on Saturday; on Saturday you would rather stay in bed, and you go. For the promise to be what it seems, the person who goes must be the person who promised; your words must have become something in her (a reliance); your action must have consequences; and the promise must *bind*, so that staying in bed would wrong her, not merely surprise her. Identity through time, carried distinction, consequence and obligation are basic to human life. How do they sit with what the physical world fundamentally is? This chapter is the hinge of the book.

## The serious alternatives

The tests are those of Chapter 2: connection, valid interfaces, preserved meaning, the round trip, surviving distinctions. The promise crosses every boundary the book cares about: physics and biology, personal life, moral order and faithfulness before God.

An argument aimed only at a crude ontology of self-contained lumps, or at the Humean mosaic of local intrinsic facts, would prove little. The serious alternatives already contain much relation:

- **Powers ontologies**: basic properties are dispositions directed beyond their bearers.
- **Field ontologies**: fields pervading space are the basic physical reality.
- **Hylomorphism** (Aristotle, Aquinas): things are constituted by form, intrinsically ordered toward activity and other things.
- **Process philosophies**: events and connections are more basic than enduring substances.
- **Structural realism**: our best theories most securely describe structure.

Several converge importantly with this book. The question is not whether they make room for relation but **where relation enters and at what cost**, including what common structure each uses to explain interoperability (Chapter 3), and whether it is constitutive relation under another name.

## Where relation enters

In the relational picture, relation is **constitutive** at the base: what a physical thing is cannot be specified apart from how it stands to others (Chapter 6 even defined identity by what possible future interactions could discriminate). The contrast is with ontologies whose fundamental entities are individuated wholly or substantially independently of constitutive relation, so that relation is something already-complete bearers *have* or *exercise*, not what they *are*. Not every substance ontology is like that: a powers theorist may hold essential powers other-directed from the start, and a hylomorphist may hold form constitutive from the beginning. Those views already share part of what this book defends, and the argument is not aimed at them.

The difference is slight in physics and significant for persons, who are partly constituted by parents, promises, friendships, history and standing before God. An ontology whose base lacks constitutive relation must introduce it as something new somewhere between physics and personhood: not impossible, but a discontinuity requiring a bridge assumption. A relation-first base already has the *form* of what appears, transformed, in human life: constitutive relation (Chapter 3), identity as history-bearing equivalence (Chapter 6), preserved and received distinction (Chapter 7), and wholes stabilised by coordination (Chapter 9).

## The argument

1. Human life, as lived and as Scripture describes it, is pervaded by constitutive relation, identity through change, interpersonal consequence and responsibility.
2. A physical ontology whose fundamental entities are individuated independently of constitutive relation must introduce constitutive relationality for the first time at some higher level, as a new kind of reality.
3. A physical ontology with constitutive relation at the base already contains the form of these features, so living, personal and moral relation are continuous in form with physical order while remaining distinct in kind.
4. Other things being equal, an account with fewer unexplained discontinuities and ad hoc bridge assumptions is more globally coherent.
5. Therefore, *if* relation-first physical order is consistent with all observation, it is to be preferred on grounds of global coherence.

The argument does not claim rival ontologies make promises impossible; that would be stronger than needed and probably false. It claims a more economical **continuity of form**, provided category boundaries hold. The conditional in step 5 is why Chapters 3 to 9 attended so closely to physics. If sound, the argument shows that *how* the material world is real bears on whether the rest of reality hangs together.

## Continuity, not reduction

Moral obligation is not a field; love is not covariance; sin is not entropy; the soul is not a carrier; resurrection is not substrate self-repair; divine action is not a wider set of physical permissions. The relational picture does not tell you that you ought to keep your promise (Chapter 13). It removes the strangeness of a world in which promises could bind.

## The emergence objection

"Promises *emerge*, as wetness emerges from molecules that are not wet." The book accepts emergence fully; living organisation is genuinely new and explanatory at its level (Chapter 11). The disagreement is narrower. In familiar cases what emerges expresses capacities the base already has: wetness expresses molecular attraction; life expresses chemistry that already includes catalysis and templating. The question is whether the base contains the **relational resources** that make emergent structures intelligible. If its entities are individuated independently of relation, constitutive relation emerges from a base where nothing is so constituted: assertible, but precisely the discontinuity of step 2. Powers ontologies narrow the gap, which the book counts as convergence, not refutation. Its claim is **continuity of structural capacity**, not derivation of moral truth from physics, and its warrant is comparative.

## The poisoned river

A factory poisons a river; fish die and villagers sicken. Each link is physical, chemicals released, carried and received, yet the case is moral. Run the round trip, from physical chain to responsibility to required action to cleaner water: in a relational creation every step concerns one world. Responsibility is not a chemical fact, but it is not a separate story attached from outside.

> `By swearing, and lying, and killing, and stealing, and committing adultery, they break out, and blood toucheth blood. Therefore shall the land mourn, and every one that dwelleth therein shall languish.`
> *Hosea 4:2-3*

With Romans 8:19-22, Scripture binds human wrongdoing and the created world together. The book does not claim relational physics *explains* these texts; their explanation is theological. It claims a relational account sits naturally with the world they describe, where an account of indifferent objects renders them poetry about a reality not really like that. The author goes further: the created order is designed to bear moral consequence, the theatre in which embodied persons act and the language in which acts take effect. **Status:** theological premise about creation's purpose, not derived from physics.

## Coherence, error and convergence

An argument can grow *more* coherent while moving *further* from truth, if every step builds on an unquestioned early mistake. Developing an AI-assisted method that runs several independent lines of reasoning on one problem, the author observed (in exploratory work) that repeatedly merging the lines into shared summaries raised agreement steadily while an early error hardened into confident consensus: a **false attractor**. Coherence is not fidelity to truth; agreement can come from a shared hidden premise.

The remedy is to ask where agreement came from. **Independent relational convergence**, when lines differing in premises, evidence and method arrive at relations that fit together, carries weight no single line could, as sailors' accounts, bones, a head and a genome constrain one dodo. Its strength depends on real independence: many voices repeating one source are one source, and separate AI systems trained on overlapping material are not independent by default. **Status:** methodological principle.

Error has an anatomy. In "2 + 2 = 5" the numbers, the operation and the question are sound; the error lies in one asserted relation. Intelligible error depends on genuine coherence, though not every falsehood hides a truth. When a claim fails, locate the failed relation, keep what is sound and record what the failure excludes. This is an account of mistaken representation, not of moral evil (Chapter 14). The book's own coherence could be a false attractor; its safeguards are accountability to observation, distinct levels of claim, alternatives kept in view, and submission to Scripture as frame rather than input.

## Relation across categories

![Figure 3. Relation across categories. Material, living, personal, moral and spiritual realities are distinct kinds, not layers built one out of another; relation runs through each in its own way; God is not a category within creation but its Creator, who upholds all things.](v2/figures/fig-categories.png)

Physical relation is constraint, equivalence, coordination and carried distinction; living relation becomes metabolism, signalling and repair; personal relation becomes address and response, knowledge and love; moral relation becomes obligation and wrong; spiritual relation becomes communion with God. God's relation to creation is that of Creator to creature, belonging to no category within it. What runs through them is continuity of form, not a single nature.

## What this chapter establishes

- The serious alternatives contain relation; the question is where it enters and at what cost.
- The central argument: constitutive relation at the base yields fewer discontinuities between physics and persons, conditionally on fitting observation.
- Continuity of form, never reduction; emergence is accepted, with continuity of structural capacity.
- Coherence can mislead; independent convergence and error anatomy are methodological safeguards.

# Chapter 11: Life

Beneath the stone lie a woodlouse, a root, a fungal thread and a germinating seed: things that feed, grow, respond, repair and reproduce. Some pictures of the world are confident with inert matter and hesitant once life appears. If the relational account is right, life should need neither a separate explanatory afterthought nor flattening into merely complicated chemistry.

## Life is given

> `And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so.`
> *Genesis 1:24*

> `Thou takest away their breath, they die, and return to their dust. Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created: and thou renewest the face of the earth.`
> *Psalm 104:29-30*

Living things are real, active and their own: the earth *brings forth*, and they reproduce after their kind. Yet life is never self-grounding; it is given, upheld and withdrawn by God (Job 33:4; Acts 17:25). The account that follows describes how life *operates* within created order, not where it ultimately comes from.

## Sustained organisation

The stone persists largely by being undisturbed. A living thing persists the opposite way, continuously taking in matter and energy, transforming them and expelling waste, re-establishing a pattern through material flow like a flame but far more intricate. **Life is sustained organisation**: persistence actively maintained, under many layers of constraint, by the living thing's own work. In Chapter 6's terms, it continually re-actualises itself within its equivalence class and actively corrects departures; its continuing individuality depends on the unbroken history of the living process.

## Transmission within life

Much of that work is T-C-R. A surface molecule changes shape on detecting a hormone; the change is passed along a signalling cascade and received as a gene switched on or a movement begun. Cells signal one another; nerves and hormones carry differences between organs. A body is a vast composition of nested, parallel and sequential transmissions, coordinated so the whole responds as one. And reception becomes partly self-governed: a cell regulates its receptors, so the admissibility of reception, fixed by molecular structure in the eye's first step, is in living things partly governed by the organism itself.

## Templates and repair

Chapter 6 showed that preservation is not repair: recovery after damage requires surviving information. Life's defining solution is the **template**. DNA participates in shaping protein synthesis, gene activity, division, repair and specialisation; damaged DNA is often repaired from the complementary strand; reproduction copies the template. But DNA is not the only surviving information: the cell's current state, epigenetic marks, its spatial architecture, chemical gradients in developing tissue and its surroundings all carry constraints that guide repair and development.

The first edition called DNA a **constraint map** rather than a program, and the description serves. DNA does not issue commands as software does; it participates in a larger web of constraint (chemistry, membranes, environment, history), and it functions only within the living whole that reads it.

## Chance within life

Molecular chance is pervasive: erratic collisions, bursty gene expression, copying errors. Genetically identical cells in identical conditions behave differently. Yet development is astonishingly reliable, through feedback, redundancy and buffering that correct fluctuations. Development is a striking case of **constrained chance producing reliable form**, the pattern of Chapters 5 and 9 in its most impressive form.

Chance also contributes to variation. "After his kind" speaks of patterned continuity, not rigid sameness. Total rigidity would be lifeless repetition; sheer randomness would mean nothing was inherited. The first edition's term **bounded generativity** is kept: variation belongs within ordered continuity, inside the admissible range. How wide that range is, and how far living kinds have changed historically, are questions on which Christians sharing this book's commitments disagree, and the book does not settle them.

## Higher-order wholes

A tissue is more than a crowd of cells, not because a substance is added but because organisation makes a real difference and explains things at its own level. Following Chapter 9, what matters is coordination aligned with consequence. A collection becomes a **higher-order whole** when its parts are coupled so that they no longer behave independently, disturbances are redistributed through the whole, and the whole has properties, a regulated temperature or a restored shape, that no part has (compare Chapter 8's tests). The body abounds in such coupling: cardiac, respiratory, neural, sleep and hormonal rhythms entrain one another; negative feedback holds temperature, glucose and oxygen within narrow ranges; positive feedback drives threshold processes such as clotting and birth. The organism is less a thing that has a pattern than a pattern that keeps itself in being.

## Direction and purpose

Living things are directed: seeds toward plants, embryos through ordered stages, wounds toward healing. Describing them without saying what parts are *for* is very difficult. The book takes this directedness to be real, neither imposed from outside at every moment nor illusory, but built into the form the organism has been given. This fits Chapter 4: divine purpose shapes what is possible, and the substrate intends nothing. The organism is not a little mind planning its development; its form unfolds, through constrained chance and coordinated relation, toward the end it is shaped for.

> `For thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother's womb. I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made.`
> *Psalm 139:13-14*

God's directing of formation does not compete with the process of development; it works through it.

## What life is not

Life is **not a vital substance or force** added to matter; living things are built from the same created order as stones, and their life is that order's most demanding coordination. Nor is life **nothing but chemistry**: organisation is real and explanatory, the living whole is a unity rather than a heap, and Scripture insists life is given and sustained by God. The breath God sends and withdraws (Psalm 104:29-30) is not one more chemical in the cell.

That leaves living things where Genesis places them, real, fruitful, ordered after their kinds and dependent on their Creator, and brings the book to the threshold Genesis marks with a third use of *bara*.

## What this chapter establishes

- Life is given by God; the account describes its operation, not its ultimate source.
- Life is sustained organisation, using transmission and templates (DNA as constraint map within a larger web).
- Constrained chance yields reliable development; variation is bounded and its historical range left open.
- Higher-order wholes arise through aligned coupling; living directedness is real and built into given form.

# Chapter 12: Dust, Breath, and Person

Chapter 7 followed the seeing of the stone as far as physical description reaches: light, retina, nerve. But the chain ended in *someone* who saw a stone, recognised it, found it beautiful or dull and decided to pick it up. Every earlier link is a transmission between configurations. The last is not. The one who sees is a person, and here the distinction between categories matters most.

## Formed from the dust

> `And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness... So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.`
> *Genesis 1:26-27*

> `And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.`
> *Genesis 2:7*

A body is *formed* from *dust*, the same material as the earth; *breath* is imparted directly by God; the result is a *living soul*. Humanity is not made of a special heavenly substance: "dust thou art" (Genesis 3:19); God "remembereth that we are dust" (Psalm 103:14). The human body belongs fully to the relational physical order of Chapters 3 to 9 and the living order of Chapter 11. But dust alone is not a person. What distinguishes humanity lies in what God has made it to be: an image-bearing, conscious, responsible person called into relation with him.

## Embodiment is good

Genesis does not present dust and breath as opposed principles yoked together, but as jointly making one living creature. The body is part of what a human person is, not a prison. Hence Christian hope is resurrection of the body (Chapter 15), and bodily life carries moral weight: limitation, vulnerability, place, dependence, the capacity to give and to be wounded are the conditions under which creaturely persons love and keep faith.

A human moral act is therefore not a spiritual event with a bodily sign attached. When someone lies, the words, the speaking body and the changes produced in others belong to the act. Its moral reality, a wrong before God and against a neighbour, is not identical with those events and not separate from them. **Within one person the two are causally connected, not identical** (Chapter 1). **Status:** the author's interpretation of a scriptural constraint; the mechanism is open. This is the sense in which created order is the theatre and language of moral consequence (Chapter 10).

## Personhood is deeper than embodiment

> `God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.`
> *John 4:24*

Reasoning only from human cases would define the person by the body. Christian faith rules this out. The Father, the Son and the Spirit are persons; the Father and the Spirit have no bodies; the Son was a person eternally before the Incarnation. If personhood were constituted by embodiment, the doctrine of the Trinity would be incoherent. Since that doctrine is foundational, personhood is the deeper category.

- A **person** is a centre of awareness, relation, agency, address and responsibility: one who can know and be known, speak and be spoken to, act and answer for the act.
- **Embodiment** is the mode in which creaturely persons exist and act within created order. For humans it is not optional, but personhood as such is not a property of bodies. In the Incarnation the eternal Son took the creaturely, embodied mode to himself, permanently.

This is Scripture shaping ontology, and the result is more coherent: it explains why persons cannot be reduced to configurations of matter while honouring the body as the God-given mode of human personhood.

## The inner person

> `For what man knoweth the things of a man, save the spirit of man which is in him?`
> *1 Corinthians 2:11*

> `Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it.`
> *Ecclesiastes 12:7*

> `And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul.`
> *Matthew 10:28*

> `For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day.`
> *2 Corinthians 4:16*

These texts offer no technical theory of soul and spirit, and the book does not settle the debates. Together they establish that the inner person is real, self-known as nothing else knows it, given by God and returning to him, not killed with the body, and renewed while the outward person wastes. The human person has depth, and the inward is not reducible to the outward.

Here the book applies its guard at full force. **The soul is not a configuration of the relational substrate.** Nothing in Chapters 3 to 9 describes, explains or contains it. When the book says relation runs through every category, it means persons are relational beings in a creation relational from its foundation, not that persons are made of the same relations as stones.

## Consciousness

Consciousness depends on body and brain: damage the brain and awareness changes or ceases. Yet why there is anything it is like to see grey, the *hard problem*, remains unsolved by any physics, mainstream or relational, and may exceed creaturely comprehension. **The Relational Substrate does not explain consciousness.** It claims something smaller: a relational world, in which differences already become consequences for something else, is a more fitting setting for conscious persons than a world of sealed objects. The step from a changed retinal molecule to the experience of seeing is not taken by the substrate. **Status:** open question.

## Knowing and choosing as events

Knowing is itself something that happens. In recognising the stone, a received distinction is related to what you already know, and you know something new: observation supplies a distinction, inference receives it, often without deliberate attention. Persons can also hold the not-yet-actual in view and choose among alternatives, and a choice actualises one configuration rather than another: the stone is now somewhere it would not otherwise be. **Knowing and choosing are actualisations in persons.** **Status:** ontological inference. Reasoning about represented relationships at this level the book calls **relational inference**.

None of this makes created order a knower. It is not conscious and did not become capable of consciousness by complexity. Creaturely personhood is where two kinds of reality meet by design: a non-conscious created runtime order fitted to bear consequence, and a conscious capacity for relation to God given with the breath of Genesis 2:7.

## Image and calling

The image of God has been read as reason, moral capacity, dominion, relationship and representative calling. Genesis joins image to dominion. The book's broad reading: the image is not a material feature but a status and calling, to know God and be known, to answer to him and to represent his care within creation. Animals are not called to account in Scripture; humans are.

> `Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.`
> *Isaiah 43:1*

Addressed to Israel, the verse reveals a pattern for personal identity: what makes a person *this* person is not finally a configuration of dust but being known, called and named by God.

## The identity of a person

Chapter 6 proposed that physical things remain the same kind through equivalence and the same individual through lineage. Persons resemble this: bodily material is replaced while you remain, and character and memory carry history. The resemblance explains why replacing atoms does not threaten identity. But it is a **structural analogy**, not a definition. Personal identity is grounded more deeply, in the continuity of the inner person and ultimately in God's knowing and calling. Hence death does not end a person, and resurrection raises the same person rather than producing a copy (Chapter 15).

## What this chapter establishes

- Humans are formed dust and imparted breath: fully within physical order, not reducible to it.
- An embodied act and its moral reality are causally connected, not identical.
- Personhood is deeper than embodiment (from the Trinity); the soul is not a substrate configuration.
- RS does not explain consciousness; knowing and choosing are actualisations in persons; personal identity rests on God's knowing and calling.

# Chapter 13: What Is Allowed

A surgeon and an assailant may make physically near-identical cuts; one heals and the other commits a crime, and nothing in the physics of the cut tells you which. Persons now enter the world of constrained possibility as agents. When we say something is "allowed", what kind of allowing is meant?

## Three registers of admissibility

Chapter 4 promised that *admissible* would carry only its physical sense until now. Three questions must be distinguished:

- **Physical admissibility**: is it possible at all within created order? If not, it is impossible for anyone.
- **Operational admissibility**: is it within this agent's power? If not, it is possible in principle but inaccessible to this agent.
- **Moral admissibility**: is it right? If not, it may be possible and within power, and still wrong.

The registers are independent. Lying is physically possible, within ordinary power, and wrong. Sustaining creation in being occurs, but is within no creature's power. Creating something beautiful is possible, within power and good. Scalpel and knife are equally possible and equally within power; the moral register divides them.

> `And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.`
> *Genesis 3:6*

The fruit was within reach and eating within their power; what was forbidden was the means, wisdom seized by taking what God had withheld. Admissible in the first two registers, not the third.

Collapsing the registers yields three characteristic mistakes, named in the first edition:

- **The naturalist mistake** collapses the moral into the physical: if nature produces it, it cannot really be wrong.
- **The pietist mistake** collapses the physical into the moral: if it is wrong, God will not let it happen.
- **The technological mistake** collapses the physical into the operational: if we can now do it, we should.

## Why the physical cannot settle the moral

This is the book's firmest safeguard against misreading Chapter 10. That chapter's argument **does** imply that moral acts are real events: a lie is sent, carried and received as a change in someone who now believes falsely; a kept promise honours a commitment carried across days. Moral life is not a ghostly overlay on sealed objects it cannot touch.

It **does not** imply that any physical fact settles moral admissibility. The physics of a lie and of a truth is the same physics. A relational account can tell you your words will have consequences; it cannot tell you to tell the truth. The moral question belongs to persons answerable to one another and to God, and is answered by a different authority.

> `He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?`
> *Micah 6:8*

> `Which shew the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness.`
> *Romans 2:15*

What is good is *shown*, revealed by God and written on the heart, not deduced from the behaviour of matter. Moral admissibility is a real layer of created order whose source is the will and character of God.

## Alignment

**Alignment** is fitting the order a thing or person is made for. A bone set straight heals aligned; a string tuned to pitch is aligned with the instrument's design. For persons, alignment is a moral and relational reality, not a measurement: right relation to God, others and creation. Justice, mercy, faithfulness, truthfulness and love are its shape in persons.

The first edition proposed that beauty, order, truth and goodness are "four perspectives on the same structural property". That went too far: it risked turning moral and aesthetic realities into physical configuration, the very collapse this chapter rejects, and it contradicted the technical volume's statement that structural alignment is not a synonym for goodness. This edition withdraws it. Goodness, truth and beauty are related, and one in God, but in creation not reducible to a single physical measure. The book defines **beauty**, in the author's terms, as the apprehensible expression of the relational patterns of goodness, spiritual in origin, within created organisation; a beautiful thing need not be perfect in every part (Chapter 16).

## Agency and responsibility

Moral admissibility presupposes genuine agency; without it, love would be mechanism and praise or blame category mistakes. Christian traditions differ on what agency requires: some hold responsibility requires that one could have done otherwise; others that it is compatible with God's sovereign ordering, provided persons act willingly from their own desires and reasons. The book does not decide between them.

> `I call heaven and earth to record this day against you, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life.`
> *Deuteronomy 30:19*

One point falls within the book's subject. A world whose actualisation is not fixed in advance (Chapter 5) does not settle human freedom in either direction. **Constrained chance is not freedom.** A coin toss is not a choice, and the book makes no claim that agency is molecular chance. Physics cannot settle whether the will is free, and the ontology does not try. Agency belongs to persons, in their own category.

## Time as moral opportunity

> `So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom.`
> *Psalm 90:12*

The days are counted as opportunity, given and limited, for wisdom. **Time as moral opportunity** is not a second kind of time and not a clock unit; it is what succession (Chapter 6) means for a person who must act within it. **Status:** the author's interpretation, within a scriptural constraint.

Opportunity is relative to the person: a nanosecond is no occasion for decision, an hour can be decisive, yet duration is not the measure. An athlete's two minutes rest on months of preparation. Earlier choices change capacity, disposition and even which options are visible, so a moral life is a trajectory in which much is open and nothing begins from nowhere. In this chapter's terms, preparation changes operational admissibility. Responsibility follows the same shape: "unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required" (Luke 12:48), a difference of responsibility, not of worth.

## Boundaries that free

Chapter 4 argued that physical constraint is generative. Scripture says the same of the moral world, emphatically:

> `The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul: the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple.`
> *Psalm 19:7*

> `And I will walk at liberty: for I seek thy precepts.`
> *Psalm 119:45*

Liberty is found *in* seeking God's precepts. A boundary rightly placed does not shrink the space of good action; it gives it shape. This is a **structural analogy**: the constraint that shapes a crystal and the commandment that shapes a life share a form, a boundary that enables. They do not share a nature: the commandment is spoken by a Person to persons and received in conscience, trust and obedience, while the crystal's constraint is received by nothing.

## What this chapter establishes

- Physical, operational and moral admissibility are independent; collapsing them yields naturalist, pietist and technological mistakes.
- Moral acts are real events in a relational world, but no physical fact settles what is right; the source of moral order is God.
- Alignment is moral and relational; the first edition's "four perspectives" claim is withdrawn.
- Constrained chance is not freedom; time is moral opportunity; moral boundaries are generative, by structural analogy.

# Chapter 14: Corruption

An account of reality that speaks only of order soon becomes unbelievable. The stone is beautiful, but the path has litter, the river may be poisoned, and the one who sees may be grieving or lying. The world is good, and it is also deeply wrong.

## Real, but not ultimate

> `Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned.`
> *Romans 5:12*

Scripture excludes two errors: disorder as ultimate, making goodness illusory, and disorder as superficial, a malfunction a serious account may ignore. Since Augustine, Christian thought has held that corruption is **parasitic**: a distortion of the good, with no being of its own, existing only by deforming what God made. Evil cannot create; it can only spoil. Hence the vocabulary of *distortion*, *misalignment* and *rupture*, which presuppose an order.

## The subjection of creation

> `And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life; Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee... for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.`
> *Genesis 3:17-19*

Three things must be kept apart.

**What the text says.** The ground is cursed because of human disobedience; work becomes toil; the man will return to dust. Creation "was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who hath subjected the same in hope" (Romans 8:20). The subjection is God's act. **Status:** scriptural constraint.

**The author's interpretation.** The curse is a deliberate divine realignment of the created runtime order to the consequences of spiritual disobedience, so that creation goes on telling the truth: a world in which the breach with God had no visible consequence would teach falsely. Creation remains an instructor after the Fall, and what it now teaches includes decay, toil and loss. The thesis concerns what changed in creation, not the manner of God's action (Chapter 4). **Status:** theological interpretation.

**The mechanism, which is open.** The author's thesis is that God realigned the primitives themselves, the explicitly intrinsic constraints of Chapter 4, fixed for creatures but not for their Creator. Two alternatives remain in view: God changed parameters within an otherwise unchanged primitive order; or he actualised a new state under the original constraints, so that the new conditions follow as implicitly intrinsic constraints. These are different claims explaining different things, and Genesis describes none. **Status:** open question (research fork).

The author also holds that the realignment introduced **animal mortality**, which was not part of creation's original conditions. The author connects this with Genesis 3:21: "Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them." That the skins mark the first death, cover shame and anticipate sacrifice is the author's reading; the text names no lamb, rite or first death. And the position meets a real difficulty: **the fossil record shows animal death in strata conventionally dated long before humans.** The book's bidirectional standard (Chapter 2) does not permit that interface to be ignored. By the author's decision, the book states the position, names the conflict with conventional chronology as an **open problem**, and does not resolve it.

On every reading of the mechanism, the subjection is God's act, and God's acts are not corrupt. The futility imposed is a judgment, coherent, purposed and given "in hope". Corruption suffered in higher orders of creation does not make its primitive order incoherent. Nor does every suffering follow from the sufferer's sin: "Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents" (John 9:3). Creation's subjection is the condition of all, not a ledger of each.

## Corruption as persisting misalignment

Misalignment alone is not the whole of corruption: growing creatures depart from mature form and learners err. A wrong act is wrong in itself, judged by the moral order of Chapter 13, not by duration: a single, swiftly repented lie was still a lie. What persistence explains is **entrenchment**: unrestored misalignment hardens into habit, narrows what a person can see and choose, and reaches into others. Corruption in the full sense includes both the wrong and its entrenchment.

> `But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.`
> *Hebrews 3:13*

> `Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and when sin is finished, it bringeth forth death.`
> *James 1:15*

Chapter 6 helps in two of Chapter 1's senses. As **structural analogy**: the past persisting in present configuration, narrowing what is next, has the same form in the bent paper clip and in a habit of deceit. As **causal connection**: sin belongs to persons in relation to God and is no configuration of the substrate, but its embodied consequences are real configurations, carried in dispositions, body and relations. Corruption works with the grain of a relational world against the purpose for which it was made.

## Corruption in transmission

T-C-R gives a second lens, used where it clarifies. A lie transmits a false distinction; slander a false distinction about a person; a broken promise breaks a carriage relied upon; gossip distorts in carriage. A believed lie changes what someone does, which changes others, until effects pass into decisions and circumstances never traced back to it. That history is real, carried in all the changed relations, the only record created order keeps; nothing guarantees a creature can recover it. **Consequence persists where provenance is lost.** Corruption can also be a **failure of reception**: "I spake unto you, rising up early and speaking, but ye heard not" (Jeremiah 7:13). These lenses help us see patterns Scripture already describes; they do not explain sin, and a single technical vocabulary for every sin would trivialise it.

## Five patterns

The first edition's five patterns of unrestored misalignment remain useful at every scale. **Degradation**: the thinning of what should hold, where sustained input is not arriving. **Fragmentation**: a whole broken into parts no longer in real relation, where coupling has failed. **Inversion**: what should serve comes to rule. **Counterfeit**: the form of a good kept while its substance is betrayed, the deception compounding the loss. **Parasitism**: sustenance drawn from a good it cannot generate, weakening its host. They combine, and none is creative; all depend on an order still functioning around them.

## Suffering, sin and limitation

Scripture voices suffering without denying creation's goodness ("My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?", Psalm 22:1) and insists humans are agents of corruption, not only victims ("All we like sheep have gone astray", Isaiah 53:6). Corruption is personal and culpable. The relational account shows how wrongdoing reaches others and the land; it cannot make sin less personal.

**Limitation is not corruption.** Finitude (sleep, partial knowledge, location, dependence) is good creaturehood, which Genesis calls very good. If every limit were evil, salvation would mean ceasing to be a creature; if corruption were collapsed into limitation, death, cruelty and injustice would be softened into mere finitude.

**Darkness, again.** The darkness of Genesis 1:2 is not evil. Moral darkness is not a created substance but *hiddenness turned to disorder*: the created capacity for concealment seized for evasion and deceit (John 3:19).

**Remembrance.** What holds a people aligned over time? Scripture insists on remembrance: Passover, memorial stones, feasts, the retold exodus, the Lord's Supper (Luke 22:19). In the book's terms, remembrance is how a people keeps carrying what was given, so that what God did is received again in each generation. Forgetting is an ordinary road into drift.

## What this chapter establishes

- Corruption is real and parasitic; it cannot create.
- The subjection is God's act; the realignment reading is the author's interpretation; its mechanism is an open research fork; post-Fall animal mortality is the author's interpretation, with the fossil-record conflict named and unresolved.
- Wrongness is intrinsic; entrenchment explains spread; corruption runs through transmission and failed reception.
- Limitation is good creaturehood; remembrance renews alignment.

# Chapter 15: Restoration

Optimism, distraction, denial and private consolation are not hope. Hope in the strong sense requires that reality contain more promise than corruption can destroy. If disorder is ultimate, hope is theatre; if creation is God's, hope may be the most realistic stance there is. This chapter's first claim is a boundary on which the rest depends.

## Restoration is God's act

Chapter 6 showed that preserving is not repairing: when information is destroyed, conservation keeps what remains and restores nothing lost. Living things partly overcome this with templates (Chapter 11), but that capacity fails; every living thing dies. The healing of creation cannot be expected from creation's own resources. If restoration came only from the persistence of the world's invariants, there would be no raising of the dead, no righting of wrongs, no new creation.

> `For it pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell; And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.`
> *Colossians 1:19-20*

The One through whom all things were made and in whom they consist is the One through whom they are reconciled. Redemption is not alien to creation, but it is *his* answer: not a process within the substrate, a self-correction of physical order, or a law of nature. Hence Chapter 4's insistence that God is not one more cause among causes, and that the book offers no account of the manner of God's action. The resurrection of Christ was not an unusually improbable configuration of the substrate but an act of the Creator. Miracles are received as Scripture records them, neither explained as hidden physics nor explained away.

## The scope of hope

> `Because the creature itself also shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God.`
> *Romans 8:21*

> `And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away. And he that sat upon the throne said, Behold, I make all things new.`
> *Revelation 21:4-5*

The hope is not souls escaping a ruined world but creation delivered, all things reconciled, new heavens and a new earth (2 Peter 3:13), the wolf dwelling with the lamb (Isaiah 11:6). On an ontology of sealed objects with no intrinsic bearing on persons, the redemption of persons and the renewal of creation would be two unrelated projects, and some forms of Christianity have drifted toward exactly that. On the relational account, persons are formed from the dust of a creation relational from its foundation, so their wounding and healing are bound up with it. The ontology does not *explain* redemption. It shows why Scripture's redemption is of *this* world.

## Restoring reception

Chapter 14 described corruption partly as failed reception; Scripture describes restoration partly as reception restored.

> `A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.`
> *Ezekiel 36:26*

The gospel that comes "in word only" has arrived; the gospel that comes "in power, and in the Holy Ghost" (1 Thessalonians 1:5) has been received. This is **structural analogy**: the Spirit's work is not a physical process, faith is not a receptor state, and hearing the gospel is not an exactly-once transaction; Scripture speaks of a word received, rehearsed, remembered and received again. What is shared is only the outline of something sent, carried and received as change.

## The same person, raised

> `Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.`
> *Luke 24:39*

> `It is sown in corruption; it is raised in incorruption: It is sown in dishonour; it is raised in glory: it is sown in weakness; it is raised in power: It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body.`
> *1 Corinthians 15:42-44*

The risen Christ is recognisably himself, wounds included. Resurrection raises the same persons, transformed, not replacements, and it concerns all, to life or to judgment (John 5:28-29).

How can the same person be raised when the body has returned to dust? Chapter 6 distinguished equivalence from individual continuity: a reconstruction may belong to the same class without being the same individual, and conservation cannot restore destroyed carriers of identity. Chapter 12 added that personal identity is grounded in the inner person and in God's knowing and calling. So resurrection is **not** the substrate preserving a person's invariants through death and **not** the reconstruction of an indistinguishable replacement. Death destroys the configuration; the spirit "shall return unto God who gave it" (Ecclesiastes 12:7); the identity of the person raised is held by God. The book makes no claim about mechanism: "it doth not yet appear what we shall be" (1 John 3:2). Its contribution is negative clarity: it shows why resurrection cannot be a natural process of created order, and so must be, as Scripture says, the act of God.

## Human death and the Cross

Human death is bound up with sin (Romans 5:12) and is an enemy to be destroyed (1 Corinthians 15:26); it is not humanity's intended end. The author's further interpretation, that animal death entered with the Fall, carries the open fossil-record problem named in Chapter 14. The narrower scriptural claim stands on any reading.

> `No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again.`
> *John 10:18*

In the ordinary case death is corruption prevailing over a creature that cannot resist. Christ's death is presented as yielded: "he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost" (John 19:30). The suffering and death were real; corruption did not overtake him: "neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption" (Psalm 16:10).

## Restored alignment can be higher than before

Offered as theological and pastoral reflection, not binding doctrine and not a claim that evil was necessary: untested innocence is real goodness but unowned. Disorder exposes what was hidden; truthfully faced, repented of and repaired, it can yield alignment that is conscious, tested, chosen and humbler, as in a marriage rebuilt or a faith come through doubt. Restoration does not erase history: forgiveness changes what can follow from a wrong without undoing every effect, and wound and repair remain part of the story (Hebrews 12:11; Romans 5:3-4).

Four clarifications. First, disorder is not good in itself; God redeems evil that occurred, a greater claim than that evil was needed. Second, restoration is costly; at creation's scale it cost the Cross, foreordained "before the foundation of the world" (1 Peter 1:20), which the author reads as a real commitment made before creation concerning an event within its history, a decision rather than a forecast. Third, freedom is for love and trust, not a licence; faith must be a genuine, willing response, however the traditions describe the freedom involved. Fourth, freedom never required actualised evil: before the Fall the command and the possibility of disobedience were real (Genesis 2:16-17), yet creation was "very good" (Genesis 1:31). **Status:** scriptural constraint (inference from the text).

## Consummation and judgment

The hope is consummation, not mere return to Eden: death "swallowed up in victory" (1 Corinthians 15:54). Judgment belongs to it; without judgment redemption would be sentimental (Matthew 25:46). Such judgment does not depend on what a damaged record can establish. Created order carries every act's consequences but not every act's provenance; God knows the whole history of each act, its intention, circumstances, effects and responsibility, which is why his judgment can be just where human courts fail. Greater material damage is not by itself greater guilt.

Between Christ's resurrection and the renewal of all things, the gathered people of God, in concrete local assemblies, can be real if imperfect places where restored relation becomes visible: truth spoken and received, wrongs confessed and forgiven, remembrance kept. The book attempts no doctrine of the church.

The final answer to corruption is not a better ontology but the Lord who creates, judges, redeems and renews. What the ontology can show is that this hope concerns the world we inhabit.

## What this chapter establishes

- Creation cannot heal itself; restoration is God's act, not a natural process.
- The hope is the renewal of this world, which the relational account shows to be one work with the redemption of persons.
- Resurrection raises the same person, held by God; it is neither preservation of invariants nor reconstruction.
- Restored alignment can exceed untested innocence, but evil was never necessary; judgment rests on God's complete knowledge.

# Chapter 16: One World, Many Kinds

The stone is still on the path, unchanged since the first page. What has changed, the book hopes, is how much can be seen in it. It is a creature, made and held by God. On the book's proposal it is a stable form within a created order of relation: real and distinct, but constituted within relation. Its possible forms were bounded and weighted before any became actual, those bounds shaped by purpose though the stone intends nothing. Its history unfolded by constrained chance. It persists because its changes preserve what it is. It reaches you because a difference at its surface constrains the light, is preserved by a chain of dependency, and becomes a consequence in you. And you, who see it, are formed from the same dust and yet a person: known and called by God, answerable, wounded by corruption and offered restoration. You belong to the same world as the stone, and are not the same kind of thing.

## The shape of the whole

God creates and upholds reality; created physical order is real and not self-grounding. The RS proposal is that it is relational rather than object-first. Actualisation is conditioned in advance (T0) by what is forbidden and favoured; actual history shapes what is currently reachable; actualisation is, on the working hypothesis, constrained-stochastic, consistent with but not established by quantum theory. Sameness of kind is the relevant equivalence class judged by reachable futures, certified by invariants; individual continuity is a further, open question of lineage. Regularity is the stable behaviour of many events, constrained and coordinated; mathematics describes it without being it. T-C-R names how any distinction becomes consequential, and the book proposes it as the form of consequential relation itself, with cause as a distinction modifying what another state can become. Material things are one achievement of this order, distinct from material organisation such as sound; *that* materiality rests on a non-material foundation is a premise, *how* a hypothesis. Organised identities persist across carriers where constitutive relations are preserved and compatible receivers recover them; identities gather recursively from distinction to higher-order wholes.

Because material reality is relational from its foundation, life, personhood, morality, consequence, corruption and restoration need not be bolted onto a world of a different kind. Yet the categories remain distinct: the substrate is not the soul, physical transmission is not divine speech, a law of physics is not a commandment, and creation is not God.

## What kind of confidence this deserves

**Scripture** asserts creation, the Creator–creature distinction, the reality of persons, corruption, restoration and resurrection: held as commitments of faith, received rather than derived, without making Scripture say more than it says.

**Observation and mathematics**: physics' precision, probabilistic quantum predictions, stable large-scale laws. None is the book's own or in dispute. What is disputed is that any one interpretation, including independent objects in a container of space, inherits their authority.

**Philosophical argument**: description does not settle ontology; elasticity is excluded; plausibility can be compared without numbers; structural analogy is not identity; global coherence is a stronger test than local success. These stand or fall with their arguments.

**The RS proposal itself**: relation as foundational, constrained stochastic actualisation, relational equivalence, T-C-R, emergent materiality, and a recursive grammar of emergence. These are hypotheses. The models show them coherent and workable, not that nature works this way, and they mark limits, such as no relational account of gravity's effect on clocks. The case for them is Chapter 10's: together they make the whole of what we know hang together better than the alternatives.

That calls for **disciplined confidence**: confidence where grounds are strong, openness where they are not, and honesty about which is which. The status table that follows gathers the main claims.

## The limits of creaturely knowing

> `Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou hast understanding.`
> *Job 38:4*

> `For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known.`
> *1 Corinthians 13:12*

Creatures know from inside creation, through effects, instruments and inference. Like the caller of a private function (Chapter 2), we learn behaviour well without its inner nature being fixed by what we learn. That is not a defect research will remove; it is creatureliness, and it is why the book's warrant is a coherence argument held with disciplined confidence, within the frame of Scripture, which comes from the One who laid the foundations. Yet Job's question is no rebuke to inquiry: the God who asks it invites Job to look, and "the honour of kings is to search out a matter" (Proverbs 25:2). Partial knowledge is knowledge.

> `Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.`
> *John 14:6*

The author reads the three words together: the way as a road walked, the truth as what grounds it, the life as participation in it, with the Father, one with the Son (John 10:30), as destination. That is a theological reading, offered as one. It fits the book's account of inferential logic: partial understanding refined toward a coherence only God knows perfectly. Truth is what is so; the way toward it is walked by creatures and is not identical with the truth it seeks.

The fitting response is **wonder**: a creaturely response to coherence that is intelligible and yet exceeds present comprehension. On the author's account creation was made to be read this way: its order represents something of the spiritual order prior to it, and its **beauty**, the apprehensible expression of the patterns of goodness, spiritual in origin, can be met in created things without being exhausted by them.

## Living in a relational world

These are not deductions from physics, which would contradict Chapter 13, but ways of living that fit the grain of the world. Science is to be honoured as the study of a real, ordered creation, and held in its place, since description does not answer what the order is, what it is for or how to live in it. Consequence is to be taken seriously: words, promises, neglect and care go out, are carried and are received. Wisdom is more than technique: "Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established" (Proverbs 24:3). And creation is to be cared for, since it groans and waits with us (Romans 8:22; Hosea 4:3).

## The end of the matter

> `Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man. For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil.`
> *Ecclesiastes 12:13-14*

The framework ends there, because that is where everything it describes finally rests.
