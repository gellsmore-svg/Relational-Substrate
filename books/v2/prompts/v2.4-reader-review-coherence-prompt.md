# 1. First principles for this pass

This is **not** another generic "improve the prose" pass.

It is a coherence-governance pass.

The mature book must communicate a clear thesis:

> Created physical reality is relational from its foundation, and the strength of that proposal is not merely that it can be made compatible with existing physics. Its strength must be assessed by **global coherence**: whether one created order can coherently account for physical regularity, life, identity, consequence, personhood, moral reality, corruption, restoration and Scripture without silently changing categories, inventing unexplained interfaces, or mistaking mathematical description for ontology.

The book has become impressively careful, but in places it has become **too careful in the wrong way**. It sometimes reads like a conventional science textbook whose claims have been wrapped in repeated caveats, rather than a confident ontological argument that uses science as evidence and constraint.

Correct that.

The book should not be reckless. It should be **clear enough to be wrong**.

A hypothesis should be stated as a hypothesis.
An inference should be stated as an inference.
An observation should be stated as an observation.
A theological conclusion should be stated as a theological conclusion.
An unresolved question should be called unresolved.

Do **not** dissolve claims into endless "may", "might", "could", "perhaps", "does not claim", and "should not be taken to mean" language.

The objective is not "avoid overclaiming at all costs." Underclaiming is also an epistemic failure. Error is inevitable in exploratory reasoning. The discipline is to make the claim visible, state why it is believed, label its epistemic status, and remain willing to revise it.

A useful authorial standard is:

> **Here is what this book proposes. Here is why. Here is what supports it. Here is what would count against it. Here is what remains unknown. Now follow the consequences.**

# 4. Re-centre the book on global coherence

The most important conceptual correction is that **global coherence must be genuinely global and bidirectional**.

The present book often gets one direction right:

> Relational Substrate proposals must not contradict established observations, successful calculations, or well-tested physical models.

Keep that.

But the reverse responsibility is underdeveloped:

> The ontological and metaphysical assumptions commonly carried by contemporary physics must themselves be subjected to global-coherence testing.

Physics does not receive a privileged exemption from coherence because it is physics.

Make the distinction precisely:

- **observation** constrains the ontology;
- **measurement** constrains the ontology;
- **successful mathematics** constrains what an adequate ontology must explain;
- **repeatable empirical regularity** constrains the ontology;
- but a conventional **interpretation**, **metaphysical picture**, **entity vocabulary**, or **disciplinary habit** does not gain evidential authority merely because it is familiar, mainstream, published, or widely taught.

Consensus, authority, familiarity and publication volume are not themselves evidence.

This does **not** mean rejecting modern physics. It means refusing to confuse:

1. what was observed;
2. what was measured;
3. the mathematical relation that predicts it;
4. the physical model used to organise it;
5. an ontological interpretation of the model;
6. a metaphysical worldview in which the interpretation feels natural.

The book must visibly keep those layers apart.

## 4.1 Define global coherence more strongly

Global coherence is not just "no contradiction."

The mature concept should include at least:

- **connectivity across domains** — the ontology can connect physical order, life, personhood, moral consequence and theology without unexplained metaphysical jumps;
- **valid interfaces** — when concepts cross domains, the interface is explained rather than assumed;
- **preservation of relevant distinctions** — a concept must not silently change meaning during translation;
- **propagation stability** — solving a problem in one domain must not break prior coherence elsewhere;
- **cycle consistency** — following a concept across several domains and back should not return a materially different concept without explanation;
- **category integrity** — connection is not reduction; structural analogy is not ontological identity;
- **epistemic compatibility** — empirical observations, mathematics, philosophical inference and theological revelation must be distinguished while remaining mutually accountable.

Retain the guard:

> Coherence is not itself proof of truth.

But say that once, clearly. Do not use it as a recurring apology.

## 4.2 The book's disciplinary purpose

The book exists partly because local disciplines can be highly successful while leaving **ontological debts** unpaid.

A theory can predict extremely well while still not answering:

- why its entities are mutually compatible;
- why the same interaction grammar holds everywhere;
- why mathematically described regularities exist at all;
- why life and personhood can arise in one coherent created order;
- why moral consequence is not metaphysically alien to the physical world;
- what is description versus what is claimed to exist.

The book should have the confidence to ask those questions.

# 11. TCR requires a fundamental rewrite of emphasis

This is perhaps the most important conceptual change in this pass.

The current book treats Transmit–Carry–Receive largely through examples within the manifested physical world.

Those examples are valid.

But they miss the deeper point.

## 11.1 New central formulation

Treat TCR as:

> **the grammar of consequential manifestation**

and, more minimally:

> **the logical structure required for one distinction to become consequential to another state.**

This is deeper than a communication model.

Do not present TCR primarily as:

- messages travelling;
- information packets;
- signals moving through pre-existing space;
- three little physical components;
- a telecom metaphor.

Those are later manifestations.

At substrate depth, the roles are functional.

### Transmit

A distinction in one relational condition is capable of constraining something other than itself.

### Carry

The dependency between source distinction and consequence preserves enough of the relevant distinction for the consequence to depend upon it.

The "carrier" need not be a separate object.

At the most primitive level, **the relation itself may be the carrier**.

A useful test is:

> If source distinction A and source distinction B can never make any difference to any downstream or relational consequence, then that distinction has not been carried in the relevant sense.

### Receive

Another state becomes actual, or its admissible actualisation is constrained, **because of** the source distinction.

Reception is consequence, not mere arrival.

## 11.2 What TCR must not presuppose

Do not smuggle in any of the following:

- physical travel;
- pre-existing metric space;
- a sequence of spatial locations;
- an intermediate object;
- encoding/decoding unless a particular case requires it;
- dimensional reduction;
- a mapping from one geometry into another;
- a stack of "invisible layer → transformation layer → visible layer";
- exactly-once reception;
- loss/consumption of the source distinction.

The earlier corrections to TCR remain binding:
source distinction need not be consumed;
reception can be repeated, cumulative, saturating, redundant, destructive or otherwise semantics-dependent;
exactly-once is a transactional subcase.

## 11.3 TCR and manifestation

The deep reason TCR matters is that an underlying created relational order can only be consequentially manifest as material/experiential reality if distinctions at that level bear a consequence-preserving relation to manifested actuality.

Avoid saying that an invisible thing is necessarily **transformed** into a visible thing.

That is only one possible architecture.

The substrate and manifested physical runtime might be:

- two organisational descriptions of one relational fact;
- different resolutions of one relational order;
- related through an unknown mapping;
- intrinsically dimensionally related;
- or organised in some way not yet conceptualised.

Therefore write carefully.

The key claim is not "hidden object X travels upward and becomes visible object Y."

It is:

> **There must be a consequence-preserving relationship between underlying relational distinction and manifested actuality.**

That is the TCR insight.

## 11.4 TCR may be substrate-deep

Explore the stronger proposition:

> TCR is not an extra mechanism bolted onto a relational substrate. It may be the operational form of consequential relation itself.

If A and B are genuinely related in a consequential sense, something about A must be capable of making a difference to B.

That primitive shape is:

`distinction -> preserved dependency -> consequence`

That is recognisably TCR.

This may explain why TCR reappears at many levels:
not because light, DNA, nerves, speech and substrate mechanics coincidentally use the same engineering pattern, but because **anything consequentially relational must instantiate some version of the same logical grammar**.

State this as a serious hypothesis, not as established fact.

## 11.5 TCR and causality

Explore whether TCR gives the book a more precise relational account of cause and effect.

In a stochastic ontology, cause need not mean deterministic production.

A distinction may cause consequence by:

- excluding some next states;
- making others admissible;
- changing their weighting;
- constraining correlations;
- preserving an invariant;
- altering subsequent accessibility.

A possible working formulation is:

> A causal relation exists where a distinction in one relational condition modifies the admissible actualisation or consequential state of another through a distinction-preserving dependency.

Test this formulation against the existing stochastic work before adopting it.

## 11.6 TCR may be recursive rather than one-way

A receiver at one stage can immediately become a transmitter relative to another relation.

Therefore substrate-level TCR may be recursively chained or networked rather than a simple linear pipeline.

Explore whether this gives a better account of the relationship between present actuality, future admissibility and inherited provenance.

# 13. Universal interoperability is an ontological datum

Bring forward a line of reasoning that is currently underexpressed.

Consider materially similar rocks in Hampshire and Australia.

The important question is not simply why they look alike.

It is:

> **Why are independently located physical things mutually intelligible to one another at all?**

They participate in the same interaction grammar.

More broadly:

- why can object A affect object B?
- why does B possess compatible receptivity?
- why do the same interaction relations hold in remote locations?
- why are physical quantities commensurable?
- why can matter form stable higher-order structures?
- why do independently located instances share interoperable interfaces?
- why can newly encountered material entities participate in the same causal grammar?

An ontology of wholly self-contained, independently individuated objects cannot simply take this universal interoperability for free.

It must explain the common interface.

Possible responses include:

- common laws;
- common fields;
- shared symmetries;
- common forms;
- universal dispositions;
- common generative history;
- constitutive relational ground.

The point is not to caricature alternatives. The point is to **charge every ontology for the explanatory machinery it uses**.

A relation-first substrate makes universal interoperability less surprising because apparent objects inherit compatibility from common relational grounding.

Develop this as an **explanatory-coherence argument**, not a pseudo-numerical probability calculation unless a defensible sample space can actually be defined.

The earlier Australia/UK rock probability exploration is useful as provenance of the question, but the mature argument is stronger when stated as:

> **Universal interoperability requires explanation.**

and:

> **A common relational ground explains universal interoperability naturally; an object-first ontology must recover that common grammar somewhere else.**

This argument should strengthen the relation-first thesis in the main book, probably in the early ontology chapters and/or where Chapter 9 already handles interactional versus constitutive relation.

Protect Chapter 9's existing strength.

# 28. Acceptance criteria

The revision is successful only if a careful reader can answer all of the following after reading the book:

1. **Why does this book exist?**
  
  - Because local explanatory success is not enough; reality must cohere globally across physical, living, personal, moral and theological domains.
2. **What is being proposed?**
  
  - A created physical order relational at its foundation, with constrained/stochastic actualisation as a live mechanism hypothesis.
3. **What is the status of modern physics?**
  
  - Highly successful observation/mathematics/model constraints, but not an ontological authority whose conventional interpretation is exempt from coherence testing.
4. **What is T0?**
  
  - A formal/current handle on the conditioning of actualisation whose minimal role is clearer than its final ontology; "possibility space" is one live interpretation, not an unquestioned substance or realm.
5. **What is T1?**
  
  - Actualised, history-bearing relational order, with provenance relevant when future consequence can discriminate it.
6. **What is TCR?**
  
  - The grammar of consequential manifestation/relation: distinction, preserved dependency, consequential reception — without presupposing packet transport, physical travel or a particular geometry.
7. **Why relation-first rather than relation-added-later?**
  
  - Because universal interoperability/common interface is itself an ontological fact requiring explanation, and common relational grounding supplies a natural account.
8. **Does the book claim the substrate is spatial?**
  
  - No. Intrinsic, emergent and proto-spatial possibilities remain open unless stronger evidence is developed.
9. **Does coherence prove RS?**
  
  - No. Coherence is the appropriate comparative discipline where empirical description underdetermines ontology.
10. **Is the book frightened of making claims?**
  
  - No. It states hypotheses clearly and labels their status.
11. **Does the book read like a science textbook?**
  
  - No. Science supports and constrains the ontology; it does not determine the narrative voice.
12. **Does the book imply a research team?**
  
  - No. Authorship/research provenance is accurate: one human researcher/author with extensive AI-assisted and computational exploration.
13. **Can the reader find the current ontology in one place?**
  
  - Yes. The new Current Ontological Reference gives the current hierarchy, claim status, open questions, rejected reifications and definitions.
14. **Are Chapters 9–14 still excellent?**
  
  - They should remain at least as strong and readable as before.
