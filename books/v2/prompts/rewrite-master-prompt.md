## Mission

Work autonomously and comprehensively on the Relational Substrate repository to create a fundamentally rewritten second edition of:

> **Coherent Biblical Ontology**

# 3. TCR MUST BECOME A CENTRAL PILLAR

The existing book does not give sufficient explanatory weight to:

> **Transmit – Carry – Receive**
> 
> **T-C-R / TCR**

This must be corrected.

TCR is not to be introduced as a piece of technical jargon and then occasionally referenced.

Treat it as one of the principal conceptual bridges of the entire ontology.

Develop it carefully from first principles.

A useful current formulation to investigate is:

> **Transmit relational difference → Carry persistent constrained correlation → Receive state change**

Do not assume this exact wording is final.

Derive the best wording from the research.

The reader should understand deeply:

### Transmit

What does it mean ontologically for some difference, constraint, state or relation to become consequential beyond its immediate locality?

### Carry

What persists between transmitter and receiver?

Is the carrier:

- an object;
- a relation;
- a persistent constraint;
- a correlation;
- a configuration;
- a succession of admissible relationships;
- something else?

How can a carrier preserve consequence without necessarily preserving implementation?

What does carrier substitutability tell us?

### Receive

What makes transmission real?

A transmission which never has any possible consequential reception is ontologically different from one which alters the state or admissibility of another relation.

Explain reception as **consequence**, not merely arrival.

Investigate whether the deepest useful TCR grammar is therefore:

```text
difference
→ transmissibility
→ constrained continuity
→ consequential reception
```

or another more precise formulation.

# 7. GLOBAL COHERENCE VERSUS LOCAL AND REGIONAL COHERENCE

Develop a precise hierarchy:

## Local coherence

A model explains a bounded phenomenon internally.

Examples might include:

- an equation fitting observations;
- a physical model predicting a measured quantity;
- an explanation working within a limited regime.

## Regional coherence

Several adjacent domains can be integrated.

Examples might include:

- chemistry and physics;
- molecular and biological behaviour;
- information and organism-level regulation.

## Global coherence

The complete account of reality remains mutually compatible across:

- physical observation;
- mathematical description;
- material ontology;
- life;
- consciousness/personhood;
- morality;
- meaning;
- philosophy;
- lived human experience;
- historical knowledge;
- theology;
- Scripture;
- corruption;
- redemption;
- restoration.

The second edition should argue that **local explanatory success is insufficient evidence of ontological truth if the assumed ontology produces contradiction elsewhere.**

However, distinguish clearly between:

1. this book's methodological thesis;
2. accepted scientific practice;
3. philosophical inference;
4. empirical observation.

Do not dishonestly present "global coherence" as though contemporary science universally accepts this framework.

Argue the case.

The book should examine the proposition that:

> if all material things emerge within/from the same relational substrate, then they are not fundamentally isolated entities which happen occasionally to interact; their identities and possibilities are already constituted within a common relational order.

- restoration;

Again, do not infer that morality is "physics".

The argument is subtler:

**a globally coherent ontology requires there not to be an inexplicable metaphysical gulf between a supposedly intrinsically disconnected physical world and a reality in which relationship, consequence, responsibility and ordered dependence are fundamental to human existence.**

Investigate whether a relation-first physical ontology removes that discontinuity.

# 9. THE BOOK IS NOT AN ATTEMPT TO "PROVE THE RELATIONAL SUBSTRATE"

State this explicitly.

The project must not be presented as:

> "Can we empirically prove that the Relational Substrate is the ultimate ontology?"

That is an inappropriate epistemic target.

The deeper ontology behind observations is not directly available to empirical inspection merely by accumulating measurements.

Empirical work can:

- test predictions;
- reject incompatible models;
- reveal regularities;
- constrain possible explanations;
- expose contradictions;
- demonstrate unexpected relationships;
- compare explanatory adequacy;
- test whether proposed mechanisms reproduce observed behaviour.

It does not automatically convert metaphysical interpretation into directly observed ontology.

The Relational Substrate project is therefore primarily:

> **a global coherence exercise constrained by observation, experiment, mathematics, reason, lived reality and — in CBO specifically — Scripture.**

This distinction must be articulated much more clearly than in the first edition.

# 10. HUMAN KNOWLEDGE ITSELF IS COHERENCE-BASED

Investigate this carefully and research it properly.

Humans do not generally possess exhaustive deterministic access to reality.

Human cognition operates under uncertainty.

We:

- infer;
- recognise patterns;
- compare accounts;
- accumulate evidence;
- revise priors;
- anticipate likely outcomes;
- detect contradiction;
- construct coherent models;
- operate probabilistically even where the subjective experience feels certain.

This provides an important epistemological connection between the project's method and ordinary human knowing.

Do not turn this into an unsupported claim that the brain literally implements a specific RS stochastic algorithm.

The more modest and useful point is:

> human understanding itself normally depends upon assembling the most coherent account available from incomplete evidence.

That makes global coherence a legitimate epistemic concern rather than an eccentric substitute for empirical method.

Research this carefully before writing.

# 11. SCIENCE, DESCRIPTION AND ONTOLOGY

Retain and improve the strongest parts of the existing discussion concerning the distinction between:

- observing an effect;
- measuring an effect;
- mathematically describing an effect;
- predicting an effect;
- modelling a mechanism;

Modern physics is extraordinarily successful descriptively.

Do not say that science "has no clue" about gravity, magnetism, light or similar phenomena.

- magnetism;
- light;

- mathematical formalism;
- experimentally supported theory;
- effective mechanism;
- unresolved interpretation;
- ontological extrapolation.

The book becomes stronger when it gives mainstream science full credit for what it genuinely knows while refusing to pretend that descriptive success eliminates metaphysical assumptions.

# 16. MODEL THE "HUMAN CONTEXT WINDOW"

Create an explicit editorial model of how much conceptual context a human reader must retain at each major point in the book.

Do **not** pretend that humans have token windows analogous to LLMs.

Use "human context window" as a useful editorial metaphor grounded in research concerning:

- working memory;
- cognitive load;
- chunking;
- schema acquisition;
- discourse coherence;
- concept mapping;
- inferential distance;
- prior knowledge.

Research this literature before defining the metric.

Create a heuristic such as **Human Context Load (HCL)**.

It should consider at least:

```text
N = number of novel active concepts
P = number of prerequisite concepts that must be actively recalled
I = interaction density between those concepts
A = abstraction level
D = dependency depth
R = recall distance since prerequisite explanation
T = terminology burden
X = inferential steps left implicit
S = support from examples/analogy/diagram
F = familiarity to intended reader
```

Do not blindly combine these into a meaningless pseudo-scientific scalar.

A vector is preferable.

For each important section estimate something like:

```text
HCL:
  novel-concepts: ...
  active-prerequisites: ...
  interaction-density: ...
  abstraction: ...
  recall-distance: ...
  inferential-gap: ...
  terminology-load: ...
  scaffolding-support: ...
  estimated-risk: low / moderate / high / critical
```

Then revise the architecture accordingly.

# 29. RECURSIVE STABILISATION

For each major artifact:

1. produce candidate;
2. score it;
3. identify the weakest dimensions;
4. revise specifically against those weaknesses;
5. rescore;
6. compare vectors;
7. repeat until improvement stabilises.

Do not recurse forever.

A sensible stopping condition is:

- no serious conceptual defect remains;
- no load-bearing dimension remains materially weak;
- successive passes produce only small changes;
- further improvement begins trading one already-strong dimension against another rather than removing defects.

For the most important conceptual areas — especially:

- global coherence;
- TCR;
- relation-first ontology;
- stochastic actualisation;
- material/spiritual distinction;
- empirical description versus ontology;

perform adversarial passes as well.

Ask:

> What would an intelligent sceptical reader misunderstand here?

> What hidden assumption have we smuggled in?

> Are two categories being conflated?

> Is the prose stronger than the evidence?

> Does a local explanation contradict another part of the book?

> Is a term being used differently in different chapters?

# 56. DEFINITION OF SUCCESS

Success is **not**:

- preserving most of the old wording;
- increasing the page count;
- adding a stochastic chapter;
- adding TCR references;
- generating a pretty EPUB;
- obtaining impressive self-scores.

Success is that a thoughtful reader can finish the second edition with a coherent mental model resembling:

```text
God creates reality.

Created runtime physical order is real but is not self-grounding.

The deepest physical ontology proposed by RS is relational rather than object-first.

Possibility is constrained.

Actualisation is relationally constrained and may be fundamentally stochastic.

Persistence, identity and regularity emerge through structured relational continuity.

Transmit–Carry–Receive expresses how consequential relational difference persists and becomes consequential elsewhere.

Stable global behaviour does not require every underlying transition to be deterministic.

Mathematics can accurately describe stable regularity without thereby becoming the ontology itself.

Because material reality is relational from its foundation, physical order does not begin as a universe of metaphysically isolated objects which must somehow later acquire relationship.

This matters to global coherence.

Life, personhood, morality, consequence, corruption and restoration need not be bolted onto an intrinsically unrelated physical world.

Yet material, biological, personal and spiritual categories remain genuinely distinct.

Relationship across categories does not erase category.

Science strongly constrains what can responsibly be claimed about physical reality, but empirical description alone does not exhaust ontology.

Scripture provides the governing theological containment for CBO.

The resulting project is not an empirical proof of ultimate ontology.

It is an attempt to construct the most globally coherent account available while remaining honest about observation, mathematics, experiment, metaphysics, uncertainty and revelation.
```

If the completed book enables the reader to understand that argument without needing to read the research repository first, the second edition has succeeded.
