# 1. The current canonical identity distinction

The manuscript now correctly distinguishes two different questions.

## A. Sameness of kind/state

A physical thing may remain within the same **relevant relational equivalence class** through admissible change.

This concerns:

- relational equivalence;
- relevant invariants;
- projection;
- future distinguishability;
- provenance where consequential;
- functional/structural/state continuity.

This does **not automatically establish numerical identity**.

## B. Same continuing individual

Whether something is the **same individual**, rather than an equivalent replacement, additionally involves:

- continuity;
- lineage;
- history;
- provenance;
- some identity-bearing relation not yet fully solved by the research.

The project currently leaves open what physically carries individual continuity in the general case.

The manuscript must not casually slide back from A into B.

# 8. Final terminology consistency rules

After edits, the terminology should settle roughly as follows.

### Relational equivalence

Use for:

- sameness of state/kind relative to reachable distinctions;
- quotienting irrelevant differences;
- preservation across admissible transformations.

### Invariant

Use as:

- a certificate;
- carrier;
- marker;
- conserved feature

of an equivalence relation in some systems.

Do **not** define identity universally as “an invariant.”

### Individual continuity

Use for:

- the same continuing individual;
- continuity of lineage/history;
- not merely a replacement in the same equivalence class.

### Provenance

Use for:

- past distinctions embodied in present structure;
- consequential history;
- lineage-relevant information where future interaction can consume it.

### Personal identity

Keep categorically distinct from physical equivalence/lineage models.
