# Preface

A discipline can be exact in its own domain and still leave the deepest questions about reality open. Physics is the clearest case. It predicts with astonishing precision, and nothing in this book disputes that. But a theory can predict well without telling us why its entities are compatible with one another at all, why the same grammar of interaction holds everywhere we look, why the world is mathematically describable, or how living things and persons belong to the same order as stones. These are not questions physics has failed at. They are questions its success leaves open.

This book asks them together. Its question is not whether science works. It is what kind of created reality can make everything we know, physical, living, personal, moral and revealed, belong coherently to one world.

Its answer is that created physical reality is **relational** from its foundation. Things are not first made as sealed objects and then connected. They are constituted within an order of relation that God made and upholds, which the project calls the **Relational Substrate**. Matter is fully real but not foundational. *That* matter derives from a foundation that is not itself material is held as a theological premise; *how* materiality arises within such an order is the book's central question. Two further proposals give the answer shape: what actually happens is drawn, with real chance, from what created order makes possible and favours; and a difference anywhere becomes consequential elsewhere through one recurring grammar, **transmit, carry, receive**.

Scripture governs the whole. The biblical distinctions between Creator and creature, dust and breath, body and person, corruption and restoration set boundaries the ontology must respect.

## How the argument is judged

The standard is **global coherence**: a proposal about what exists must hold together across every domain it touches, without changing the meaning of its terms, without unexplained interfaces, and without breaking one domain to fix another. The standard runs both ways. The book must not contradict observation, measurement or successful mathematics. Equally, the picture usually carried along with physics, independent objects in a container of space, is itself a proposal, tested the same way.

The book labels its claims: Scriptural or theological constraint; empirical observation; mathematical or physical constraint; ontological inference; Relational Substrate (RS) working hypothesis; mechanism candidate; open question; rejected. A **model result** comes from the project's computer models; it can show an idea is possible and workable, not that nature works that way. A status table at the end of this edition gathers the main claims.

No measurement could display the deepest structure of created reality directly, so the warrant offered is that of a global coherence argument, not of experiment. The book states its hypothesis clearly enough to be wrong, says why it is believed, what supports it and what would count against it, and follows its consequences.

## About this edition

This edition is for readers comfortable with structured argument, such as students of philosophy, religious studies or physics at about A-level. Technical terms are defined at first use. One scene recurs as a reference point: seeing a stone on a path in morning light.

Earlier editions relied on specific mechanisms that later work did not support, including an "ontological stack", a "transformation tier" and a knotted field structure called the vorton presented as settled. This edition does not rely on them. The vorton and related structures remain **mechanism candidates**, named and not relied on.

The research behind the book is the work of one author, carried out with extensive computational and AI-assisted exploration, and its records are public. A free electronic edition is available at <https://relational-substrate.blogspot.com/>, with the research repository at <https://github.com/gellsmore-svg/Relational-Substrate>.
