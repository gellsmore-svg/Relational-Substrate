# Chapter 4: What Can Be and What Comes to Be

Water freezes into one characteristic lattice, and every snowflake, however individual, is six-sided. The possible is wide and yet firmly bounded. If physical things are configurations within relation, what bounds the configurations created order can bear, and how does a particular one become actual?

## T0: whatever conditions actualisation

The possible is structured before anything happens: conservation laws, symmetries and quantised energy levels bound what can occur. The project uses the label **T0** for *whatever conditions actualisation*: the constraints, potential or relational organisation according to which an actual state can come to be at all. That minimal commitment, that something is logically prior to any happening and bounds it, the book holds with confidence. **Status:** ontological inference.

What *kind* of thing T0 is remains **open**. The book's working reading is **constrained possibility**: the shape of what created order can bear, meaning which configurations and transitions can occur and how they are weighted. Live alternatives include a created potential order, a generative architecture of constraint, or the relational state of the substrate prior to a given actualisation. "Possibility space" is a way of speaking, not a realm or store.

## Admissibility and tendency

A rule may **forbid** a transition, making it impossible, or **favour** one, weighting it while leaving every option open. The project's **constraint laboratory** made this exact. In the smallest relational worlds, a few points between which links form and dissolve, it enumerated every rule of these kinds and every combination of up to three, and computed exactly, by calculation rather than simulation, each rule set's effect on long-run behaviour.

The result was clean: **only forbidding rules changed which patterns could be reached at all.** Only they created dead ends, altered the basic rhythm of movement or divided the space of possibilities; favouring rules shifted occupancy, sometimes greatly, but left every reachable pattern reachable. Later work located the difference more finely: in aggregate statistics a ban behaves like the limit of ever-stronger disfavouring, but only a ban changes the *structure of possible histories*, making some changes one-way and some states recoverable only by first undoing another relation. A persisting absence is therefore either **rate-limited** (return possible but unlikely) or **gate-limited** (return impossible until something else changes). These are **model results**, not discoveries about nature. They fix two terms:

- **Admissibility**: whether a transition is possible at all.
- **Tendency**: how likely an admissible transition is relative to others.

Tendency is not a probability anyone assigns; it names how created order is disposed among admissible options. The book proposes that created order has both. **Status:** mathematical result in models; RS working hypothesis for created order. *Admissible* means physically possible within created order, never morally permitted (Chapter 13).

## Constraint as generative

Constraint, in all its forms, makes things possible. A river is a river because of its banks; without them the water becomes a marsh. Coupling between transitions and relations among more than two things also generate structure where nothing new is forbidden (Chapter 9). Scripture presents creation as division and bounding:

> `Thou hast set a bound that they may not pass over; that they turn not again to cover the earth.`
> *Psalm 104:9*

> `He hath also stablished them for ever and ever: he hath made a decree which shall not pass.`
> *Psalm 148:6*

## T1, reachability and two kinds of constraint

**Actualisation** is the coming-to-be of one configuration from among the possible; **T1** is actual order, the configurations and transitions that have actually come to be. T1 is not a bare snapshot: it carries its history (Chapter 6). So the T0–T1 relation is not a one-way pipeline. Distinguish **total admissibility**, everything created order could ever bear, from what is **currently reachable** given the present state and its history. A stone that has cracked presents a fracture path that was not there before, changing how later forces act on it; a germinated seed can no longer stay dormant. Actual history changes what is reachable without changing whatever T0 ultimately is. **Status:** RS working hypothesis, with model support.

Constraints differ in level. **Explicitly intrinsic** constraints belong to the primitive configuration of created order, the conditions under which anything can happen; total admissibility rests on them. **Implicitly intrinsic** constraints arise once a configuration is actual and follow from it necessarily: once a crack exists, it necessarily affects the conditions of subsequent fracture, perhaps favouring propagation along it, without determining whether or exactly how the stone breaks. **Status:** working definitions. The point that matters next: **a configuration that arose by chance can carry necessary constraints.**

## Opportunity

Possibility, opportunity, admissibility and actualisation are four different things. **Working definition:** an **opportunity** is a distinguishable occasion at which some admissible actualisation is actually available. An opportunity can pass unused, like the seed that could have germinated this spring and did not. How such an unused occasion is to be individuated is **open**.

## Chess without players

The rules of chess are admissibility; commonly preferred moves are something like tendency; the game played tonight is actualisation. The analogy captures that possibility is structured but not determining, and that the actual is drawn from the admissible. It fails at one point, and the failure is its most important lesson: chess has *players* who choose. Created physical order has none. Nothing within it selects a transition or wants anything. An analogy pressed to supply a player turns the substrate into a mind. Chapter 5 asks how one transition becomes actual without a chooser.

## Purpose and non-agentic runtime

> `While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.`
> *Genesis 8:22*

Scripture calls the order of the heavens *ordinances* (Jeremiah 31:35-36) and binds God's faithfulness to it. Creation is purposed through and through. The question is where purpose sits. The book's theological inference:

1. **Purpose shapes what is possible**: God's intention is expressed in the shape of constrained possibility.
2. **Possibility shapes what can be actualised.**
3. **Actualisation proceeds without the substrate intending anything.**

This protects purpose (the world's possibilities were given with intent) and creatureliness (physical order is lawful in its own right, neither puppet nor mind in disguise). The book calls this **non-agentic runtime**: purposed without being purposeful in itself. It avoids occasionalism, on which created things have no real operations, and deism, on which God withdrew, in favour of the mainstream Christian position of lawful created operation under continual upholding (Colossians 1:17; Hebrews 1:3).

One caution, developed in Chapter 15: God is not one more cause among the world's causes. The primitive order is fixed for every creature operating within it; it is not fixed for its Creator. The book describes the ordinary operation of creation, and makes no claim about the manner of God's action in miracles, providence or redemption.

## What this chapter establishes

- T0, whatever conditions actualisation, is a confident minimal commitment; its nature is open.
- Admissibility and tendency are distinct (model result; RS hypothesis for created order); only forbidding changes reachability.
- Actual history alters what is currently reachable; explicit and implicit constraints differ in level; chance configurations can carry necessary constraints.
- Non-agentic runtime: purpose belongs to the Creator, the substrate intends nothing, and God is not confined by the primitive order.
