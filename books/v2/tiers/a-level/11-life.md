# Chapter 11: Life

Beneath the stone lie a woodlouse, a root, a fungal thread and a germinating seed: things that feed, grow, respond, repair and reproduce. Some pictures of the world are confident with inert matter and hesitant once life appears. If the relational account is right, life should need neither a separate explanatory afterthought nor flattening into merely complicated chemistry.

## Life is given

> `And God said, Let the earth bring forth the living creature after his kind, cattle, and creeping thing, and beast of the earth after his kind: and it was so.`
> *Genesis 1:24*

> `Thou takest away their breath, they die, and return to their dust. Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created: and thou renewest the face of the earth.`
> *Psalm 104:29-30*

Living things are real, active and their own: the earth *brings forth*, and they reproduce after their kind. Yet life is never self-grounding; it is given, upheld and withdrawn by God (Job 33:4; Acts 17:25). The account that follows describes how life *operates* within created order, not where it ultimately comes from.

## Sustained organisation

The stone persists largely by being undisturbed. A living thing persists the opposite way, continuously taking in matter and energy, transforming them and expelling waste, re-establishing a pattern through material flow like a flame but far more intricate. **Life is sustained organisation**: persistence actively maintained, under many layers of constraint, by the living thing's own work. In Chapter 6's terms, it continually re-actualises itself within its equivalence class and actively corrects departures; its continuing individuality depends on the unbroken history of the living process.

## Transmission within life

Much of that work is T-C-R. A surface molecule changes shape on detecting a hormone; the change is passed along a signalling cascade and received as a gene switched on or a movement begun. Cells signal one another; nerves and hormones carry differences between organs. A body is a vast composition of nested, parallel and sequential transmissions, coordinated so the whole responds as one. And reception becomes partly self-governed: a cell regulates its receptors, so the admissibility of reception, fixed by molecular structure in the eye's first step, is in living things partly governed by the organism itself.

## Templates and repair

Chapter 6 showed that preservation is not repair: recovery after damage requires surviving information. Life's defining solution is the **template**. DNA participates in shaping protein synthesis, gene activity, division, repair and specialisation; damaged DNA is often repaired from the complementary strand; reproduction copies the template. But DNA is not the only surviving information: the cell's current state, epigenetic marks, its spatial architecture, chemical gradients in developing tissue and its surroundings all carry constraints that guide repair and development.

The first edition called DNA a **constraint map** rather than a program, and the description serves. DNA does not issue commands as software does; it participates in a larger web of constraint (chemistry, membranes, environment, history), and it functions only within the living whole that reads it.

## Chance within life

Molecular chance is pervasive: erratic collisions, bursty gene expression, copying errors. Genetically identical cells in identical conditions behave differently. Yet development is astonishingly reliable, through feedback, redundancy and buffering that correct fluctuations. Development is a striking case of **constrained chance producing reliable form**, the pattern of Chapters 5 and 9 in its most impressive form.

Chance also contributes to variation. "After his kind" speaks of patterned continuity, not rigid sameness. Total rigidity would be lifeless repetition; sheer randomness would mean nothing was inherited. The first edition's term **bounded generativity** is kept: variation belongs within ordered continuity, inside the admissible range. How wide that range is, and how far living kinds have changed historically, are questions on which Christians sharing this book's commitments disagree, and the book does not settle them.

## Higher-order wholes

A tissue is more than a crowd of cells, not because a substance is added but because organisation makes a real difference and explains things at its own level. Following Chapter 9, what matters is coordination aligned with consequence. A collection becomes a **higher-order whole** when its parts are coupled so that they no longer behave independently, disturbances are redistributed through the whole, and the whole has properties, a regulated temperature or a restored shape, that no part has (compare Chapter 8's tests). The body abounds in such coupling: cardiac, respiratory, neural, sleep and hormonal rhythms entrain one another; negative feedback holds temperature, glucose and oxygen within narrow ranges; positive feedback drives threshold processes such as clotting and birth. The organism is less a thing that has a pattern than a pattern that keeps itself in being.

## Direction and purpose

Living things are directed: seeds toward plants, embryos through ordered stages, wounds toward healing. Describing them without saying what parts are *for* is very difficult. The book takes this directedness to be real, neither imposed from outside at every moment nor illusory, but built into the form the organism has been given. This fits Chapter 4: divine purpose shapes what is possible, and the substrate intends nothing. The organism is not a little mind planning its development; its form unfolds, through constrained chance and coordinated relation, toward the end it is shaped for.

> `For thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother's womb. I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made.`
> *Psalm 139:13-14*

God's directing of formation does not compete with the process of development; it works through it.

## What life is not

Life is **not a vital substance or force** added to matter; living things are built from the same created order as stones, and their life is that order's most demanding coordination. Nor is life **nothing but chemistry**: organisation is real and explanatory, the living whole is a unity rather than a heap, and Scripture insists life is given and sustained by God. The breath God sends and withdraws (Psalm 104:29-30) is not one more chemical in the cell.

That leaves living things where Genesis places them, real, fruitful, ordered after their kinds and dependent on their Creator, and brings the book to the threshold Genesis marks with a third use of *bara*.

## What this chapter establishes

- Life is given by God; the account describes its operation, not its ultimate source.
- Life is sustained organisation, using transmission and templates (DNA as constraint map within a larger web).
- Constrained chance yields reliable development; variation is bounded and its historical range left open.
- Higher-order wholes arise through aligned coupling; living directedness is real and built into given form.
