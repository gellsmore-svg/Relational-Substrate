# Chapter 14: Corruption

An account of reality that speaks only of order soon becomes unbelievable. The stone is beautiful, but the path has litter, the river may be poisoned, and the one who sees may be grieving or lying. The world is good, and it is also deeply wrong.

## Real, but not ultimate

> `Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned.`
> *Romans 5:12*

Scripture excludes two errors: disorder as ultimate, making goodness illusory, and disorder as superficial, a malfunction a serious account may ignore. Since Augustine, Christian thought has held that corruption is **parasitic**: a distortion of the good, with no being of its own, existing only by deforming what God made. Evil cannot create; it can only spoil. Hence the vocabulary of *distortion*, *misalignment* and *rupture*, which presuppose an order.

## The subjection of creation

> `And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life; Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee... for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.`
> *Genesis 3:17-19*

Three things must be kept apart.

**What the text says.** The ground is cursed because of human disobedience; work becomes toil; the man will return to dust. Creation "was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who hath subjected the same in hope" (Romans 8:20). The subjection is God's act. **Status:** scriptural constraint.

**The author's interpretation.** The curse is a deliberate divine realignment of the created runtime order to the consequences of spiritual disobedience, so that creation goes on telling the truth: a world in which the breach with God had no visible consequence would teach falsely. Creation remains an instructor after the Fall, and what it now teaches includes decay, toil and loss. The thesis concerns what changed in creation, not the manner of God's action (Chapter 4). **Status:** theological interpretation.

**The mechanism, which is open.** The author's thesis is that God realigned the primitives themselves, the explicitly intrinsic constraints of Chapter 4, fixed for creatures but not for their Creator. Two alternatives remain in view: God changed parameters within an otherwise unchanged primitive order; or he actualised a new state under the original constraints, so that the new conditions follow as implicitly intrinsic constraints. These are different claims explaining different things, and Genesis describes none. **Status:** open question (research fork).

The author also holds that the realignment introduced **animal mortality**, which was not part of creation's original conditions. The author connects this with Genesis 3:21: "Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them." That the skins mark the first death, cover shame and anticipate sacrifice is the author's reading; the text names no lamb, rite or first death. And the position meets a real difficulty: **the fossil record shows animal death in strata conventionally dated long before humans.** The book's bidirectional standard (Chapter 2) does not permit that interface to be ignored. By the author's decision, the book states the position, names the conflict with conventional chronology as an **open problem**, and does not resolve it.

On every reading of the mechanism, the subjection is God's act, and God's acts are not corrupt. The futility imposed is a judgment, coherent, purposed and given "in hope". Corruption suffered in higher orders of creation does not make its primitive order incoherent. Nor does every suffering follow from the sufferer's sin: "Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents" (John 9:3). Creation's subjection is the condition of all, not a ledger of each.

## Corruption as persisting misalignment

Misalignment alone is not the whole of corruption: growing creatures depart from mature form and learners err. A wrong act is wrong in itself, judged by the moral order of Chapter 13, not by duration: a single, swiftly repented lie was still a lie. What persistence explains is **entrenchment**: unrestored misalignment hardens into habit, narrows what a person can see and choose, and reaches into others. Corruption in the full sense includes both the wrong and its entrenchment.

> `But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.`
> *Hebrews 3:13*

> `Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and when sin is finished, it bringeth forth death.`
> *James 1:15*

Chapter 6 helps in two of Chapter 1's senses. As **structural analogy**: the past persisting in present configuration, narrowing what is next, has the same form in the bent paper clip and in a habit of deceit. As **causal connection**: sin belongs to persons in relation to God and is no configuration of the substrate, but its embodied consequences are real configurations, carried in dispositions, body and relations. Corruption works with the grain of a relational world against the purpose for which it was made.

## Corruption in transmission

T-C-R gives a second lens, used where it clarifies. A lie transmits a false distinction; slander a false distinction about a person; a broken promise breaks a carriage relied upon; gossip distorts in carriage. A believed lie changes what someone does, which changes others, until effects pass into decisions and circumstances never traced back to it. That history is real, carried in all the changed relations, the only record created order keeps; nothing guarantees a creature can recover it. **Consequence persists where provenance is lost.** Corruption can also be a **failure of reception**: "I spake unto you, rising up early and speaking, but ye heard not" (Jeremiah 7:13). These lenses help us see patterns Scripture already describes; they do not explain sin, and a single technical vocabulary for every sin would trivialise it.

## Five patterns

The first edition's five patterns of unrestored misalignment remain useful at every scale. **Degradation**: the thinning of what should hold, where sustained input is not arriving. **Fragmentation**: a whole broken into parts no longer in real relation, where coupling has failed. **Inversion**: what should serve comes to rule. **Counterfeit**: the form of a good kept while its substance is betrayed, the deception compounding the loss. **Parasitism**: sustenance drawn from a good it cannot generate, weakening its host. They combine, and none is creative; all depend on an order still functioning around them.

## Suffering, sin and limitation

Scripture voices suffering without denying creation's goodness ("My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?", Psalm 22:1) and insists humans are agents of corruption, not only victims ("All we like sheep have gone astray", Isaiah 53:6). Corruption is personal and culpable. The relational account shows how wrongdoing reaches others and the land; it cannot make sin less personal.

**Limitation is not corruption.** Finitude (sleep, partial knowledge, location, dependence) is good creaturehood, which Genesis calls very good. If every limit were evil, salvation would mean ceasing to be a creature; if corruption were collapsed into limitation, death, cruelty and injustice would be softened into mere finitude.

**Darkness, again.** The darkness of Genesis 1:2 is not evil. Moral darkness is not a created substance but *hiddenness turned to disorder*: the created capacity for concealment seized for evasion and deceit (John 3:19).

**Remembrance.** What holds a people aligned over time? Scripture insists on remembrance: Passover, memorial stones, feasts, the retold exodus, the Lord's Supper (Luke 22:19). In the book's terms, remembrance is how a people keeps carrying what was given, so that what God did is received again in each generation. Forgetting is an ordinary road into drift.

## What this chapter establishes

- Corruption is real and parasitic; it cannot create.
- The subjection is God's act; the realignment reading is the author's interpretation; its mechanism is an open research fork; post-Fall animal mortality is the author's interpretation, with the fossil-record conflict named and unresolved.
- Wrongness is intrinsic; entrenchment explains spread; corruption runs through transmission and failed reception.
- Limitation is good creaturehood; remembrance renews alignment.
