# Chapter 2: How We Come to Know What Is There

Physics knows how gravity *behaves* with extraordinary precision, from a falling stone to the relativistic corrections in satellite navigation. What gravity *is*, a force, the curvature of a substantival spacetime, or a pattern of relations among events, remains disputed among people who accept every measurement, and how gravity fits with quantum theory is unknown. That gap between describing behaviour and knowing what something is shapes everything in this book. This chapter assembles the tools the rest of the book uses, in order: the layers of knowing; what behaviour can and cannot reveal; the rule against elastic explanation; coherence as the standard of judgment; comparison without numbers; and a bounded account of logic, inference and proof. Each later chapter draws on them, so it is worth returning here when one is invoked.

## Six layers of knowing

1. **Observation**: the stone falls.
2. **Measurement**: numbers by a defined procedure (mass, time).
3. **Mathematical relation**: distance fallen grows with the square of the time.
4. **Physical model**: a force between masses, or a geodesic in curved spacetime.
5. **Ontological interpretation**: what must actually be there for the behaviour to occur.
6. **Worldview**: the background in which that interpretation feels natural.

Each layer depends on those before it, but is not guaranteed by them. Newtonian gravitation predicted superbly for two centuries before general relativity replaced its picture of instantaneous action across empty space, while preserving most of its predictions. Quantum theory sharpens the point: one formalism, confirmed with great precision, is read as fundamentally indeterministic on some interpretations and as deterministic (with hidden variables or branching worlds) on others, all agreeing on every measurement so far. **A successful description can be joined to more than one ontology.**

## Behaviour and intrinsic nature

A programmer can call a *private function* without reading its code, and by systematic testing learn a great deal about it. But different code could produce the same outputs for every input tried. Knowledge of **consequential behaviour** is genuine; it is not, by itself, knowledge of **intrinsic ontology**. Mathematics offers a precise case: in 1992 Gordon, Webb and Wolpert constructed two differently shaped drums that sound exactly the same notes. The sound tells you much about the drum, and not everything.

Observability and understanding answer different questions. A question may stand in four **respects of accessibility**:

1. **Mechanistic explanation**: we can account for how specified processes produce an outcome.
2. **Relational understanding without a complete mechanism**: we genuinely understand what trust is, or why a promise binds, without an exhaustive account of how it operates.
3. **Present investigative limits**: beyond current instruments or methods, but possibly accessible later.
4. **Possible limits of exhaustive creaturely comprehension**: never fully comprehended by a creature, though partial understanding remains possible.

These are respects in which questions are approached, not kinds of reality. Observability cuts across them: atoms were inferred long before they were imaged, and quantum theory limits what can be learned from a single unknown system, yet unobservability does not forbid inference from consequences. Two errors are avoided: declaring something permanently inaccessible without reason, and assuming that every underlying reality will eventually be observed as one more object. The governing principle: **a successful description of observable consequences does not, by itself, establish the ultimate ontological nature of their cause.**

## Every account starts somewhere

Every ontology contains assumptions beyond measurement, whether materialist, religious or this book's. **Methodological naturalism**, the working rule of explaining natural phenomena by natural causes, is sound practice. **Metaphysical naturalism**, the claim that nothing exists except the natural world, does not follow from it; a metal detector cannot discover that there is no wood. The same discipline binds theology: quoting Scripture does not make an argument rigorous if the step from text to conclusion is unexplained.

Assumptions fail characteristically through **metaphysical elasticity**: assigning additional, insufficiently justified properties to an explanatory entity whenever needed to preserve an interpretation, without acknowledging the added assumptions or the evidence they would require. The author's informal name is the *Harry Potter problem*: in a world of spells, an unseen cause can be produced for anything. It occurs when a successful mathematical description is taken, without separate argument, as evidence that its entities are fundamental substances, and when a word is widened to rescue a thesis: if "matter" covers whatever physics finds, materialism cannot be wrong and has stopped saying anything. It could occur in this book. The rule binds the Relational Substrate as strictly as its rivals; hence fixed commitments, stated defeaters (Chapter 9) and priced additions.

## Coherence: local, regional, global

Without direct measurement of ontology, accounts are judged by **coherence**: not mere consistency, but connection with real distinctions preserved.

- **Local coherence**: a model explains a bounded phenomenon.
- **Regional coherence**: neighbouring domains integrate (chemistry with atomic physics, molecular biology with chemistry).
- **Global coherence**: the whole account remains mutually compatible: physics, life, consciousness and personhood, morality, history and, for a Christian account, Scripture.

Global coherence adds tests beyond non-contradiction:

- **real connection** between domains;
- **valid interfaces** where one kind of reality meets another;
- **meaning preserved** through explanatory levels;
- **stability under repair**;
- the **round trip**, in which a claim taken through other domains and back returns substantially unchanged;
- **accountable kinds of knowing**, so that observation, mathematics, philosophical inference, personal encounter and revelation each contribute without silently overruling the others.
 Throughout, real distinctions must survive: Creator and creature, analogy and identity, probability and arbitrariness, a person and a configuration. Connection is not reduction.

The book's central methodological thesis: **local explanatory success is not sufficient evidence that an ontology is true if that ontology produces contradiction elsewhere.** Chapter 10 argues for it.

**Coherence runs both ways.** The RS proposal is finished if it contradicts robust observation. But the conventional metaphysics of physics, space as container, independent objects, laws as rules things obey, belongs to layers 4 to 6. It gains no evidential weight from familiarity or consensus, and it is held to the same standard.

## Comparing without numbers

Three judgments are distinct: **logical possibility** (no identified contradiction), **qualitative plausibility** (more or less plausible given observations, assumptions and alternatives), and **quantitative probability** (a number under a specified model). The absence of the third does not invalidate the second. Passing a hand along an electric fence at one centimetre is more likely to end in contact than at one metre; other factors bear on both passes alike, so the ordering holds across every reasonable variation of them. The mathematics of comparative probability shows that "more probable than" can be reasoned about rigorously without numbers. Comparisons are therefore **robust** (surviving all reasonable variation), **conditional** (holding under stated assumptions) or **unavailable**. An account is not improbable merely for being complicated, and global coherence concerns how naturally an account explains observed relationships without unnecessary independent assumptions.

## Logic, inference and proof

Human understanding normally assembles the most coherent account available from incomplete evidence, as a doctor diagnoses or a historian reconstructs. The book needs further distinctions here.

**Logic** is the necessary structure of consistency, implication, exclusion and possibility; **reasoning** is its application. The complete coherence of all that is, perfectly comprehended and perfectly applicable, the author calls **true logic**; it belongs inseparably to God's nature, and no creature possesses it exhaustively. **Status:** theological premise. Finite minds move towards it, and the author's definition is exact: "Inferential logic is the recursive refinement of partial understanding towards true logic through the discovery and application of relational coherence within the boundaries of created runtime order." **Status:** working definition. *Created runtime order*, the operating created world, is defined in Chapter 3. *Partial* means genuine but incomplete; *recursive* means a later finding can revise a conclusion, its assumptions and even the question. Revising an account of coherence is never revising the coherence of what God has made.

**Proof**, in the book's bounded sense, is the achievement of sufficient warrant, under stated premises, scope and constraints, for an inferred relation to be relied on in further reasoning: to become a **coherence factor**. Formal proof is conclusive relative to its axioms and rules; experimental, philosophical and theological warrant differ in kind. A coherence factor keeps its scope, source, uncertainties and dependencies visible so it can be revised. The aim of inquiry is **inferential precision**, not possession of the whole truth.

## What observation decides

**Coherence is not truth**: a consistent flat-Earth story is still false. Observation rules out accounts that contradict it, reveals regularities any account must respect, and tests specific proposals; several of the project's have failed, and the failures are recorded. It narrows the field without displaying the deepest structure of reality. The book's warrant is therefore a global coherence argument, held with **disciplined confidence**: confidence must not outrun warrant, and justified understanding should not be discounted for falling short of exhaustive knowledge.

## What this chapter establishes

- Description underdetermines ontology; behavioural knowledge is genuine but not knowledge of intrinsic nature.
- Metaphysical elasticity is excluded for every account, including RS.
- Global coherence, tested both ways, is the standard; plausibility can be compared rigorously without numbers.
- Proof is bounded warrant yielding a coherence factor; coherence is not truth, and disciplined confidence is the posture.
