# Chapter 9: Regularity, Coordination, and the Place of Mathematics

A bicycle tyre's steady pressure is the combined effect of countless erratic molecular impacts, and it obeys a one-line law. If created order is constrained-stochastic at depth, how is the world so reliably regular, and what is the mathematics that describes it so well?

Four problems are distinct. (1) Probabilistically described events can combine into **stable aggregates** without settling whether the underlying processes are chancy. (2) How events are **related** can change the behaviour of the whole while each event's probability is unchanged. (3) A simple law can describe a whole at one scale **without describing what lies beneath**. (4) Such a description may **omit lower-level distinctions** irrelevant to its question but relevant to others.

## Aggregation

The **law of large numbers** guarantees that ten million coin flips almost certainly yield heads within a tenth of a per cent of half; versions hold for dependent events whose dependence fades sufficiently. Physics uses such statistical regularities widely: the gas laws, Brownian motion (traced by Einstein in 1905 to irregular molecular impacts), radioactive half-lives. These establish **reliable aggregate behaviour**, not by themselves a fundamentally stochastic microscopic ontology: classical statistical mechanics may rest on deterministic dynamics, while quantum decay is predicted probabilistically and its interpretation remains contested.

Physicists call large-scale laws **effective**: precise at their scale without describing what lies beneath. **Effective determinism** is behaviour regular and predictable to observable precision, whatever the underlying process: a fact about regularity, not a metaphysical claim. **Mathematical determinism** is a property of equations, where the state at one time fixes all later states; a deterministic large-scale description, such as the fluid equations, does not decide whether underlying processes are deterministic or ontologically stochastic. And an **attractor**, precisely, is a state or set toward which a system tends from many starting points and returns when slightly disturbed; a reliable outcome is not by itself evidence of one (the Chapter 5 calculator succeeded by conservation, not attraction).

## Same odds, different behaviour

Two rooms of a hundred people toss fair coins repeatedly. In one, tosses are independent; in the other, people are paired and each pair reports opposite results. Every individual's chance of heads is one half in both rooms, so the **marginal** probabilities are identical. But the **joint process** differs: the first room's total wanders, the second's is exactly fifty every time.

```text
the same individual odds
  are not the same joint process,
  which is not the same pattern of co-variation,
  which is not the same behaviour of the whole
```

The book calls such relations among chance events **coordination**. It is not a controller above the events; it is a relation among them. And it is not what stabilises a mean; aggregation does that. Coordination governs everything else: the size and shape of fluctuations, whether a quantity is held exactly, the path of the whole, its response to disturbance, and which futures remain open. Exploratory work on rings of locally coupled chance units, not yet a formal record, found that correlation as such was not what mattered. A strong dependence can be irrelevant to a property, and a modest one decisive if aligned with it. Stability also comes in layers: a mean can be stable while fluctuations are not, and some quantities can be exactly conserved while others vary.

None of this mathematics belongs to a relation-first ontology. What the relational proposal changes is the reading: on an object-first reading, dependence structure is an extra fact about how complete things happen to be related; on the relational reading, it belongs to *what the physical whole is*. **Status:** RS working hypothesis (dependence structure is constitutive). The book does not claim that local relations alone account for the regularity of created order.

## What mathematics is doing

Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics" invites the question. The Navier–Stokes equations treat a fluid as a smooth continuum, though it consists of molecules in chaotic motion; they are approximately derivable from that statistical behaviour, fail in very thin gases, and whether they always have smooth solutions is a celebrated open problem. Two lessons: deterministic mathematics at one scale does not imply determinism at every scale, and a smooth field description need not be the ontology of what it describes. The project draws a research question rather than a result: *what kinds of relational constraint and coordination preserve the invariants, correlations and continuity needed for stable effective laws to emerge?*

**Hidden structure.** In the simplest models, adding a genuinely three-way relation and observing only two-way links makes the visible process *appear* to have memory, though nothing in the rules records the past: hidden present structure masquerades as history. Hence **mathematical equivalence under one description does not settle sameness**, and **a mathematical structure that describes behaviour is not, by that fact, what reality fundamentally is.** Mathematics works because created order is structured by constraint, preserved by equivalence and invariance, and regular through coordination, exactly what mathematics describes best.

Creation is intelligible, "understood by the things that are made" (Romans 1:20). Scripture does not supply the equations; it explains why a world in which they hold, and in which minds find them, is what a creation made through the Word should be.

## Measurement, model and interpretation

Where measurement and prediction stand, the relational reading must agree. What a field is, whether spacetime is a substance, whether quantum chance is fundamental, and what a particle is between measurements are interpretive and open. In quantum field theory five things are distinct: the formalism; the measurable phenomena it predicts; interpretations of its states and operators; the claim that fields are fundamental; and the claim that whatever is fundamental is material. Predictive success bears directly on the second, not by itself on the others. The same formalism of quantised excitations describes crystal phonons, where no new material entity is inferred. Nothing in the formalism proves the substrate either; what is required is compatibility.

## Gravity and time in the simplest models

The Gravity/Time programme obtained exact results on systems of at most seven points, adopted at that scope. A favouring rule defines a **relational potential**, a weighted count of a link pattern; "attraction" toward a structure is then participation in the favoured pattern, with no centre and no force across. **Status:** mechanism candidate, model-scoped. **Gathering**, a lasting preference for relation to a reference structure, is distinct from **succession**: as systems grew from six to seven points the directed handover kept its direction in every case, while the only accompanying gathering fell to the level of the null comparison. Apparent gathering must survive adjustment for density, and a relational reference is not a neutral frame.

A negative result: the rules do **not** tie activity rates to the potential, so a relational account of gravitational time dilation would need a principle they lack. Every model claim must name its clock, since step counts and continuous time can give opposite signs. None of this is gravity or a theory of time.

## What would count against these proposals

- **Contradicting precise measurement.** Any specific mechanism contradicting measurement fails. One has: a 2026 model predicted the direction of a metal-fatigue order effect but its magnitude many times too small, and was recorded as a failure.
- **Intrinsic properties at the base.** Evidence that fundamental entities have fully interaction-independent properties would seriously weaken relation-first.
- **Entanglement and mediation.** Bell-violating correlations, experimentally confirmed, rule out a class of local explanations; they cannot signal, and several interpretations (relational ones included) account for them. They remain the hardest test for mediated relation, unsolved here.
- **Gravity's clocks.** No relational account of time dilation exists in the project, and the models show one needs a new principle.
- **Stochasticity is hard to test.** Standard interpretations agree on all measurements (collapse theories, testable in principle, aside), which protects the hypothesis and limits the confidence it warrants.
- **Matter's emergence.** B's mechanism would be weakened by persistent failure to obtain individuated organisation from simpler relations; the comparative argument by A or C explaining interoperability as economically. The premise of non-material origin rests on other grounds. The debt is unpaid, not defaulted.
- **The models do not single out RS.** Ordinary computer science and probability explain every model result, and a hoped-for pattern of "dissolution leading to new organisation" did not appear.

The value of the proposals lies in coherence: whether a relation-first account helps the whole of what we know hang together better than its alternatives. Chapter 10 makes that case.

## What this chapter establishes

- Aggregation stabilises means, but statistical regularity does not establish ontic stochasticity.
- Coordination, distinct from correlation, shapes fluctuations, exactness and accessible futures; its constitutive reading is an RS hypothesis.
- Mathematics describes regularity without being the reality; hidden structure can mimic history.
- Model results on gravity and time are scoped and partly negative; explicit defeaters and pressure points are stated.
