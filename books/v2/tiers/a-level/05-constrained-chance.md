# Chapter 5: Constrained Chance

A single atom of carbon-14 has, on the standard understanding of quantum physics, no internal clock: it may decay in the next second or in fifty thousand years, and not for lack of information. (Some interpretations dispute this.) Yet half of a large sample decays in about 5,730 years, precisely enough to date an ancient campfire. Open in the particular, exact in the aggregate: this pairing is among the most important facts in the book.

## The meaning of "stochastic"

"Random" can mean **lawless** (anything could happen), **unpredictable to us** (a cause we could not foresee), or **probabilistic** (outcomes open, frequencies fixed). The book uses **stochastic** for the third: a process whose outcomes are governed by probabilities over defined possibilities. A fair die is stochastic, not lawless; it never shows seven. Stochastic is also distinct from **chaotic**: a chaotic system is fully deterministic but so sensitive to initial conditions that it is practically unpredictable.

Calling a process stochastic says how it is *described*, not why. The probabilities may be **epistemic**, expressing ignorance of hidden details in a deterministic process, or **ontic**, expressing genuine openness in the world. The same description fits both.

## Chance in physics

Repeated quantum measurements yield stable, exact frequencies (observation), and quantum theory gives its predictions as probabilities (mathematical constraint). Whether nature is *fundamentally* indeterministic is a matter of interpretation: some readings take the probabilities as ontic, others as epistemic, all agreeing on every measurement so far. **The probabilities are established; fundamental indeterminism is not.**

Jacob Barandes has argued that any quantum system can be described as an *indivisible* stochastic process among ordinary configurations, one whose transition probabilities from an earlier to a later time need not compose through intermediate times, which take on a role only when something, typically the environment, registers the system's state. This shows a stochastic reading of quantum theory can be worked out in detail. It does not show the world is chancy, since it reproduces standard predictions, and it supplies no probability for complete histories. **Status:** an illuminating mathematical connection, not adopted as support.

## The proposal: constrained stochastic actualisation

Chapter 4 left the question of how one admissible transition becomes actual without a chooser. The book proposes that **actualisation is fundamentally stochastic** in the ontic sense: which currently reachable transition occurs is *not uniquely determined* by the preceding created configuration together with its governing relations; tendency weights outcomes without selecting one in advance. This openness is the book's claim, not part of the meaning of "stochastic".

The weight falls on **constrained**. *Unconstrained* stochasticity, every conceivable outcome equally open, would yield a world without stable form. *Constrained* stochasticity is chance operating within the shape of the possible: admissibility excludes whole regions, tendency weights what remains, and history alters what is reachable. Accordingly the book rejects **true randomness**, occurrence governed by no admissibility relation or pattern, as incompatible with a coherent created order.

Necessity is not **determinism**. **Working definition:** determinism holds where a complete preceding configuration, under its applicable governing relations, admits exactly one successor; and the governing relations are not properties hidden inside the state. Absolutely binding constraints can leave several outcomes open, as the rules of chess never select the move. Created order is **necessary in its constraints and open in its actualisations.**

**Status:** RS working hypothesis. It fits the probabilistic character of quantum theory without following from it. A demonstration that the probabilities are wholly epistemic would count against it. Its positive support is coherence: it explains how a non-agentic creation actualises without a hidden chooser, and it fits openness in the particular with exactness in the aggregate.

## An illustration: exact answers from chance

One of the project's models adds two numbers by building populations of positively and negatively oriented relations and letting them change at random under local rules (re-attachment, cancellation of opposite pairs, creation and annihilation of neutral pairs). Thousands of runs from one starting arrangement followed thousands of different paths, and every one reached the correct sum. **An exact outcome can coexist with great diversity of chance-driven paths.** The exactness came from rules conserving the total; chance supplied the variety. A fixed sequence would also be exact, and ordinary computer science explains the model fully. It illustrates how chance and exactness relate, not that nature computes this way.

Weakening the rules, by occasionally permitting a forbidden deletion, degraded accuracy smoothly and progressively. Stability is always stability **of something, against some disturbance, over some timescale**.

## Chance and providence

> `The lot is cast into the lap; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD.`
> *Proverbs 16:33*

The lot was the ancient paradigm of chance, used even for dividing the land. Scripture does not deny that it falls by chance as far as anything within the lap can tell; it says the whole disposing belongs to the Lord. "Time and chance happeneth to them all" (Ecclesiastes 9:11) is stated without alarm.

The book's theological inference: if created actualisation is constrained-stochastic, chance is a **created mode of actualisation**, not a rival power and not God's uncertainty. The openness is openness *with respect to created determination*; it is **not a claim that God lacks knowledge** of the actual outcome. How God's exhaustive knowledge and action relate to that openness is not a mechanism the book claims to explain, and the long Christian discussion of sovereignty and contingency is left where it stands.

Four ideas must be kept apart: **mathematical randomness** (a property of a description), **ontological stochasticity** (actualisation is open), **purposelessness** (nothing is for anything) and **atheism** (there is no God). None follows from the one before. Proverbs 16:33 accepts the first, perhaps the second, and denies the third and fourth together.

## What this chapter establishes

- "Stochastic" describes probabilistic governance; whether probabilities are epistemic or ontic is a separate question, unsettled in physics.
- RS hypothesis: actualisation is constrained-stochastic, not uniquely determined by prior created configuration; true randomness is rejected.
- Necessary constraint is not determinism, which is defined precisely.
- Chance is a created mode of actualisation; its openness concerns created determination, not divine knowledge.
