# Chapter 11: Putting Things Right

Real hope needs something real underneath it: a reason to believe that what is broken can be mended. If the world were finally chaos, hope would be pretending. If the world belongs to God, hope may be the most sensible thing there is.

## Only God can mend it

Chapter 6 showed something important. When something is truly destroyed, nothing in the world can bring it back by itself. Living things can repair a lot, but in the end every living thing dies.

So the healing of creation cannot come from creation itself. The Bible says it comes from God:

> `And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.`
> *Colossians 1:20*

The One who made all things is the One who puts all things right. That is not something the world does to itself. It is God's act. When Jesus rose from the dead, that was not a very unusual pattern in the physical world. It was an act of the Creator. The book does not try to explain miracles as hidden science.

## Hope for the whole world

The Bible's hope is not that souls escape from a ruined world. It is that God will make the world new:

> `And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away. And he that sat upon the throne said, Behold, I make all things new.`
> *Revelation 21:4-5*

Creation itself "shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption" (Romans 8:21). If people are made from the dust of a world built from relationships, then their healing and the world's healing belong together. It is one rescue, by one God, of one world.

## The same person, raised

The centre of Christian hope is the resurrection, and it is a hope about being the *same person*. After he rose, Jesus said:

> `Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see.`
> *Luke 24:39*

Remember the difference between the same kind and the same one (Chapter 6). A perfect copy of you would be the same kind, but it would not be *you*. So resurrection is not God making a copy. It is God raising the same person. How? The book does not know, and the Bible does not tell us. But it is clear why it must be God's work: nothing in the physical world could do it. God, who calls each of his people by name, does not lose track of who they are.

## Mended, and more

A restored life is not always just put back where it was. A friendship mended after a serious quarrel, or a faith that has come through hard doubts, can become deeper and more honest than before. The wound and the healing both stay part of the story.

That does **not** mean evil was needed, or that suffering is good in itself. God made a "very good" world with no evil in it, where Adam and Eve could truly choose (Genesis 2:16-17). Evil was never part of the plan. What God does is answer the evil that happened, and the answer cost him dearly:

> `For when we were yet without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly.`
> *Romans 5:6*

## A taste now

Between Jesus' resurrection and the renewal of everything, God gathers his people together. In local churches, people pray, worship, forgive, remember and serve. They do not bring in the new creation; only God does that. But they can give a real, if imperfect, taste of it now.

## What this chapter has shown

- Restoration is God's act, for the whole world.
- Resurrection is God raising the same person, not making a copy.
- A mended life can be deeper than before, but evil was never needed.
