# Chapter 4: What Can Be and What Comes to Be

Water, cooled enough, becomes ice. It never becomes glass or iron. Snowflakes differ endlessly, yet every one is six-sided. Something leaves room for huge variety and still draws firm limits.

The previous chapter proposed that things are configurations within a created order of relation. This chapter asks what bounds the configurations that order can bear, and how a particular one becomes actual.

## Possibility is not a blank cheque

What *could* happen is wider than what *does*. A coin could land heads or tails; it lands tails. But the possible is itself structured. A coin cannot land on a colour. An acorn cannot grow into a sunflower. Physics describes these bounds in many ways: conservation laws, symmetries, the fixed energy levels allowed in an atom. The bounds are part of what the world is.

The book is confident of a minimal claim: **something is prior to any particular happening and bounds it.** What kind of thing that is remains an **open question**. The book's working reading is **constrained possibility**: the shape of what created order can bear. "Possibility space" is a useful way of speaking, not a separate realm or store.

## Forbidding and favouring

A rule can shape what happens in two quite different ways:

- It can **forbid**, making something impossible: a locked door.
- It can **favour**, making something more or less likely while leaving every option open: a well-worn path.

The project's **constraint laboratory** tested every simple rule of both kinds, exactly, in tiny worlds of a few points where links form and dissolve.

The result was clean. **Only forbidding rules changed which patterns could be reached at all.** Favouring rules shifted how much time the system spent in each pattern, sometimes a lot, but never closed anything off.

These are **model results**, not discoveries about nature. They give two terms the book uses throughout:

- **Admissibility**: whether a transition is possible at all.
- **Tendency**: how likely an admissible transition is compared with the others.

Admissibility decides the shape of the possible. Tendency weights it. Tendency is not a probability anyone assigns; it names how created order is disposed among its options. The book proposes that created order has both.

One caution. In this book *admissible* means **physically possible**. It never means "morally allowed". Chapter 13 keeps those apart.

## Constraint makes things possible

We often think of limits as negative. But a river is a river because of its banks. Remove them and the water becomes a marsh, not a freer river. The rules that bound which crystals water can form are the reason ice exists at all.

Scripture describes creation in just these terms:

> `Thou hast set a bound that they may not pass over; that they turn not again to cover the earth.`
> *Psalm 104:9*

Light from darkness, sea from land, kind from kind: each division is a constraint, and each makes room for a richer world.

## From the possible to the actual

A space of possibilities does not contain a single actual stone. Something must happen. The book calls this **actualisation**: one of the possible becoming actual.

What is actual is not a bare snapshot. It carries its history, and that history changes what can happen next. So the book distinguishes:

- **Total possibility**: everything created order could ever bear.
- **What is currently reachable**: what can actually happen next, given the present state and its history.

A stone that has cracked now has a fracture line that was not there before. That does not decide whether it will break, or exactly how, but it changes how later forces act on it. A seed that has germinated can no longer stay dormant. Actual history changes what is reachable. This is an **RS working hypothesis**, and model systems show the pattern.

Note the crack carefully. It may have formed by chance. Once there, it *necessarily* changes the conditions of later fracture. **A thing that came about by chance can carry constraints that are necessary.**

One more distinction. An **opportunity** is an occasion at which some possible thing is actually available. An opportunity can pass with nothing happening: the seed that could have germinated this spring, and did not.

## A game with no players

Chess helps. Its rules define the legal moves: that is admissibility. Some legal moves are played often and some rarely: something like tendency. And there is the game actually played tonight: actualisation.

But the analogy fails at one crucial point. In chess, **players** choose the moves. In created physical order there are no players. Nothing inside it chooses which transition happens, and nothing inside it wants anything. Press the analogy to supply a player and you have turned the substrate into a mind, the very error Scripture forbids.

So how does one transition, rather than another, become actual? Chapter 5 answers: constrained chance.

## Purpose shapes what is possible

If nothing inside creation chooses, is the world purposeless? Scripture says no:

> `While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.`
> *Genesis 8:22*

The question is *where* the purpose sits. The book's answer, a theological inference:

1. **Purpose shapes what is possible.** God's intention is expressed in what created order can and cannot bear.
2. **Possibility shapes what can become actual.**
3. **Actualisation proceeds without the substrate intending anything.**

This protects two truths. The world is not an accident, since its very possibilities were given with intent. And physical order is real and lawful in its own right, neither a puppet nor a mind in disguise. The book calls this **non-agentic runtime**: the ordinary running of creation, purposed without being purposeful in itself.

It avoids two older mistakes: that created things have no real operations of their own, and that God made the world and withdrew. And one caution: to say the substrate intends nothing is not to say God is confined to its possibilities. The primitive order is fixed for every creature that works within it. It is not fixed for its Creator. The book describes the ordinary working of what God made, not the manner of his action in miracles or redemption.

## Chapter recap

- Forbidding (admissibility) and favouring (tendency) differ, and only forbidding changes what is reachable.
- What has happened changes what can happen next; chance events can carry necessary constraints.
- Nothing inside creation chooses; purpose belongs to the Creator, who is not confined by it.
