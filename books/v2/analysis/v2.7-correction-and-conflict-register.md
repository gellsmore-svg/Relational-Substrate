# v2.7 correction and conflict register (Phase A)

**Reader:** the author. **Use:** decide the items marked *ask* before Phase B begins; the rest are reported for the record. **Read time:** about 12 minutes.

Two kinds of entry are kept here. Part 1 lists the places where, according to the supplied sources, the author explicitly rejected or corrected an assistant framing. Under the brief's source policy these corrections govern interpretation. Part 2 lists every collision found between the sources and the revision 2.6 manuscript, or between the sources themselves. None has been silently resolved. Each states the options, a recommendation, and whether the author must decide.

The unabridged conversations were not supplied (see the provenance warning in `v2.7-integration-ledger.md`). Every attribution below is *as reported by the briefs or the audit*.

## Part 2. Conflicts

Each entry: where the collision is, the options, a recommendation, and whether it needs the author.

### C-01. "Proof": conventional sense or the author's sense? (ask; highest impact)

**Collision.** The book uses *proof* in its conventional, demonstrative sense in seven places: Preface L32 ("does not prove the Relational Substrate"); Ch2 L126 ("Coherence is not proof of truth"); Ch10 L82 ("not one that can be proved"); Ch16 L17 and L43; App E.2 last row ("coherence is not proof"); E.7 last bullet. The author's position (brief §2; Add2 §3) redefines proof as a bounded warrant that admits an inferred relation into the framework as a candidate coherence factor. In that sense coherence *can* supply proof, without guaranteeing truth. The seven sentences would change meaning.

**Options.** (a) Adopt the author's sense book-wide. Define it once in Ch2, and rewrite each of the seven sentences to say what warrant is and is not claimed. (b) Keep the conventional word and name the author's concept *warrant*, so that "proof" stays demonstrative.

**Recommendation.** (a). Add2 §3 asks for exactly this rewrite, since each "not proof" sentence must say what kind of proof is meant. The rewrite also removes generic disclaimers. Add2 §3 itself flags its core formulation as possibly an assistant synthesis, so the definition's wording must be confirmed first.

### C-02. Two definitions of "true logic" (ask)

**Collision.** Brief §2: true logic is ultimate global coherence *together with* complete comprehension of every coherence factor and the capacity to apply every relation, inseparable from God's nature. Add2 §3: true logic is "the genuine logical and relational order of reality, whether discovered or undiscovered", with no comprehension component. The first is personal and divine. The second is an impersonal order.

**Recommendation.** Use brief §2, which the brief attributes to the author and which matches audit G01–G04. Add2's sentence describes the coherence that true logic comprehends. That is a part of the definition, not a rival to it. Add2 marks its own wording for review.

### C-03. The grounding objection: how strong? (ask)

**Collision.** Brief §2 says to preserve the author's stronger objection to an endlessly deferred hierarchy of derivative grounds, and not to substitute philosophical permissiveness. Audit Q02 and Pass IV C.1 narrow "any infinity is formally contradictory" to "a purely derivative chain fails ultimate grounding", noting that infinite mathematical sequences are consistent.

**Recommendation.** State the objection as failure of total grounding. An endlessly deferred chain never supplies the ground it defers, so it cannot answer why there is a created order at all. This is the dependency argument the brief asks to preserve, and it does not need the claim that every infinite structure is contradictory. Use the software recursion image only as an illustration (brief §2). **Ask:** does that statement carry the author's full objection, or did the author intend the formal-contradiction claim?

### C-04. Is the ordering of time an open bottleneck or a premise? (ask)

**Collision.** The audit treats a non-circular ordering as the open bottleneck for time (T05, T08, T15, R5): cadence presupposes order, and order seems to presuppose time. Brief §7.2 and Add2 §9 make *consequential sequence* fundamental: a directed relation between action and what follows, belonging to the spiritual order grounded in God's nature. On that premise the ordering is supplied, not derived, and the circularity does not arise. The manuscript (Ch6 L151-155) reads time as the ordering of actualisation, an ontological interpretation, and calls a directed order **succession**. It also reports that in the models direction needs a non-gradient tendency or a one-way rule.

**Recommendation.** Adopt consequential sequence as the author's stated premise, labelled as such. Use the book's existing word **succession** for it rather than a new synonym. Redefine *succession* conceptually in App C, keeping the model result as a note. Record T08 as answered by premise adoption, with the premise visible. **Ask:** is the premise theological (grounded in God's nature) or philosophical, and does the author accept *succession* as the name?

### C-05. Analogy only, or also representation and causal connection? (ask)

**Collision.** The manuscript repeatedly limits cross-category links to structural analogy:

- Ch1 L106;
- Ch6 L90 ("never as an explanation of moral life");
- Ch7 L151 ("likeness is the most that can be claimed");
- Ch14 L36;
- Ch15 L56;
- Ch16 L15 ("Transmission is not revelation").

The author holds two stronger relations:

1. The created order is a *designed representation* that instructs about spiritual relational structure. Romans 1:20 is the anchor; the audit units are O11, G06, H07 and W02.
2. For human persons, the embodied act is part of the actual moral event, *causally connected* to its moral significance though not identical with it (brief §7.1).

**Recommendation.** Introduce one **bridge taxonomy** at the book's existing canonical home (Ch1, "The same word is not the same thing"), following the audit's Pass VI.

- *Structural analogy:* shared form, different natures.
- *Representation or revelation:* a created form that by design shows something of the spiritual order.
- *Causal connection:* within one person, an embodied act and its moral reality.
- *Identity:* rejected.

Then each later passage says which relation it means. Ch7 L151 becomes "at least a likeness; whether a designed representation as well is taken up in Ch16". Ch6 L90 and Ch14 L36 separate the moral reality, which is not a configuration, from its embodied consequences, which are real and causally connected. The non-reduction guards stay. **Ask:** confirm the four relations and the wording "causally connected, not identical".

### C-06. What is the "non-material foundation" of matter? (ask; most important)

**Collision.** Brief §7.1 and Add2 §11: "the spiritual and moral order is prior; the material order is created from a non-material foundation and deliberately expresses or reflects aspects of that prior order". The manuscript holds:

- *Proposition B:* a non-material **created relational** order constitutes matter (Ch3 L60). It is an RS working hypothesis argued comparatively, with C left open.
- *E.7:* "The substrate is not a mind, not a soul, not God, and not a spiritual ontology."
- *Figure 5:* categories are "distinct kinds, not layers built one out of another".
- *Ch12 L71:* "The soul is **not** a configuration of the relational substrate".

**Readings.**

- (i) The non-material foundation is the created relational substrate. Then no conflict exists, but the author may now hold B as a premise rather than a hypothesis. That would exclude C by premise, not argument.
- (ii) It is the spiritual order itself. This conflicts with E.7, Figure 5 and Ch12 L71, and would be a governance-level change.
- (iii) Layered. The spiritual and moral order is prior in being and purpose, and matter is ordered to it and expresses it, so "derivative of" means dependent on and ordered to, not made of. The created relational substrate, non-conscious and not spiritual, constitutes matter (B, a hypothesis). Material individuality is emergent.

**Recommendation.** (iii). It keeps every existing guard and states the author's priority claim exactly. **Ask:** which reading is the author's, and is B now a premise or still a hypothesis?

### C-07. "Opportunity" already means something else (ask)

**Collision.** Ch7 L123 uses *opportunity* as one of four senses of "enabled": removing competitors so that another event becomes the likelier next one. The term comes from the project's later models. The author's *opportunity* (audit A02, L10, T01; brief §7.4) is a distinguishable occasion at which candidate actualisations are available, and it underlies cadence and "time as moral opportunity". The narrative-arc rule is never one word for two things.

**Recommendation.** The author's sense keeps *opportunity*, because it is central and the author's own word. Ch7 L123's sense is renamed (for example *clearance*), with a note that the research records use "opportunity" for it. **Ask:** approve the rename, and the replacement word.

### C-08. "True randomness" and "rhythm" (ask; needs the transcript)

**Collision.** Add2 §10 asks that "the day's accepted definitions of true randomness, constrained stochasticity, determinism, rhythm, cadence and temporal ordering" be preserved. The supplied sources do not contain them. The manuscript distinguishes lawless, unpredictable and probabilistic "random" (Ch5 L9-17), defines stochastic and chaotic, and defines effective and mathematical determinism (Ch9 L15-17). It has no *rhythm*.

**Recommendation.** Do not invent definitions. Supply the relevant transcript passages, or state the definitions, before the Ch5 and Ch6 edits.

### C-09. Two senses of "time" (ask)

**Collision.** Ch6 uses time physically and ontologically: the ordering of actualisation, measured by clocks that are physical processes. Brief §7.2(4) and §7.4 use time in "the author's distinctive theological sense", a relative unit or expression of moral opportunity. Both are needed. Using one word for both silently would break the book's one-sense-per-term rule (App C preamble).

**Recommendation.** Keep **time** for the physical sense (Ch6). Introduce the theological sense in Ch13 with its own marked phrase, for example *time as opportunity*, and an explicit sentence relating the two. **Ask:** which sense the author regards as primary, and the phrase.

### C-10. "The book makes no claim to describe how God acts" vs the Fall thesis (ask)

**Collision.** Ch4 L96 and Ch15 L18 say the book offers no account of how God acts in miracles, providence or redemption. The author's Fall thesis (F04) is a claim about what God's judgement changed in created operating conditions.

**Recommendation.** No contradiction once separated. F04 interprets *what changed in creation*. It is not an account of *the manner of divine action*. Reword Ch4 L96 to "the manner of God's action", and say in Ch14 that the thesis concerns the created order's conditions. **Ask:** confirm the framing.

### C-11. Fall mechanism fork, and animal mortality (ask)

**Collision.** The author's thesis is primitive realignment (P0→P1). The audit's alternatives (C13, F08) are a parameter change within a fixed primitive, or a divinely actualised state under the original primitives. The brief requires the author's thesis to be stated as the author's, and the alternatives kept as research without substitution. Separately, Ch15 L79 deliberately leaves the wider history of animal mortality unsettled. If the realignment covers decay and mortality in the created order generally, the Fall section would settle a question the book leaves open.

**Recommendation.** State the thesis, the two alternatives and what each would explain, without merging them. **Ask:** does the author's realignment thesis extend to non-human death? If not, the section says so. If it does, Ch15 L79 changes and Appendix E records why.

### C-12. Design criterion: status (ask)

**Collision.** The manuscript says a relational world "sits naturally with" Scripture's moral world (Ch10 L98). The author holds that bearing moral consequence is *a primitive design criterion* of the created order (brief §7.1).

**Recommendation.** Status: theological premise, labelled as such (the author's interpretation of creation's purpose). It is not an RS hypothesis about mechanism. Wording at Ch10 L98 and Ch16 L53. **Ask:** confirm the status.

### C-13. Oscillation: adopted candidate or refused primitive? (ask)

**Collision.** E.9 "Not adopted: oscillation as a primitive of the substrate". Ch8 L108: "Oscillation is not assumed to be a primitive of the substrate". Brief §7.5: oscillation is the author's favoured, economical candidate for an intrinsic reference cadence, not derived and not exclusive, and is not to be quietly replaced or dismissed.

**Recommendation.** Compatible if worded exactly. Add an E.2 row: "Oscillation as the source of an intrinsic reference cadence: mechanism candidate, favoured by the author". Keep the E.9 refusal as "not adopted *as an assumption*". Ch8 L108 names the second role. **Ask:** approve.

### C-14. "Epistemic proportionality" vs "disciplined confidence" (ask; minor)

**Collision.** Add2 §7 names *epistemic proportionality*. The book already has the first edition's **disciplined confidence** (Ch16 L31) for the same posture, and practises it in the Ch2 status vocabulary.

**Recommendation.** Keep *disciplined confidence*. State its two-directional rule (over-claiming and under-claiming are both errors) once in Ch2, with "proportionate" as a gloss. Two names for one thing would cost the reader.

### C-15. "Created runtime order": mode or scope? (ask; minor)

**Collision.** The book defines *created runtime order* by **mode**: creation as operating, as against creation's origin in God's act (Ch3 L116; App C). The author uses it by **scope**: the bounded, non-conscious created domain, as against everything that exists, including realities beyond observation (audit O01–O03, H01). On the scope reading, spiritual creatures and souls are created but not part of the created runtime order. On the mode reading, that is undecided.

**Recommendation.** Keep both in one definition, since they are compatible. The created runtime order is the operating, non-conscious created order, and is not all that exists. **Ask:** confirm that souls and spiritual creatures fall outside it.
