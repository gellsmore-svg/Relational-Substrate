# v2.7 definition and terminology register (Phase A)

**Reader:** the author, approving wording before Phase B; then Phase B as the drafting source. **Read time:** about 10 minutes.

Every term the 31 October material adds or changes, with proposed working wording, where it comes from, its canonical home, and any collision. App C's rule still governs: one sense per term, defined once, used without re-definition elsewhere. Wording marked *candidate* is proposed, not adopted. Nothing here is canonical until the author approves it and App E records it (revision rule: change App E first).

Source codes:

- **AQ** author's quoted wording, as given in the brief;
- **AP** author's position, paraphrased by the brief;
- **A2** Addendum 2 wording (which flags itself as possibly assistant synthesis);
- **AU** audit Pass V candidate;
- **BK** existing book definition;
- **NW** new wording proposed here.

## 5. Open research questions, separated from established principles

Addendum 2 §9 and §13 ask for this separation. "Established principle" means held by the book at its stated status, with only the exhaustive mechanism open. It does not mean empirically proven.

**Established principles whose full mechanism is open** (do not present as missing foundations):

- Time as the ordering of actualisation, with succession given in consequence (on the author's premise). Open: physical clock mechanisms; gravitational time dilation (Ch9 L110).
- Identity as relational equivalence, with individual continuity as lineage. Open: what carries individual continuity in physical things (Ch6 L60).
- Coherence as distinct from truth, and error as local misassignment. Open: calibration of warrant thresholds.
- Embodied personhood as the meeting of the created order and the spiritual capacity for relation. Open: the spirit–soul–body relation in mechanistic terms (Add2 §4 category 2, possibly 4).
- History as its own record. Open: how persistent consequence relates to irreversibility.

**Genuine open research questions** (held as such in App E):

1. How an occasion that passes without actualisation is individuated (A11, A14).
2. Whether opportunity cadence rests on actualisations at a deeper order (T13); a possible link to the recursive relational complexity programme.
3. Which minimal primitives suffice for a reference cadence; whether oscillation is necessary, sufficient, one candidate or neither (T15; brief §7.9 Q2).
4. Whether direction can be read from asymmetric propagation of consequence, and irreversibility from persistent consequence (T10, T11, T16).
5. Whether derived constraints are wholly reconstructible from primitives and history (C10).
6. The Fall mechanism: altered primitive, changed parameters within a fixed primitive, or a divinely actualised state under the original primitive (C13, F08).
7. How spiritual-to-created correspondences are classed and tested (Q06, I15).
8. What distinguishes conscious from computational reception (H13), and what change makes intentional choice possible (I16).
9. The minimum warrant for admitting a candidate coherence factor (P12), and how inferential precision can be calibrated (M15).
10. Whether true logic is structure, act, or both (G12); whether logic covers contingent relations (L14).
11. Whether other kinds of consciousness fit the architecture (Q07).
12. How identity, causal provenance and accessible evidence are modelled separately (brief §7.9 Q5).
