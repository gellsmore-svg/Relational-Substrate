## Recursive revision of D

### Pass 1: weakest dimensions of D

1. **human-context-load (82).** Chapter 2 introduces about 11 method concepts. They are low-interaction (I ≈ 2) but numerous.
2. **narrative-flow (75).** Six physical chapters (3–8) risk reading as a technical interlude.
3. **semantic-density and concision (75).** Chapter 6 also carries time and matter on top of identity.

### Revisions (D → D′)

1. Chapter 2 keeps description/ontology, assumptions, levels of claim, the three coherence levels, human knowing and "not a proof". *Coherence is not fidelity* (false attractors) moves to Chapter 9, where global coherence is put to work and its limits matter most.
2. Each physical chapter opens from the stone scene or a question the previous chapter left open, and closes in prose on its consequence, never with a bulleted summary. Physical chapters are budgeted at about 2,500–3,500 words. With the bridge chapter, the physical arc is about 35% of the body. That is a bridge, not an encyclopaedia (brief §23).
3. Time moves out of the main load in Chapter 6. It gets one paragraph (time as the ordering of actualisation), with the relativity discussion in Appendix B.
4. "Matter as stable form" stays in Chapter 6, because the stone scene needs it.

### Pass 2: red-team of D′

- **Does weaving Scripture through the physics imply Scripture teaches RS?** This is a risk (brief §49). Each physical chapter states the relation explicitly: Scripture *bounds* the proposal and does not state it. **Action:** a standing sentence pattern in drafting ("Scripture does not describe X; it requires that whatever X is…"), varied in wording to avoid tics.
- **Does the stone scene become a hidden mechanism?** The scene illustrates. It must not argue. Its limits are stated at first use: it is one everyday case, chosen because every concept appears in it, not because it proves them.
- **Is Chapter 9 overloaded,** being the bridge plus the payoff of global coherence plus analogy/identity plus false attractors? It has four load-bearing concepts at the deepest point of the graph: **high risk by design**. **Action:** allow it to be the longest chapter (about 5,000 words), with two concrete cases (a promise kept; a poisoned river).
- **Does Chapter 12 ("What is allowed") duplicate Chapter 4 (admissibility)?** It deepens it: Chapter 4 covers physical admissibility, and Chapter 12 adds operational and moral layers and explicitly distinguishes them. A one-clause reactivation of Chapter 4 is needed.

Pass 2 produced drafting rules, not structural changes. Further structural passes would trade one dimension against another (for example, merging Chapters 5 and 8 improves concision at the expense of dependency order). **Stabilised: D′.**
