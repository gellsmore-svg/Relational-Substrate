## 5. Anti-ceremony red-team pass

Several scores are at or above 85, so a red-team pass is required.

1. **Circularity.** Does Ch 9 assume what it argues? Premise 1 (human life is relational) is drawn from experience and Scripture, not from RS. Premise 3 depends on Chs 3–8, which were careful to label their claims as hypotheses. The conclusion is conditional on consistency with observation. **No circularity found**, but the argument's force is only as strong as premise 4, a coherence preference, and the book states this.
2. **One term, two senses.** "Admissibility" in Ch 4 (physical) and Ch 12 (three layers). This is handled explicitly in both places. "Identity" for things versus persons is also handled explicitly. "Receive" in Ch 7 (physical) and Ch 14 (receiving the word) is marked as analogy.
3. **Unstated premise.** Ch 3 assumes that a relational base would *ground* human relation, not merely be compatible with it. The book claims only "continuity of form", which is weaker and defensible. A reader may still feel the bridge as suggestive rather than compelling. That is an honest limit.
4. **Hardest passage for the least-knowledgeable reader.** Ch 7, "Step three: carriage". Read cold, the pond and signal-fire cases carry the idea. The calculator certificate paragraph is the most demanding. It is retained because it is the research evidence, and it is placed after the intuitive cases.

**Result:** no material defect requiring structural change. The remaining weaknesses are recorded above for future editions.
