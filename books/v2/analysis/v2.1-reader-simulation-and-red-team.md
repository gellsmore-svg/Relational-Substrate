# v2.1 reader simulation and red-team record

This record is not for the public manuscript. It holds the first-time-reader checkpoints (instruction §35) and the adversarial reviews (§36) for the v2.1 manuscript, with the corrections each produced.

## Simulated first-time reader

**Profile:** intelligent adult, no specialist training.

**Method:** read the chapters in order, then state what the reader now believes the ontology is. Compare that with the concept graph.

### After Chapter 4

> "God made a real world, not divine, held by Christ. Things are configurations within relations rather than self-contained lumps. What can happen is bounded: some transitions are impossible, others merely more or less likely. One history becomes actual from among the possible. Purpose shaped what is possible, but the substrate doesn't choose."

- **Matches the graph:** creator-creature, relation, substrate, possibility, admissibility, tendency, actualisation, purpose-upstream, non-agentic.
- **Misreading found:** the chapter's final sentences ("Something weighted… something made this history actual") invited a *hidden chooser*.
- **Correction made:** reworded to "with no hidden chooser selecting it."
- **Residual risk:** the chess analogy's "players". The chapter already marks this as the point where the analogy breaks.

### After Chapter 7

> "Chance is real but lawful. Things stay the same by staying in their 'class': what nothing they could meet could tell apart. That can include their history. A distinction at a source becomes available beyond it, is carried by things that can change along the way, and makes a difference in a receiver able to take it. The source doesn't always lose anything. Some receivers must count once, but not all."

- **Matches the graph:** equivalence, provenance, transmit, carry, receive and compatibility in their v2.1 senses.
- **Misreading checked:** a "signal substance". The reader instead says "carried by things that can change", which is correct.
- **Misreading checked:** "the soul is a receiver". The chapter's final section blocks it, and Chapter 11 reasserts the block.
- **Residual risk:** "recoverable" may be read as "someone can recover it". The criterion is that the receiver's change *depends* on the source distinction. This is stated in step three and in the glossary.

### After Chapter 9

> "Because physics is relational at bottom, the relational life of persons isn't a strange add-on. Other views can include relation but have to bring in the kind of relation persons have at a higher level. RS doesn't make morality physics, and coherence doesn't prove it true."

- **Matches the graph:** bridge as economy and continuity, analogy-vs-identity, coherence ≠ fidelity.
- **Misreading checked:** "everything is relation, therefore everything is the same thing". The non-reduction list and Fig. 5 block it.
- **Misreading checked:** "coherence proves truth". The section "Coherence is not the same as truth" blocks it.
- **Misreading checked:** "the other views are silly". The serious alternatives are named and partial convergence is conceded.
- **No correction needed.**

### After Chapter 15

The reader's summary matches the synthesis paragraph, which was itself revised to the v2.1 identity and T-C-R definitions.

- **Misreading checked:** hidden determinism. Effective versus mathematical determinism is stated in Chapter 8 and recalled in Chapter 15.
- **No correction needed.**

## Tensions revealed by this revision

1. **Constructive reorganisation.** The constraint laboratory's negative result means the project's hoped-for motif of "dissolution then new organisation" has no formal support yet. The book does not rely on it, but Chapters 13 and 14 speak of restoration producing richer order. That stays theological reflection, explicitly not grounded in the lab.
2. **The identity criterion.** The criterion ("what reachable futures could discriminate") is clear in principle and hard to apply when reachable futures are unknown. The book states the principle without claiming it is operationally easy.
3. **Exploratory research dependence.** Chapters 6 and 8 now lean partly on exploratory research without formal records. The book labels this, but the dependence should be reduced by reconstructing those experiments (see `conversational-research-import-v1.md`).
