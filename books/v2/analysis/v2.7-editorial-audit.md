# v2.7 editorial audit: repetition, defensive framing and hidden premises (Phase A)

**Reader:** the author, deciding which editorial changes to approve. **Scope:** the whole revision 2.6 manuscript (`manuscript/`, 7dd10a8), read in full and in order. **Method:** the `human-book-readability` skill (repetition judged as reinforcement, deepening or redundancy; HCL; rubric vector), with the brief's §8 occurrence labels and Addendum 2 §10's assumption list. **Nothing in the manuscript has been edited.** **Read time:** about 20 minutes; the registers can be consulted rather than read.

Reader profile for the book (unchanged since v2.4): an intelligent general reader with no specialist training in physics, philosophy or theology, willing to think carefully.

## 1. What the evidence shows

Metrics (`scripts/book_metrics.py` with `analysis/terms-edition2.json`; full output in `reviews/v2.7-baseline-metrics.md`) are indicators only.

- 60,924 words in the assembled book. Mean sentence length 14–17.5 words in every chapter; no chapter has more than five sentences over 40 words.
- The script's "not…but" count and "meta" count, rough proxies for corrective and defensive framing, are highest in Chapters 7 (4/0), 10 (4/0), 14 (3/2), 15 (4/0) and 16 (2/2).
- A phrase census for disclaimer wording ("has not derived", "does not prove", "is not a claim", "this does not mean" and similar) found the most in Chapters 6 (6), 10 (5), 9 (4), 5 (3) and 7 (3).

The metrics locate candidates. The judgements below come from reading each passage in its chapter and in the book's dependency order, as §8.2 requires. Similar wording was never treated as repetition by itself. Two passages that look alike often do different work, and the register records why.

**Overall finding.** The book is not padded. Most returns to an idea are legitimate *reinforcement* after distance, or *deepening* at a new level. The real problems are narrower:

1. Six claim clusters are restated without gain, several times each (§3).
2. One explicit promise ("the book says this once") is broken.
3. A habit of ending sections with a disclaimer has accumulated, concentrated in Chapters 6, 9 and 10 (§4).
4. A few formulations carry an unstated premise that the new material makes visible (§5).

None of this needs restructuring. Every fix is local.

## 7. Concept-order findings for the new terms

- **Inference as T-C-R** (I02, I03, I09) needs Chapter 7. It cannot sit in Chapter 2 and is placed in Chapter 12. Chapter 2 carries the epistemology without T-C-R.
- **Opportunity** is introduced in Chapter 4. Its rival sense in Chapter 7 (L123) must be renamed before Phase B, or the reader meets one word with two meanings (C-07).
- **Explicitly and implicitly intrinsic constraints** are introduced in Chapter 4 and used in Chapter 14, ten chapters later. A one-clause reminder is required there (risk rule: recall distance beyond a chapter on a load-bearing prerequisite).
- **Coherence factor** is introduced in Chapter 2 (with proof) and used in Chapter 10 (convergence): order correct.
- **True logic** depends on Chapter 1's Creator–creature distinction: order correct.
- **The bridge taxonomy** (Chapter 1) precedes every passage that applies it (Chapters 6, 7, 10, 13, 14, 15, 16): order correct.
- **Succession** keeps its Chapter 6 home; Chapter 9's use follows it.
- `terms-edition2.json` should gain these terms with their `requires` links, so the metrics script can check the order mechanically after Phase B.
