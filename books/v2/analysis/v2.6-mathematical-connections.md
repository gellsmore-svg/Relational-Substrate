# Mathematical connections: assessment for revision 2.6

Deliverable 5 of the v2.6 brief (`../prompts/v2.6-ontological-revision-brief.md`, Part 10). The book's compact version is Appendix B, "Mathematical connections". Governance is in Appendix E.9.

**Rule applied throughout.** Mathematics supplies coherence constraints, structural analogies and possible experimental frameworks. It is not an ontological revelation. For each framework below, the entry records what it is, what was verified and how, where it touches the ontology, the exact limit of its relevance, its status in the book, and the research question it opens. Sources marked *verified 2026-10-24* were checked against the primary text or the publisher's record during this revision. The others are standard references whose bibliographic details are well established; they are cited only for what they are known for.

## 1. Comparative and qualitative probability (Part 4 of the brief)

- **What it is.** A theory of the relation "A is at least as probable as B" taken as primitive. Keynes (*A Treatise on Probability*, 1921) held that some probabilities are non-numerical and some pairs incomparable. De Finetti (1931) and Koopman (*Annals of Mathematics* 41, 1940) axiomatised comparative probability. Kraft, Pratt and Seidenberg ("Intuitive probability on finite sets", *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 30, 1959, 408–419) showed that for finite sets of five or more outcomes the class of qualitative orderings satisfying de Finetti's axioms is strictly larger than the class representable by a probability measure, and gave necessary and sufficient cancellation conditions for representability. *Kraft–Pratt–Seidenberg result verified 2026-10-24* (several independent secondary sources agree; the five-element counterexample is standard). Imprecise-probability theory (Walley 1991) treats judgments that fix only bounds or orderings.
- **Where it touches the ontology.** It gives a rigorous footing to the book's three-way distinction between logical possibility, qualitative plausibility and quantitative probability (Chapter 2). Comparative judgments can be made, and can be valid, without being representable by numbers.
- **Limit.** It shows that qualitative comparison is a legitimate form of reasoning. It supplies no premises. Every comparison in the book still rests on stated assumptions, and the robust / conditional / unavailable classification is the book's own discipline, not a theorem.
- **Status in the book.** Supports an ontological inference (methodological), QP1 in the epistemic register.
- **Research question.** Can the coherence assessments in `research-synthesis/` record comparative judgments as partial orders with their premises, rather than as scores?

## 2. Spectral geometry (Part 10.2)

- **What it is.** The study of how the shape of a domain determines the frequencies (eigenvalues of the Laplacian) of its vibrations. Weyl's law (1911) shows that area is "audible". Kac ("Can one hear the shape of a drum?", *American Mathematical Monthly* 73, 1966) asked whether shape is determined. Gordon, Webb and Wolpert ("One cannot hear the shape of a drum", *Bulletin of the AMS* 27, 1992, 134–138) constructed two non-isometric planar domains with identical spectra. *Verified 2026-10-24* (arXiv math/9207215 record; AMS feature column).
- **Where it touches the ontology.** (a) Epistemology: a precise case of behaviour underdetermining structure while still revealing some of it (Chapter 2's private function). (b) Constraint: geometry constrains which modes of activity are admissible. (c) The brief's question whether geometry constrains activity, activity generates geometry, or each constrains the other. In an instrument geometry constrains activity. In general relativity the distribution of energy shapes geometry. Which holds in the substrate, if either, is open.
- **Limit.** The results concern idealised membranes in a given space. They say nothing about whether the substrate is spatial; E.5 keeps intrinsic, emergent and proto-spatial families open.
- **Status in the book.** Illustration of an ontological inference (EB1). Not a mechanism.
- **Research question.** In the project's finite relational models, which observables (analogues of spectra) do and do not fix the underlying relational structure? Constraint Laboratory Generation 1b ("observable equality is not kernel equality") is already a result of this kind.

## 3. Synergetics (Part 10.1)

- **What it is.** Haken's theory of self-organisation in open systems far from equilibrium (*Synergetics: An Introduction*, Springer, 1977). Near an instability, a few slowly varying unstable modes, the *order parameters*, determine the many fast, stable modes (the *slaving principle*). The order parameters are themselves generated by the components, so causation runs in a loop (*circular causality*). The laser is the paradigm: above threshold, one mode's amplitude entrains the atomic dipoles. *Concepts verified 2026-10-24* (multiple secondary sources, including a Springer chapter treating the laser as the example from which the general concepts were developed).
- **Where it touches the ontology.** It is the clearest established case of the downward role in the grammar of emergence: a higher-order organisation produced by its components that then constrains them (Chapter 8). It also gives one concrete sense in which an identity organiser can become part of the constraint structure at a higher order.
- **Limit.** Synergetics presupposes the components and a material medium. It describes emergence within matter. It does not touch the emergence of matter, and Haken proposed nothing like the Relational Substrate.
- **Status in the book.** Mathematical/physical constraint within matter; RS working hypothesis across orders (GR4).
- **Research question.** Can an order-parameter reduction be found in any of the Constraint Laboratory's generations, and does it pass the persistence-before-reference tests of E.8?

## 4. Coupled and nonlinear oscillation, solitons and oscillons (Part 10.3)

- **What it is.** Phase locking and synchronisation (Huygens's pendulum clocks, 1665; Kuramoto, *Chemical Oscillations, Waves, and Turbulence*, Springer, 1984). Mode coupling and resonance; the wolf tone of bowed strings, where a string mode and a strong body resonance couple (Fletcher and Rossing, *The Physics of Musical Instruments*, 2nd ed., 1998). Nonlinear frequency mixing. Stable localised structures: the solitary wave (Scott Russell, British Association report, 1844); magnetic skyrmions (Nagaosa and Tokura, *Nature Nanotechnology* 8, 2013); oscillons in vertically vibrated granular layers (Umbanhowar, Melo and Swinney, *Nature* 382, 1996, 793–796; *verified 2026-10-24*), which are localised, particle-like and subharmonic, and can form bound states. The binding is supported by later modelling; the original paper's own reports of "molecules" were not verified in this revision, so the book says only that oscillons "can bind to one another".
- **Where it touches the ontology.** These show that interacting activity can produce persistent, individuated and interacting structures, and that identity can sit in the *relations among modes*. That matches music, where a melody's identity is its intervals. It also continues the first edition's language of phase and coupling, and the technical volume's knotted-structure (vorton) programme.
- **Limit.** Every example lives in a medium that is already given and already material. Nothing here shows that material individuality is constituted this way.
- **Status in the book.** Mechanism candidate (OS1). Oscillation is explicitly not a primitive of the substrate.
- **Research question.** OTR-style: in a minimal relational model with stochastic actualisation, do any rule families yield localised, persistent, interacting patterns, judged against matched nulls? The answer should not be presupposed by the rules (OTR §3, inadmissible result bias).

## 5. Computational mechanics (Part 10.4)

- **What it is.** For a stationary stochastic process, two past histories are *causally equivalent* if they induce the same conditional distribution over futures. The equivalence classes, *causal states*, form the ε-machine, which is provably the minimal maximally predictive representation and is unique (Crutchfield and Young, *Physical Review Letters* 63, 1989, 105; Shalizi and Crutchfield, *Journal of Statistical Physics* 104, 2001, 817–879; *verified 2026-10-24*).
- **Where it touches the ontology.** It is the established mathematical form of the book's identity criterion: sameness of kind or state judged by what reachable futures could discriminate (Chapter 6). The book's criterion is therefore not novel, and Appendix D now says so. It also formalises history-dependent behaviour (causal states can carry history) and fits the E.6 result that admitting carried history into a richer present state restores dependence on the present.
- **Limit.** Causal states are defined relative to a chosen observed process, which is the problem of projection. Predictive equivalence is not individual continuity, and not ontological identity.
- **Status in the book.** Mathematical attribution (ID6) and support for an ontological inference.
- **Research question.** Compute ε-machines for the visible processes in Constraint Laboratory Generations 3 and 8H–8P, and compare the causal states with the hidden relational states. This is a direct, already-specified way to measure "history as present state".

## 6. Information preservation (Part 10.5)

- **What it is.** The communication-engineering toolkit: redundancy and error-correcting codes, channel matching and modulation, invariant-preserving transformations, lossy (perceptual) coding, reconstruction, and provenance tracking.
- **Where it touches the ontology.** It makes precise how identity survives a change of carrier (relational identity compatibility) and why recovery depends on the receiver (receiver compatibility). Perceptual audio coding is a clean case of *receiver-relative equivalence*, the Chapter 6 principle that a distinction can be dropped only where no reachable consequence draws on it. Frequency modulation shows the carrier format doing cohering work.
- **Limit.** Engineering channels are designed. Whether created order produces its own carriers is the open "where carriers come from" problem (Appendix B).
- **Status in the book.** Illustration and support for ontological inferences (ID1, ID5).
- **Research question.** Does any minimal relational rule family generate redundancy, meaning multiple independent carriers of one distinction, without its being designed in?

## 7. Indivisible stochastic processes (Part 10.6)

- **What it is.** Barandes, "The stochastic-quantum correspondence", *Philosophy of Physics* 3 (2025), doi:10.31389/pop.186; *verified 2026-10-24 against the published PDF*. Related papers: arXiv:2309.03085 (the stochastic-quantum theorem) and arXiv:2507.21192. A process is given by a configuration space and transition matrices Γ(t←t₀) from *conditioning times* to *target times*. It is **indivisible** when, for intermediate t′, there is generally no genuinely stochastic Γ̃(t←t′) with Γ(t←t₀) = Γ̃(t←t′)Γ(t′←t₀) (§2.1). The process is therefore generally non-Markovian, and it has no dynamical law between arbitrary intermediate times.
- **How it differs from ordinary dependence.** An ordinary non-Markovian process specifies higher-order conditional probabilities. Barandes instead works with the *equivalence class* of non-Markovian processes that share the first-order conditionals p(i,t | j,t₀). These suffice for all empirical predictions of quantum theory, and the higher-order probabilities are "left unspecified" (§2.2).
- **Complete histories and intermediate times.** "Probabilities assigned to whole trajectories … are then left unspecified as well". Indivisible processes "do not assign unique probabilities to whole trajectories". An intermediate time becomes a valid conditioning time at a **division event**, typically when a system interacts with an "eavesdropping" environment (§3.7). Interference is "a generic discrepancy between the actual indivisible stochastic dynamics and a heuristic-approximate divisible stochastic dynamics".
- **Assumptions for the correspondence.** A configuration space (finite in the main development) and unistochastic transition matrices built from unitary evolution. The correspondence runs in both directions via Hilbert-space dilations. Its extension to uncountable configuration spaces is claimed, but the construction has been questioned (nLab entry "indivisible stochastic process interpretation of quantum mechanics").
- **Where it touches the ontology.** (a) It shows ontic stochasticity can be formulated in detail for quantum systems (Chapter 5). (b) Indivisibility is a mathematically exact notion of *non-decomposable* dependence across time. It resonates with T-C-R's refusal to presuppose a chain of intermediate carriers, and with E.6's history as present state. (c) Division events tie the appearance of new conditioning times to registration by an environment, which resembles reception in T-C-R. (d) Its use of equivalence classes of processes parallels the book's equivalence-class account of identity.
- **Critical assessment.** It reproduces standard quantum predictions and so cannot be empirically separated from rival interpretations. The brief asked for "arguments distinguishing trajectories of probabilities from probability measures over trajectories". No published critique stating the point in those terms was found in this revision (searches 2026-10-24). The point follows directly from Barandes's own §2.2: the formalism yields, for each conditioning time, a trajectory of probability distributions over configurations, but no unique probability measure over trajectories. The interpretive claim that the system occupies one definite configuration at each time, tracing a definite path, is therefore an addition the mathematics does not fix. That is an instance of the book's own rule that a description of consequences does not settle ontology. Calvo (arXiv:2601.18720) offers a sympathetic reformulation. Doukas (arXiv:2602.22095) treats stochastic realisers of quantum dynamics without discussing Barandes.
- **Status in the book.** Open question; a mathematical connection, not adopted (BA1). The book's stochastic hypothesis neither depends on it nor is confirmed by it.
- **Research question.** Do the project's own models, which already show apparent memory from a hidden present relation (Generation 3), admit a precise statement of when a coarse-grained visible process is divisible? That would connect directly to Barandes's division events.

## 8. Quantum Darwinism (Part 8.3, mentioned)

- **What it is.** Zurek, "Quantum Darwinism", *Nature Physics* 5 (2009), 181–188, arXiv:0903.5082; *verified 2026-10-24*. Decoherence selects pointer states. Many redundant records of them proliferate into the environment, and observers who intercept small fragments agree, which accounts for classical objectivity ("environment as a witness"; Ollivier, Poulin and Zurek 2004).
- **Where it touches the ontology.** Objectivity through redundant, independent carriers, structurally like the corroboration of the dodo's identity by independent records, and like T-C-R with many receivers.
- **Limit.** It is a programme within quantum theory. The name is a metaphor for selection of states that leave many copies. It is not Darwin's work and makes no biological claim.
- **Status in the book.** Mentioned for structural kinship only.

## 9. Quantum field theory (Part 10.7)

- **The five-way distinction kept in the book (Chapter 9).** The mathematical formalism; the measurable phenomena; interpretations of states and operators; field realism; material realism. Success bears directly on the first two.
- **Phonons as a calibration case.** The same Fock-space mathematics of quantised excitations describes lattice vibrations in a crystal (phonons). There the ontology is settled on other grounds, and nobody infers additional material entities. This shows the formalism does not by itself decide ontology. It does not show that electrons are like phonons, and the book says so.
- **Bearing on Propositions A, B and C.** A field realist who calls fields "material" changes Proposition A (metaphysical elasticity). A field realist who does not is in the B family. QFT's success therefore does not favour plain A, and it does not single out RS within B.
- **Limit in both directions.** QFT does not establish fundamental material fields, and it does not prove the substrate. Compatibility is the requirement: whatever the substrate is, it must yield QFT's predictions.
