# Architecture candidates

Derived after the concept graph (`concept-graph.md`). Every candidate must be a topological ordering of the REQUIRES backbone. They differ in where method sits, how the physical arc is chunked, and where Scripture carries weight.

## Constraints read off the graph

1. **Shallow roots must come first.** Scripture (depth 1) and the Creator/creature distinction (depth 2) are the root of 44–47 transitive dependents each.
2. **The method cluster is shallow and early.** Description, ontology, assumptions and coherence have depth 2–4. *Global coherence* has the second-highest betweenness (0.171) but few direct dependents: it is a *frame*, introduced early and paid off late.
3. **The physical arc is a long chain:** relation → substrate → possibility → admissibility/tendency → actualisation → constrained stochasticity → identity/persistence → difference/transmit/carry/receive → T-C-R → coordination → regularity → mathematics. The chain is 6–16 deep, and its concepts have difficulty and abstraction of 4–5. No chapter may take on more than about four new load-bearing concepts from it.
4. **The bridge ("no metaphysical gulf") sits deepest of the physical-to-human transitions** (depth 17, difficulty 5). It needs relation, T-C-R, global coherence *and* the analogy/identity guardrail already in place.
5. **Personhood, alignment, corruption and restoration are the deepest nodes** (16–20). They belong late. Their scriptural anchors, however, are shallow and can be planted early.

## Candidate A — Scripture → ontology → physical order → life → person → corruption → redemption

The first-edition shape, modernised. Ch 1 Scripture and creation; Ch 2 the substrate; Chs 3–7 physical ontology; Ch 8 method (description, coherence); Chs 9–10 life and consciousness; Ch 11 persons; Chs 12–13 corruption and redemption; Ch 14 synthesis.

- **Strength:** CBO identity; Scripture visibly first.
- **Weakness:** method comes *after* the physical claims, so the reader meets RS hypotheses before learning how to weigh them. This was the first edition's calibration failure (red-team items 1–2).

## Candidate B — Coherence → Scripture → relation → physical order → life → person → corruption → restoration

Ch 1 coherence and how we know; Ch 2 Scripture as governing frame; then as A.

- **Strength:** epistemic calibration in place before any claim.
- **Weakness:** opens a *Scripture-first* book with generic epistemology. Scripture becomes one input to a coherence method, which inverts the book's governing commitment (brief §26). It also front-loads abstraction before the reader has a felt question.

## Candidate C — graph-derived: frame, method, physical arc in five acquisitions, bridge, then the human and theological arc

1. Scripture and the world that is there (roots: Scripture, Creator/creature, creation real, darkness, categories named).
2. How we know what is there (description/ontology, assumptions, levels of claim, coherence levels, human knowing, coherence ≠ fidelity, observation constrains, not a proof).
3. Relation before things (object-first picture, relation, substrate, runtime, mediation).
4. Possibility and actualisation (possibility, admissibility, tendency, actualisation, purpose upstream, non-agentic).
5. Constrained chance (stochasticity, constrained stochastic actualisation).
6. Identity and persistence (invariant, persistence, information loss, memory, matter, generative constraint, time).
7. Transmit, Carry, Receive (difference, transmit, carry, substitutability, receive, T-C-R, nesting).
8. Regularity, coordination and the place of mathematics (coordination, local/global, regularity, mathematics; science credited; what would count against RS).
9. Why relation matters beyond physics (the bridge; analogy versus identity; global coherence paid off).
10. Life.
11. Dust, breath and person.
12. What is allowed (three admissibilities, alignment, freedom).
13. Corruption.
14. Restoration and redemption.
15. One world, many kinds (synthesis).

Appendices: points of divergence; technical pointers; glossary; notes and sources.

- **Strength:** a strict topological order; method before claims; the physical arc chunked to four or fewer new load-bearing concepts per chapter.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Eight chapters before life (Chs 1–9) may feel long for a theological book.
  - Chapter 2 is abstract before any concrete physical example.

## Candidate D — C with an advance organiser and Scripture woven through the physical arc

As C, plus:

- **D-i.** The book opens on one concrete scene, *seeing a stone in daylight*. Every later concept returns to it: relation (stone, light, eye), possibility, chance in emission and absorption, identity (the same stone tomorrow), T-C-R (the light), regularity (the optics). This is an Ausubel-style organiser, and it gives Chapter 2 a concrete case to reason about.
- **D-ii.** Each physical chapter carries its own scriptural boundary in the body text: Colossians 1:17 with the substrate, Genesis 8:22 with regularity, Isaiah 55 with transmission, and so on. Scripture thereby *governs* the arc rather than framing it from outside, which avoids a "physics middle" that reads as a separate book.
- **D-iii.** Method splits across two placements. The early chapter (Ch 2) gives the *tools*: description and ontology, levels of claim, coherence levels. Chapter 9 gives the *payoff*: global coherence and the bridge. This keeps the frame early and the burden of proof where the material exists to carry it.
- **D-iv.** Chapters 5 and 8 are tempted to merge (chance and regularity are one story). Kept apart because identity and T-C-R must sit between them: regularity *requires* both (graph depth).

## Candidate E — thematic loops (spiral curriculum)

Three passes over the whole arc — creation → persons → restoration — at increasing depth: Part I sketch, Part II physical depth, Part III theological depth.

- **Strength:** reactivation built in.
- **Weaknesses:**
  - Repetition risk is very high (the first edition's worst redundancy dimension).
  - Readers meet "corruption" three times before it is defined.
  - It needs about 30% more words.

## Selection

See `architecture-vector-scores.md`. **D is selected**, after one recursive revision (D → D′) described there.
