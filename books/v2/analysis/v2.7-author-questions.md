## Q1. What does the book mean by "proof"?

**What it is about.** The book uses "proof" in the ordinary sense of conclusive demonstration, mostly to say what the book does *not* do. The 31 October material gives your own, different meaning of proof. The book cannot use both meanings.

**The book now says (eight places):**

- Ch2 L126: "Coherence is not proof of truth. A worldview can be internally coherent and false; a detailed and consistent story about a flat Earth is still wrong. The book says this once, here, and takes it as read from now on."
- Ch10 L82: "That is a more coherent position, though not one that can be proved."
- Ch13 L58: "Physics cannot prove free will, and the relational ontology does not try to."
- Ch16 L17: "The whole project is not an empirical proof of ultimate ontology."
- Ch16 L43: "It is also why this book has insisted that its ontology is a coherence argument, not a proof…"
- Appendix E.2: "A coherent account is thereby true | Rejected (coherence is not proof)".
- Appendix E.7: "Coherence does not prove the Relational Substrate."

**The source material says.** Brief §2 (reported as your position): "What is conventionally called 'proof' is understood as acceptance of a sufficiently warranted inferred relation into the developing framework as a reusable **candidate coherence factor**, open to recursive revision. Retain legitimate conditional deduction without making 'established' or 'proven' a reflexive gate for ontology."

Addendum 2 §3 adds a fuller formulation, and flags that it may be the assistant's wording, not yours: "Proof is a bounded achievement of relational understanding in which an inferred relationship has acquired sufficient warrant, through the applicable coherent structure and constraints, to function as a coherence factor in further reasoning."

**Options.**

- **(a) Adopt your meaning throughout.** Define proof once in Chapter 2 and rewrite the eight sentences so that each says what kind of warrant the book does and does not claim. Example: "The book's argument is a global coherence argument, not experimental demonstration." Mathematical proof keeps its full force within its axioms.
- **(b) Keep the ordinary meaning,** and use another word, such as *warrant*, for your concept. Less rewriting, but your own word "proof" would not appear in your sense.
- **(c) Something else.**

**Recommendation.** (a). Addendum 2 asks for exactly this, because every "not proven" sentence should say which kind of proof is meant. Please also confirm or correct the wording of the definition, since the long form may not be yours.

**Proposed definition for Chapter 2, if (a):**

> **Proof** is the achievement of enough warrant, under stated premises and constraints, for an inferred relationship to be relied on in further reasoning: to become a **coherence factor**. A mathematical proof has this character in its strongest form: within its axioms its conclusion is settled. An experimental result has it within what its procedures can show. A coherence factor keeps its scope, its source and its dependencies in view, so that it can be revised if later work requires. Proof in this sense is real. It is never the possession of the whole truth of what it concerns.

## Q5. How are physical and spiritual realities linked: likeness only, or more?

**What it is about.** The book repeatedly says that links between physical and spiritual realities are only likenesses of form ("structural analogy"). The 31 October material says two stronger things.

**The book now says.**

## Q6. What is the "non-material foundation" of matter? (most important)

**What it is about.** You hold that the material order comes from a non-material foundation. The book already argues something close to this, but its wording rules out one possible reading of your statement. Which reading you intend decides how much of the book must change.

The **spiritual and moral order is prior**; the material order is created from a non-material foundation and deliberately expresses or reflects aspects of that prior order. *That* this emergence occurs is a theological premise in the author's account; *how* it occurs is open research."

**The book now says.**

- Figure 5 caption: categories are "distinct kinds, not layers built one out of another".
- Ch12 L71: "The soul is **not** a configuration of the relational substrate."
