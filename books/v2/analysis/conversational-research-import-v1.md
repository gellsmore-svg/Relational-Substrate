## 1. The 502-series: contextual identity, equivalence and provenance

**Supplied conclusions.**

- Research moved from object identity toward relational identity under transformation.
- A state can be equivalent under one projection and non-equivalent under future interaction.
- Persistence can be re-instantiation within an equivalence class, not a thing travelling unchanged.
- Provenance (past distinctions) matters only where it remains consequential.
- A prior path can be encoded in present relational structure without a separate memory substance.
- A distinction may safely be compressed away only if no later reachable operation needs it.

**Repository evidence for adjacent points.**

- Calculator SC-001/019: a conserved equivalence class across 4,000 distinct paths.
- SC-008: destroyed information is not recovered.
- Calculator MECHANISMS audit: "the certificate's information persists, though the same entity does not travel".
- Constraint-lab Generation 1b: within one coarse qualitative family, up to 1,042 distinct exact kernels at N = 3. At N = 5, two kernels share one observable signature, so observable equality is not kernel equality.

These repository results support the *form* of the 502 conclusions, namely that coarse descriptions hide dynamically relevant distinctions. They do not reproduce the 502 experiments.

**How it is used in the book.** Chapter 6 states identity continuity as preservation of the relevant relational equivalence class, relative to what reachable future consequences can discriminate. It introduces provenance and its quotient criterion. Chapter 7 uses this for carrier substitution.

**To reconstruct.** A formal experiment showing two states equal under a projection that diverge under a reachable operation, with a provenance-carrying versus provenance-erased comparison. Constraint-lab history depth `H = 1` is a candidate next step on the lab's roadmap.

## 2. Stochastic ring and local–global coordination

**Supplied conclusions.**

- Similar local stochastic characteristics do not uniquely determine global behaviour.
- Global behaviour depends on how local stochastic events are relationally coordinated.
- Correlation by itself is not enough. What matters is consequence-aligned, state-dependent coordination: which variables co-vary, under what conditions, through which channels, and with what effect downstream.
- Mean-level closure can be achieved while fluctuation closure stays open, higher moments differ, carrier continuity is unresolved and local histories differ.
- Stable global laws therefore need neither deterministic local transitions, nor independent local chance, nor a unique microhistory. They also do not arise from "randomness plus vague coherence".

**Repository evidence for adjacent points.**

- SC-017: designed covariance cancels the variance of an aggregate exactly.
- SC-013: local cancellation on a ring of regions stays exact but costs 30 → 356 mean events as regions go from 2 to 32.
- H9: multiplication, division and completion needed global orchestration.

These are the calculator's ring and coordination results. The supplied ring conclusions go further, about joint processes with equal marginals, and are not reproduced in the repository.

**How it is used in the book.** Chapter 8 teaches "same marginal probabilities ≠ same joint process ≠ same covariance structure ≠ same global dynamics" with a worked example, and distinguishes mean stability from fluctuation stability.

**To reconstruct.** A published ring experiment comparing equal-marginal processes with independent, shared and state-dependent coupling, reporting means and higher moments.

## 3. Navier–Stokes and effective determinism

**Supplied conclusions.** These are philosophical and ontological, not a derivation.

- Deterministic continuum equations describe macroscopic behaviour accurately even when the microscopic world is stochastic or chaotic.
- Deterministic mathematics at one scale does not imply metaphysical determinism at every scale.
- A smooth field description need not be the ontology of what it describes.
- Effective laws arise through coarse-graining, statistical regularity, conservation, symmetry and local equilibration.
- The RS question is which relational conditions preserve the right invariants, correlations and continuity for stable effective laws to emerge.

**Status.** The scientific content is mainstream (kinetic theory, Chapman–Enskog, hydrodynamic limits). There is no RS derivation of Navier–Stokes, and the book must not imply one.

**How it is used in the book.** Chapter 8, already partly present. The RS research question is now stated explicitly.

## 4. Grassmannian / amplituhedron: projection and hidden fibres

**Supplied conclusions.**

- Observable or geometric equivalence does not necessarily imply dynamical equivalence.
- A projection can map several distinct underlying configurations to one visible or mathematical object, which the research called "hidden fibres".
- If hidden relational degrees of freedom stay correlated with future evolution, projected-identical states can later diverge.
- Coarse-graining can hide distinctions. Hidden distinctions are ontologically irrelevant only if no admissible future process can consume them.
- Mathematical equivalence under one representation may not settle ontological identity.

**Status.** This is a conceptual lesson drawn while studying positive-Grassmannian and amplituhedron structures (one literature summary, RS-705 on Elvang and Huang's amplitudes text, is in the curated reading corpus). It is not an RS result about scattering amplitudes, and the book makes no claim about amplitude physics.

**Repository evidence for adjacent points.** Constraint-lab Generation 1b, "observable equality is not kernel equality", is a direct, repository-verified instance of the same lesson in a different formal system.

**How it is used in the book.** Chapters 6 and 8 (projection and coarse-graining), and Appendix B as a note on provenance.

## 5. Stronger global-coherence dimensions

**Supplied conclusions.** Global coherence requires more than non-contradiction:

- **connectivity** between domains;
- **boundary validity:** translation across a domain boundary preserves what is relevant without category collapse;
- **propagation stability:** a claim keeps its meaning through explanatory layers;
- **cycle consistency:** translating A → B → C → A returns materially the same claim;
- **preservation of relevant distinctions.**

**Status.** Methodological and philosophical. It is not an experimental claim.

**How it is used in the book.** Chapter 2 introduces the dimensions accessibly; Chapter 9 applies them.
