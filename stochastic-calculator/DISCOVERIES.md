# Discovery register

Discoveries here mean observations learned from this apparatus, not claims of
novelty in mathematics, computing or physics. All entries distinguish observation
from RS interpretation. Detailed counts and intervals are in `research/analysis/`.

## D-001 - One output, many paths from the same state

Status: Supported within model.
Observation: fixed initial 3+4 and 17+28 produce 4,000 distinct paths and exact outputs.
Evidence: SC-019, supported by SC-005.
Why it matters: removes boundary randomization as the sole source of diversity.
RS connection: microscopic variation with stable macroscopic identity.
Alternative explanations: conventional invariant-preserving stochastic rewriting.
Next test: quotient structural paths by region relabelling.

## D-002 - Identity preservation stops at invariant destruction

Status: Strong negative finding.
Observation: neutral damage normalizes correctly; single erasure does not recover.
Evidence: SC-001, SC-008.
Why it matters: forbids conflating conservation with error correction.
RS connection: identity recovery needs surviving relational information.
Alternative explanations: exact signed-count invariant, no redundancy.
Next test: matched redundant witnesses without a numeric template.

## D-003 - Reliability curve has a conventional survival explanation

Status: Supported by held-out noise rates.
Observation: six no-fit predictions all fall inside pointwise 95% intervals.
Evidence: SC-006, SC-022; 4,800 held-out trials.
Why it matters: defeats an unwarranted phase-transition interpretation.
RS connection: constrain the meaning of 'constraint strength' and 'probability well'.
Alternative explanations: geometric survival exactly models this scheduler and fault law.
Next test: predeclare a discriminator against survival in a different relational model.

## D-005 - Locality changes latency, not the invariant

Status: Supported within cancellation model.
Observation: larger rings increase passage time and finite-budget timeout rates.
Evidence: SC-013, SC-018.
Why it matters: correctness conditional on termination is weaker than timely output.
RS connection: stability and coordination have an operational timescale.
Alternative explanations: random-walk meeting times and a centralized observer.
Next test: genuinely distributed completion detection.

## D-006 - Large component noise can cancel exactly

Status: Supported, designed correlation.
Observation: component variances 77.19697 cancel through covariance -77.19697.
Evidence: SC-017.
Why it matters: low aggregate variance does not imply quiet constituents.
RS connection: collective coherence may depend on covariance.
Alternative explanations: neutral pair insertion enforces this familiar identity.
Next test: graded covariance with independently specified correlated damage.

## D-007 - An apparent oscillation is a forced parity residual

Status: Supported negative reinterpretation.
Observation: odd identities reach +/-1 alternation; even ones reach zero.
Evidence: SC-009; isolated in SC-020 across 1,400 paths.
Why it matters: seemingly interesting dynamics require controls for trivial invariants.
RS connection: no spontaneous synchronization or new attractor mechanism detected.
Alternative explanations: charge changes in steps of two under periodic sign flips.
Next test: asynchronous perturbations and autonomous recurrence rules.

## D-008 - Endpoint accuracy can conceal repeated loss of identity

Status: Supported within forced-cycle control.
Observation: starting at 1, all 200 paths end at 1 after 24 kicks but each departs repeatedly.
Evidence: SC-020, 2,400 departures and 2,400 returns.
Why it matters: a calculator's final correctness metric cannot measure temporal durability.
RS connection: durability is not equivalent to endpoint identity recovery.
Alternative explanations: deterministic parity under an even observation horizon.
Next test: predeclared random observation horizons and residence-time statistics.

## D-009 - More carry boundaries create more failure opportunities

Status: Supported under replay fault model.
Observation: unprotected accuracy drops from .7725 to .4525 from one to three carries.
Evidence: SC-021, 1,200 trials.
Why it matters: reliability compounds with coordination cost; high-column errors carry more weight.
RS connection: scale-sensitive coordination is a concrete experimental variable.
Alternative explanations: independent replay opportunities and decimal positional weights.
Next test: protected cascades, burst faults, and feedback damping.
