# WP-A: the *origin* of θ=π — is it forced by the substrate?

**Date:** 2026-07-13 · **Status:** analytic result (conceptual, not numeric).
Follows the [θ scope](theta-generation-scope-2026-07-13.md) and WP-B (mechanism
verified). This is the honest edge of the program.

## The question

WP-B showed the *mechanism*: an odd (single) Dirac cone in the hedgehog gives the
Jackiw–Rebbi ½ = θ=π. WP-A asks the *origin*: does the substrate **force** an odd
Dirac sector, so θ=π is generated rather than inserted?

## Result A — θ=π is not an independent knob (positive, real)

θ=π and "the vorton is a fermion" are the **same Z₂**, stated in two languages:

- Finkelstein–Rubinstein: `π₁` of configuration space has a Z₂ generator
  (2π-rotation = exchange) → fermionic quantisation.
- Wilczek–Zee: the soliton's statistics is `J = θ/2π`; **θ=π ⟺ fermion**, θ=0 ⟺
  boson.

So θ mod 2π *is* the statistics. Having (in the conceptual phase) tied the vorton's
statistics to `π₁(RP²)=Z₂`, **θ=π is locked to that same invariant — it is not a
free parameter and was never tuned.** Given a fermionic vorton, θ=0 is *forbidden*.
This is a genuine tightening: θ=π is an *equivalent restatement* of a derived
result, not an extra assumption.

## Result B — but the fermionic (odd) class itself is *not generic* (the honest limit)

To *generate* θ via Abanov–Wiegmann you need a fermionic sector to integrate out,
and Θ = N·π, so θ=π needs **N odd**. Whether the substrate supplies an odd Dirac
sector is a **microscopic choice the director ontology does not fix**, and — the
sharp point — the *standard* fractionalisation does **not** give it:

- **Bosonic route (NCCP¹):** bosonic spinons + a noncompact U(1); no fermion to
  integrate out. The θ/WZW content comes from Berry phases (Haldane, θ ~ 2πS).
- **Fermionic route (the dual):** the standard Néel–VBS fermionic dual is an SU(2)
  gauge theory with **N_f = 2** two-component Dirac fermions — an **even** number →
  Θ = 2π ≡ 0 → a **bosonic** soliton.

So a *natural* microscopic completion of a 3-vector order parameter tends to land in
the **even / θ=0 / bosonic** class — the **opposite** of what the program needs.

*Numerically confirmed* (`instantiation/theta_parity.py`, extending WP-B): N_f
decoupled cones in the hedgehog bind N_f zero modes → induced charge N_f/2, so
N_f=1 → ½ (fermion, θ=π), **N_f=2 → 1 (boson, θ=0)**, N_f=3 → 3/2 (fermion). Parity
is what matters, and the standard N_f=2 dual is a boson.

## The deep finding — a consistency requirement with a real risk of tension

Putting A and B together, honestly:

- The whole fermion + charge-quantisation edifice **requires** the substrate to sit
  in the **odd-Dirac / θ=π** class.
- Result A says that *within* that class θ=π is locked to the fermion (no tuning) —
  good.
- But Result B says that class is **non-generic**, and a natural completion gives
  the **even / θ=0 / bosonic** alternative, which would make the vorton a *boson* —
  **contradicting the FR-derived fermion and collapsing the charge-quantisation
  chain.**

**This also qualifies the conceptual-phase fermion result.** Finkelstein–Rubinstein
shows fermionic quantisation is *allowed* by `π₁(RP²)=Z₂`; θ=π is what *selects* it.
So "the vorton **is** a fermion" was really "**can be**, and **is** iff the
substrate is in the odd/θ=π class." That class is assumed, non-generic, and could
fail. WP-A therefore **lowers** confidence in the fermion and charge-quantisation
results by exposing the dynamical conditional they carry.

## Net status of the θ=π conditional

- **Mechanism:** verified (WP-B).
- **Not a free knob:** verified (Result A — θ=π ≡ the FR fermion Z₂).
- **Forced by the substrate:** **NO — not established, and non-generic.** A natural
  completion gives even/θ=0/bosonic, which would break the chain. **Origin
  unresolved, with a flagged vulnerability.**

Honest label: **PARTIAL, with a genuine risk of failure** — better than WP-B's
"mechanism only," short of closure, and it *reduces* rather than inflates the
standing of the dependent results. Reporting it otherwise would be exactly the
overclaim (D1) this re-anchoring exists to prevent.

## What would actually settle it (and could come out negative)

A concrete microscopic model of *this* substrate whose fractionalisation is
**provably odd-Dirac (θ=π)** — a real research programme with a real chance of a
**negative** result (that the nematic generically fractionalises bosonic/even, θ=0,
making vortons bosons). Until then the fermion/charge results stand **only under the
assumed odd/θ=π class**, which must be stated wherever they are used.

## Anti-drift

No parameter was tuned to π (Result A makes π structural given fermions). The
even/θ=0 risk (Result B) is surfaced, not buried — the honest thing is to carry the
"odd-class assumption" as an explicit, non-generic condition on the whole edifice.
