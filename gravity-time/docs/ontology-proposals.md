# Ontology proposals for later review

This file records proposals that GT findings might make to the RS ontology. Each is for later review and changes nothing in CBO, in the research baseline, or in Appendix E.

## OP-1. "Potential" as a function of relational configuration (from G0)

**Proposal.** Record, as a mechanism candidate, that a label-covariant constraint template defines a scalar relational potential (a count of a relational motif, weighted by the log of the rule's factor). Under a continuous clock, stationary occupancy follows that potential. In this model, "attraction" toward a relational structure is participation in the counted motif, with no named centre or direction.

**Status if adopted:** mechanism candidate, model-scoped.
**Would count against:** a basin produced by these templates that is not described by any motif potential; a physical setting where the corresponding motif has no meaning.

## OP-2. Kinetics are not fixed by potential (from G1)

**Proposal.** Record, as an open question with a negative model result, that the current grammar does not tie relative recurrence rates to the potential. The same stationary basin admits very different near/far activity ratios, depending on how weight is split between formation and dissolution. If RS is to read gravitational clock-rate differences as relational, it needs an additional principle linking kinetics to potential, and the grammar does not supply one.

**Status if adopted:** open question; the negative result is model-scoped.

## OP-3. Clock conventions must be named (from G0 and G1)

**Proposal.** Add to Appendix E's negative register that no temporal claim drawn from these models is clock-free. The lab's jump-chain normalisation and a continuous-time reading give different stationary laws and different rates.

**Status if adopted:** rejected reification. The step count is not a universal clock.

## OP-4. Total admissibility arrest (from G0)

**Proposal.** Note, under the open candidate on limits of primitive event succession, that hard admissibility can drive a relational system into a configuration where no event is admissible. In the model this is total arrest. Whether that bears on T-LIMIT is open.

**Status if adopted:** open question.

## OP-5. Directed succession requires non-gradient constraint (from E0A, E0B)

**Proposal.** Record, as a model-scoped mechanism candidate, that the grammar separates two kinds of tendency. Gradient tendencies, where the log rate ratio is the gradient of a relational configuration function, produce potentials: basins with no stationary direction of succession. Non-gradient tendencies produce a stationary, directed order of relational succession. Any RS account that ties the direction of temporal ordering to constraint structure would need a non-gradient component. The baseline's "time as the ordering of actualisation" is untouched as an interpretation; the proposal concerns *direction*, not ordering.

**Status if adopted:** mechanism candidate, model-scoped.
**Would count against:** a directed stationary order produced by gradient constraint, which is excluded by GT-F12 inside this grammar, so the test would have to come from a richer grammar; or a physical arrow shown to need no constraint asymmetry.

## OP-6. Under direction, kinetics and occupancy are not separable (from E0A, E0B)

**Proposal.** Amend the framing of OP-2. In reversible dynamics, potential (occupancy) and kinetics are independent primitives. Under irreversibility, the kinetic channel a constraint acts through (formation or dissolution) changes occupancy itself (GT-F17, GT-F27). Any principle linking clock rate to potential presupposes reversibility.

**Status if adopted:** refinement of OP-2; open question.

## OP-7. Release of supporting relations as a relational motif (from E0A, E0B)

**Proposal.** Note, as a candidate under persistence and identity, the handover motif. An attachment to a reference structure is followed by dissolution of the peripheral relation that supported it, in a stationary, oriented cycle. The motif is legible relative to an imposed source and relative to an emergent hub. Bears on the laboratory's CL-Q05.

**Status if adopted:** candidate motif, model-scoped, mechanism sketched not derived.

## OP-8. Succession and gathering are distinct (from E1)

**Proposal.** Keep two notions apart in any RS account built on this grammar:
- **succession** is a stationary, oriented order of actualisation;
- **gathering** is a time-weighted preference for relation to a reference structure.

E1 found the handover (succession) undiminished from N=6 to N=7 in every non-gradient family and under every strength scale, in 30 of 30 cells (GT-F37). It found the only gathering effect seen alongside it (CSPH's attachment margin) at or below the null at N=7 in every form and clock (GT-F36). Evidence for one is therefore not evidence for the other (GT-F43, GT-F44). This strengthens OP-7's standing as a motif and qualifies any reading of OP-5 that would link direction of succession to attraction.

**Status if adopted:** distinction, model-scoped. No primitive is added.
**Would count against:** a rule family in which the two are shown to be coupled, so that one cannot change without the other, across sizes.

## OP-9. "Attraction" must name its clock and its density reference (from E1)

**Proposal.** Amend OP-3. An attachment preference in this grammar is an average of a per-density preference over the occupied density profile. Its sign can differ between the per-event and per-time clocks: strengthening CSPH raises one and lowers the other (GT-F40). Size and strength act on the two factors differently (GT-F39, post hoc decomposition). Any statement that a configuration "attracts" should name:
- the clock;
- whether it is raw or density-standardised;
- the density regime.

**Status if adopted:** methodological refinement of OP-3.

## Not proposed (from E1)

A size-normalised family, in which per-witness factors are divided by the number of possible witnesses, would be a grammar change. E1 shows it is not needed to explain the N=6 → N=7 changes of the arrow-carrying rules, because their engaged load barely changes (GT-F38). It is therefore not proposed.

## OP-10. Tendency and admissibility order succession differently (from E2A, E2B)

**Proposal.** Record, as a model-scoped distinction, that this grammar has two generators of directed succession:
- **Soft tendency** orders succession only through cycle affinity. The affinity is exactly σ times the degree difference of the far endpoints (GT-F45), so the orientation is a degree comparison: when σ > 0, relations to better-connected partners persist (GT-F46, GT-F47).
- **Hard admissibility** can order succession with *no* cycle affinity, by making some releases unrecoverable (one-way release, GT-F51). Its orientation overrides σ.

Any RS account of temporal direction built on constraint structure should say which of the two it means. This refines OP-5 ("directed succession requires non-gradient constraint"): within this grammar a hard formation exclusion with σ = 0 also suffices.

**Status if adopted:** refinement of OP-5; distinction, model-scoped. No primitive is added.
**Would count against:** a grammar in which one-way release is shown to be a limit of soft cycle affinity, which would collapse the two.

## OP-11. "Dissolution enables" must name its sense (from E2A, E2B)

**Proposal.** In RS language about release and fragility, distinguish:
- **precedence**: A dissolved, then B happened;
- **opportunity**: B became the likelier next event because competitors were removed;
- **rate**: B's own tendency changed;
- **support**: B became admissible.

They separate cleanly in the model:
- soft-rule "enabling" is mostly opportunity (GT-F49);
- support enabling needs hard admissibility, and is neither necessary nor sufficient for direction (GT-F50);
- an enabled change beats restoration of its support only about half the time.

This bears on CL-Q05. It adds no purpose to fragility.

**Status if adopted:** methodological distinction.

## OP-12. An imposed reference is not neutral to hard rules (from E2B)

**Proposal.** Record that a held reference structure can act as a *witness*, not only as a frame. Under hard path-form or adjacency exclusion, the held triangle forbids every attachment (GT-F53). Statements about "a source" in RS-motivated models should declare whether the source's own relations participate in the constraints applied to others.

**Status if adopted:** methodological caution, model-scoped.

## OP-13. A reference takes part (from E3B)

**Proposal.** Record, as a model-scoped caution with general reach, that a reference structure made of relations acts on other relations through the same rules. In GT, the held triangle's internal paths witness every attachment, which gives an exact, orientation-neutral bias against gathering for every rule that carries the handover direction (GT-F56). With that bias divided out (a counterfactual instrument), those rules gather while keeping their arrow (GT-F57). RS statements about "a source" or "a centre" should declare whether the reference's own relations participate. Strengthens OP-12.

**Status if adopted:** methodological principle, model-scoped. No primitive is added.
**Would count against:** a label-covariant test (a held structure without internal two-paths) in which the direction-carrying families still fail to gather (GT-Q14).

## OP-14. Two kinds of persistence of absence (from E3A)

**Proposal.** Distinguish:
- **rate-limited absence:** re-formation is admissible but slow;
- **gate-limited absence:** re-formation is inadmissible until a supporting relation is undone.

Both occur in the model and separate by whether recovery can happen directly (GT-F55). Under hard formation bans nothing is permanently lost (T3). An absence can persist without becoming arrest. This is offered as a candidate ingredient for later notions of boundary or identity. No identity construction has been made.

**Status if adopted:** distinction, model-scoped.

## OP-15. A hard ban as the end of a soft ladder (from E3A)

**Proposal.** In aggregate observables (orientation, flux, absence duration), a hard formation ban behaves like the extreme of soft suppression (GT-F54). What is qualitatively new is the support: one-way edges, recovery only through gate-clearing, and no direct recovery. RS readings of "admissibility" and "tendency" should therefore treat them as continuous in their statistical effects but distinct in the structure of possible histories. This refines OP-10. The distinction between the two generators of direction becomes a difference of *support*, not of orientation.

**Status if adopted:** refinement of OP-10.

## OP-16. Support can make a site less available (from E4A, E4B)

**Proposal.** Record, model-scoped, that within a structured reference, new relations form preferentially at the sites that are themselves least supported, whenever the rules suppress formation along supported paths or favour release of supported relations (GT-F60). A free internal relation can switch a site off by adding support (GT-F64). Relational accounts of "where things attach" should therefore distinguish a structure's **presence** from its **internal support distribution**. This qualifies OP-13 ("a reference takes part") by saying *how* it takes part.

**Status if adopted:** model-scoped mechanism candidate. No primitive is added.
**Would count against:** a derivation (GT-Q16) showing the sorting to be a kinetic artefact independent of support, or its absence under other formation-suppressing families.

## OP-17. Gathering must survive density adjustment (from E4A)

**Proposal.** Treat a source-relative occupancy preference as gathering only if it survives standardisation to a declared density reference. Natively, the above-null attachment under floor-free references is mostly density composition (GT-F62). This is a methodological rule for any gravity-motivated use of occupancy statistics.

**Status if adopted:** methodological principle.

## OP-16, qualified by GT-P008 (the text of OP-16 above is unchanged)

- **What the derivation adds.** "Support can make a site less available" survives as the description of an exact local lever: an attachment's stationary odds carry the factor 2^(−floor) under the path families (GT-F67).
- **What it shows is not true.** It is **not a general law**:
  - at first order a site's held degree matters as much as its support, and some held structures favour their more-supported site (GT-F68);
  - rate-symmetric rules cancel the lever entirely in continuous time (GT-F69).
- **If adopted, OP-16 should read:** "support acts as a lever on availability, alongside other structural channels". It should not read: "support makes a site less available".

## OP-18. Persistence before reference (from the GT-P008 architecture review)

**Proposal.** Treat a "reference" as model-scoped only after it is shown to persist beyond its constituent relations. That means a lifetime-to-turnover ratio above a matched-density null, in each clock. The steps are:
- in finite label-covariant models, references the system makes for itself can only be metastable (GT-F72);
- held references are boundary conditions whose results do not transfer to emergent references without this test;
- identity through change, and then displacement, come after persistence.

**Status if adopted:** a methodological ordering principle. It adds no primitive. Tracking identity needs no history axis (8G gate: an observable on trajectories).
**Would count against:** a natural reference notion in the grammar that needs no persistence (none is known).
