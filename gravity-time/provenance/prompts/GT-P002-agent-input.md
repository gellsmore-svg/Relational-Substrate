## Mission

Continue the existing GT research programme using **gravity-motivated, coherence-guided, recursively adaptive exploration**.

The programme is motivated by whether relational constraints could eventually account for basin-like attraction, temporal succession, relative clock behaviour and emergent spatial relations. **This gives the inquiry direction; it does not prescribe experimental outcomes.**

Do not organise the next stage as “choose a gravitational hypothesis and try to prove it.” Equally, do not launch an indiscriminate atlas of all possible relational rules disconnected from the inquiry.

Instead, choose **several plausible, complementary primitive constraint configurations** that might illuminate the relational conditions under which persistent structures, asymmetric dynamics, connection, dissolution, recurrence or basin-like behaviour arise. Observe what actually happens. Interpret it globally, update the coherence map, and select the next experiment *because of those observations*.

The governing loop is:

> **Orient → Select informative contrasts → Freeze → Explore → Observe → Explain → Update coherence → Recursively redesign → Freeze again**

Run a **bounded sequence of adaptive exploratory rounds**, normally two and at most three in this execution, if computationally feasible and justified by information gained. Do not merely plan the programme; conduct the scientific exploration. Do not automatically execute the existing G2 plan.

## 1. Shared methodological definition: Open-Thetical Research

The author calls this method **Open-Thetical Research (OTR)**. The name is a project-coined working term, not a claim to a recognised established scientific category.

**Definition:** Open-thetical research is a coherence-guided, recursively adaptive method that begins with a meaningful field of inquiry and several incompletely defined possibilities. Experiments are selected for their ability to clarify relations among those possibilities and discover unexpected behaviour, without requiring a particular hypothesis to be confirmed. Every bounded exploration is followed by analysis, global-coherence mapping and a newly reasoned experimental choice. The desired convergence is **greater understanding**, not necessarily agreement with the starting intuitions.

This method is intended to be reusable across **both** the Constraint Laboratory and the GT programme. Treat its scientific governance as shared while respecting separate programme objectives, experiment histories and provenance.

Create a clean, durable methodological reference suitable for adoption by both strands (suggested path `research-methods/open-thetical-research.md`). If possible, import the exact shared definition supplied with this prompt, rather than independently rewriting it. If that file is not supplied, use the above definition and clearly flag it as a local transcription awaiting comparison. Do not quietly assume it exists on another branch. Document a practical cherry-pick or integration route so the two programmes can reference one version.

### Difference from other research modes

- **Confirmatory testing:** asks whether a precisely stated prediction holds.
- **Unbounded exploration:** asks what happens anywhere in a vast possibility space.
- **Open-thetical inquiry:** asks which *bounded, purposefully selected* experiment could reveal the most about a cluster of open possibilities, including ones we have not yet recognised.

A candidate need not yet be falsifiable to motivate inquiry. Once a precise scientific claim emerges, introduce discriminating and independently frozen confirmatory tests when justified.

## 2. The research direction: a basin, not a baked-in gravitational result

Our motivating picture is a stable relational configuration that could condition the actualisation of other configurations. Depending on the constraints, this might manifest as enhanced approach, retention, arrest, cyclic return, fluctuating organisation, directional asymmetry or something entirely different.

Keep these questions open:

1. What kinds of relational configuration generate persistent concentrations or “basins” of subsequent relational activity?
2. How do **formation preference**, **dissolution suppression**, **hard exclusion**, and **combinations** behave differently?
3. What role do persistent structures, transient structures, feedback and present-state context play?
4. When, if ever, do asymmetric local relationships produce persistent ensemble or trajectory-level direction?
5. Do local event patterns or recurrent structures change with relational context, and are those effects intrinsic or consequences of an imposed clock convention?
6. Does a coherent notion of relative nearness or distance emerge from the configuration, or depend entirely on the observer's choice of graph statistic?
7. Which behaviours are contrary to our starting picture, and what do they reveal?

These are **open-thetical questions**, not acceptance criteria. No run needs to answer all of them. Do not demand gravitational analogy from every result.

### Permitted directional selection

The experiment designer **should intentionally favour the conceptual neighbourhood of gravity**: persistent motifs, source–environment coupling, relational incidence, competing formation and dissolution rules, stochastic constraints, possible recurrent dynamics, and reference versus unscaffolded arrangements. This prevents wasted effort on an almost limitless unrelated space.

### Forbidden outcome selection

Do not encode an inward direction, a Newtonian potential, inverse-square acceleration, clock dilation, preselected radial decay, or the answer “gravity emerges” in the primitive rules. Do not select only attractive-looking trajectories or exclude negative results. Do not optimise a single score for similarity to gravity.

A deliberately **held source** is allowed as an exploratory instrument, not evidence of spontaneously emergent mass. **FREE systems** are also essential, not the only permitted legitimate systems. Study meaningful held/free comparisons and keep their conclusions distinct.

## 4. Establish several live candidate lenses without treating them as targets

Maintain an intentionally plural, provisional possibility register. Initial lenses may include:

- **D — Directional tendency:** conditional transition weights create a bias in sequences of changes.
- **A — Asymmetric admissibility:** constraints change the available future pathways.
- **A→D — Derived tendency:** some path availability structures generate an aggregate directional preference.
- **B — Basin by retention:** apparently attractive occupancy arises mainly because configurations are retained rather than approached.
- **C — Coordination or competing rules:** interactions between constraints yield behaviour no individual rule predicts.
- **K — Kinetic freedom:** the same stationary configuration can support very different event dynamics.
- **E — Emergent source and proximity:** persistent reference structures and local orientation arise within free dynamics.
- **X — Artefact families:** density, imposed source, finite boundaries, finite-state saturation, clock normalisation, projection and estimator bias explain apparent patterns.

The register is **not a checklist of phenomena to manufacture**. Add new lenses freely when observations warrant it. Split or retire lenses when necessary; preserve their history and rationale.

A useful exploratory experiment may illuminate three or four lenses without resolving any of them. That counts as success in information value, not failure because no hypothesis was proven.

## 5. Selection principle: choose complementary informative constraints

Pick a *small set of deliberately contrasted rule families*, guided by the high-level basin question and the findings from G0/G1. Consider, without blindly repeating G0:

- favouring formation conditional on relational support;
- suppressing dissolution conditional on support;
- hard prohibitions, but with attention to arrest and absorbing states;
- opposing or competing constraints that can cancel, frustrate or redirect one another;
- context-sensitive combinations or higher-order relational gates available in the current grammar;
- neutral and density-only controls;
- HELD and FREE arrangements with matched meaningful initial conditions.

Use the existing formal grammar first. Follow the 8G compilation test before adding any purported new primitive, history axis, spatial property or clock.

**Select for expected information value, not probability of confirming a preferred mechanism.** When deciding which constraints to run, justify their complementarity: what different observations could they produce, what could we learn even from a null result, what interactions might be exposed, and what assumptions might be challenged?

Do not hand-pick only rules already proven to give attractive occupancy. Include enough alternatives to challenge that pattern.

For a large search domain, use reproducible stratified sampling across **mechanism families** and an explicit coverage manifest. Exact analysis of a small canonical cell is preferred when feasible. Avoid unwarranted claims of exhaustive search.

## 6. What to observe without narrowing the discovery window

Predeclare a broad **observation portfolio**, not an outcome score. It should include as feasible:

| Category | Observables / open questions |
| --- | --- |
| Relational occupancy | Connection densities, motifs, clustering and stationary distributions |
| Local organisation | Concentration around persistent substructures; spontaneous localisation in FREE histories |
| Dynamical change | Formation, dissolution, approach, escape, capture, cycling and arrest |
| Trajectory shape | Transient sequences, rare excursions, reversible versus irreversible-looking episodes |
| Persistence | Survival, recovery, transformation and breakdown of distinguishable structures |
| Coordination | Cross-component dependence, covariance, conditional gating and synchronised or anti-correlated changes |
| Asymmetry | Labels versus symmetry orbits; individual histories versus ensemble behaviour |
| Succession | Event order, waiting/recurrence, activity and differences under explicit clock conventions |
| Emergent spatiality | Graph connectivity, shortest paths, shells and alternative relational closeness descriptors |
| Equivalence | Same occupancy with different kinetics; different syntax with the same kernel; projection losses |
| Surprises | Anything not anticipated by the above and not adequately described by current motif labels |

No one observable defines scientific value. Preserve weak and strong findings, negative results, confounders, ordinary behaviours and anomalous trajectories.

Avoid promoting an imposed source, chosen shell measure or continuous-time normalisation to primitive physical reality.

Where physical analogies are later considered, distinguish gravitational attraction from mere stationary clustering, and physical clock comparisons from raw event counts under a chosen mathematical clock.

## 7. Recursive experimental programme: GT-E0A, E0B, optional E0C

The key revision from the original GT-P002 is **real iteration**, not one atlas followed by a static recommendation.

### Round E0A — Orient and illuminate

Choose a compact, scientifically meaningful collection of basin-relevant but varied constraints, including complementary positive/negative and scaffolded/unscaffolded conditions. Search for broad patterns and interactions that existing G0/G1 observables did not fully characterise.

Before execution: plan, cost, nulls, measurement portfolio and stop conditions are committed and pushed. Run the round, verify it, then publish an **intermediate coherence reflection**. Explicitly state the observations that surprised the investigators, including disappointing and weak findings.

### Round E0B — Adjust because of what E0A revealed

Choose the second round **only after reading and interpreting E0A**. Do not select all E0B scientific cells in advance. Options include changing a constraint pair, adding a contrasting gate, varying the relative grade, comparing HELD/FREE conditions, examining overlooked transients, or applying a genuinely new observational channel to existing kernels.

Record an *E0A → E0B decision note* explaining what was learned, which competing explanations are now live, why this experimental adjustment has information value, and why seemingly obvious alternatives were not chosen.

Freeze and push a separate E0B plan **before** any E0B scientific cells are run; execute, verify, record all results and update coherence.

### Round E0C — Optional, only if informative and affordable

After E0B, decide whether one further small round could illuminate a genuinely consequential ambiguity or an unexpected mechanism. Do not run it by habit, and do not turn it into parameter tuning to obtain gravitational resemblance.

If justified, predeclare and freeze it independently and repeat the process. If E0A/E0B have resolved the accessible questions, exhausted the resource budget or identified a barrier requiring substantial new machinery, stop transparently.

**Execution expectation:** Complete at least E0A and, normally, one observationally motivated E0B round within a predeclared bounded budget. If a second round is not scientifically justified or a documented technical limit prevents it, stop and explain rather than invent a reason for further computation. Never perform post-freeze covert retuning.

## 8. Scientific governance and anti-bias checks

For **each** new round freeze and push, before actual scientific execution:

- canonical grammar cell, rule-family coverage and sampling strategy;
- choice and meaning of HELD/FREE scaffolds;
- initial-condition policies;
- observable definitions and clock/projection conventions;
- seed schedules or exact kernel methods;
- controls and artefact comparisons;
- computational work count, memory/disk budget, runtime cap and termination conditions;
- acceptance tolerances for numerical verification (not outcome desirability);
- code/engine versions, frozen-plan hash and reproducibility information.

Engineering smoke tests use synthetic fixtures or declared pre-exposed cells. Do not use representative unopened scientific outcomes to tailor the plan. Declare any unavoidable pre-freeze exposure.

Do not replace a negative result with a revised positive-looking estimand. Publish the first result and open a separately frozen improvement or check.

Keep a clear division between:

1. **formal-system observation**;
2. **mathematical mechanism/inference**;
3. **RS/global-coherence interpretation**;
4. **physical comparison or external evidence**;
5. **theological premise or interpretation**.

A successful numerical experiment validates its declared model and calculations, not the physical truth of RS. A failed proposed mechanism can still be an important coherence finding.

Retain nulls, counterexamples and fragility: a weak or short-lived outcome may be informative by its *conditions of existence or failure*.

## 9. Explanation after each exploratory round

After the observational record is frozen, ask:

1. What actually occurred, as opposed to what was expected?
2. Which classes of behaviour are qualitatively distinct rather than representational variants?
3. What explains each distinctive observation, and is that explanation already known mathematically?
4. What changed when formation and dissolution rules competed or combined?
5. Did a structure appear, persist, disappear, oscillate or transform—and through which transitions?
6. Which effects vanished after controlling for density, clock, finite boundary, symmetry or normalisation?
7. Which existing possibilities were clarified without being decided?
8. What new question emerged that we did not know to ask?
9. What is the *smallest* feasible new experiment that could reveal more?

Do not measure success by the number of supportive hypotheses or the visual impressiveness of an attractor. Preserve the ordinary baseline distribution so rare selected exemplars are not mistaken for typical behaviour.

For unexpected post-hoc discoveries, record discovery status. Seek separate predeclared confirmation only when the observation matures into a precise claim.

## 10. Coherence map and cross-programme propagation

Use the existing `gravity-time/coherence/` ledger and generated Markdown/Mermaid graph. Do not create an incompatible second evidence schema.

Each observation or inference needs a unique ID, provenance, quantitative value where relevant, magnitude, robustness, temporal character, dependence on scaffolding, clock, projection and parameters, epistemic layer, and labelled relations to other findings. Support, contradictions, qualifications, explanations and open questions should remain distinct.

**Keep a shared methodological reference** for both programmes, but do **not** merge their result registers. Instead:

- link GT findings to specific Constraint Laboratory theorem, motif or counterexample IDs;
- identify when a GT result suggests a new generic Constraint Laboratory question;
- identify when a Constraint Laboratory result rules out or reframes a GT candidate;
- propose cross-programme updates with clear source/branch/commit references;
- never silently modify the other branch's scientific history;
- preserve historical records; publish corrections as new linked findings.

When possible, commit the shared OTR definition to a stable common repository path and document how both branches will reference the same version. If this requires editing the other active branch or merging independent work, **prepare an integration proposal rather than doing so without explicit direction**.
