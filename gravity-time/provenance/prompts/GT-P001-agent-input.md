
# Relational Substrate — Gravity, Temporality and Emergent Spatiality Research Programme

## Mission

Establish a new, independently governed research programme within:

`gellsmore-svg/Relational-Substrate`

Working title: **Gravity, Temporality and Emergent Spatiality**

Proposed branch: `research/gravity-time-admissibility`

The programme must build on the existing Relational Substrate ontology, Constraint Laboratory, stochastic research, formal results and historical investigations.

It must not begin from scratch or needlessly duplicate experimental infrastructure.

## Central research question

Can a minimal architecture of relational constraints, operating through constrained stochastic actualisation, produce both:

1. emergent behaviour resembling gravitational attraction; and
2. systematic differences in the relative progression of recurrent physical processes, analogous to observed gravitational clock-rate differences?

Can both behaviours arise from the same underlying constraint architecture without independently imposing the equations or mechanisms that describe them?

A further question is whether the same architecture helps explain the emergence of spatial ordering, distance, direction and temporal succession.

## Foundational commitments and open questions

Respect the current CBO v2.4 research baseline.

In particular:

* Relational primacy is the governing RS proposal.
* T0 names whatever conditions actualisation; its deeper ontology is open.
* T1 is actualised, history-bearing relational order.
* Constrained stochastic actualisation is a working hypothesis.
* Admissibility and tendency are distinct.
* TCR is the candidate grammar of consequential relation.
* Substrate spatiality is unresolved.
* Time as the ordering of actualisation is a working ontological interpretation, not a completed temporal theory.
* A fundamental upper limit or constraint on primitive event succession remains an open candidate.
* Design is an explicit upstream premise of the research programme, not an experimentally derived outcome.

Do not canonise historical mechanisms such as substrate tension, closure knots or universal cadence.

Preserve them where appropriate as alternative candidate interpretations.

## Research candidates

**D — Directional tendency**

A relational configuration modifies transition weightings such that some trajectories become preferentially realised.

**A — Asymmetric pathway availability**

A relational configuration changes the accessible future pathways, potentially producing an emergent directional tendency without a primitive directional instruction.

**A → D — Emergent tendency from admissibility**

A deeper primitive constraint changes the structure of admissible pathways, and directional bias is a derived statistical consequence.

**U — Unified gravitational-temporal constraint**

The same underlying constraint configuration that generates basin-like trajectories also modifies the accessibility, coordination or frequency of recurrent actualisations.

All remain candidates. Do not privilege one because it resembles familiar physics or because it fits the researcher's intuition.

Investigate additional mechanisms if the existing candidates are inadequate.

## Scientific and conceptual discipline

Preserve the existing laboratory's standards:

* predeclared experimental plans;
* exact small-system analysis where possible;
* reproducible seeds and replay;
* parameter and symmetry robustness checks;
* negative controls;
* null and failed results;
* explicit separation of observation, inference and ontology;
* frozen comparison targets before decisive runs.

Before adding any new primitive or expressivity axis, investigate whether the proposed behaviour can be represented by the existing grammar.

Do not confuse representation multiplicity with genuine additional admissible pathways.

Do not introduce primitive metric space, gravity, mass, direction, time or a universal clock merely to make a model behave as desired.

Any imposed structures must be declared openly as experimental scaffolding rather than presented as emergent discoveries.

An experiment that cannot produce gravitational behaviour may still provide a valuable constraint on the ontology.

## Physical relationships as comparison targets

Relevant observations and mathematical relationships include:

* central gravitational attraction;
* radial dependence of gravitational acceleration;
* gravitational potential differences;
* gravitational frequency shifts and clock comparisons;
* recurrent oscillation and frequency stability;
* inertial and freely falling behaviour;
* physical symmetry and coordinate invariance;
* relevant conservation relationships.

Do not require the initial ontological hypothesis to reproduce all these equations before it can be investigated.

Equally, do not claim physical corroboration merely because a toy model produces a visually similar trajectory.

## Initial scope

The first implementation stage should prioritise the emergence of a stable relational basin from minimal constraint structures.

Only progress to temporal behaviour when the mechanism and relevant observables are adequately defined.

Provide a computational feasibility assessment before initiating expensive exhaustive searches.

Avoid broad brute-force searches whose state spaces, costs or expected information value have not been established.

## Deliverables

Produce:

1. A research charter and programme architecture.
2. A map of existing findings, code and mechanisms to reuse.
3. An explicit register of hypotheses, alternatives and conceptual dependencies.
4. A phased experiment programme with clear discrimination tests.
5. Initial small-scale executable experiments where justified.
6. Reproducibility records and null controls.
7. A coherence analysis connecting findings to the wider RS ontology.
8. A record of open questions, negative results and next investigations.
9. A draft pull request documenting the new research programme.

Do not rewrite CBO, revise established ontology, merge the branch or silently reinterpret previous findings.

Where new findings suggest changes to the ontology, produce explicit proposals for later review.

## Governing objective

Discover the simplest relational constraint architecture capable of explaining the greatest number of genuinely distinct phenomena, while preserving the ability to discover that a proposed mechanism is wrong.

The programme is not being asked to prove Relational Substrate.

It is being asked to discover what follows from its foundational proposals, where those proposals are coherent, where they need refinement, and whether their explanatory reach can be extended towards gravitation, temporality and emergent spatiality.
