## 2. The E5C question and competing explanations

> **Once a flexible relational property has reached a fully renewed partner set, how often does it remain continuously true with the original partners absent over several *subsequent anchor-local changes*, relative to specified traffic-matched and null dynamics?**

The word *once* means the **first qualifying renewal of a Palm entrance-sampled episode**, not a cherry-picked particularly long renewal, and not a configuration selected from the stationary law at a random time. Retain both the unconditional probability of reaching durable renewal and the probability conditional on first renewal.

Keep multiple live accounts, with predictions expressed as discriminating interpretations rather than success targets:

- **D1 — durable reconstruction:** Replacement is followed by a nontrivial interval of continuing organisation with no re-entry by original participants, more often than NK and the other declared controls.
- **D2 — flicker-assisted renewal:** Renewal is frequently followed by immediate re-entry or exit; E5B's renewal statistic was too permissive.
- **D3 — density-layer traffic:** The apparent effect is mostly explained by NK; previous ND excess was a weak comparator.
- **D4 — within-layer/local kinetics:** Any remaining advantage stems from local kinetic arrangements not matched by NK. This is a valid mechanism in the finite process but not automatically an endogenous self-maintaining object.
- **D5 — occupancy and entrance composition:** Long apparent durability is amplified by high predicate prevalence or by the original-entry participant-count distribution. Stratifying entrance sizes changes the interpretation.
- **D6 — clock/readout dependence:** Comparison signs or magnitudes change when continuity is measured by local events rather than continuous-time or global events, without implying physical clocks.
- **D7 — null, structural impossibility or unanticipated mechanism:** The effect disappears, cannot be measured at this size, is numerical, or behaves unexpectedly.

Do **not** require a positive effect to justify the work. A clean negative or a rigorous reduction to a known mechanism is a full scientific advance.

## 4. Required mathematical definition of *durable renewal*

You may refine implementation details after proof-fixture analysis **before the freeze**; record each choice. The following semantics are mandatory. Prove what is feasible, not just what the code returns.

### 4.1 Episode and original participants

Let `X_t` be the same irreducible finite continuous-time chain with jump chain `P`. Let `A` be one of the E5B anchored predicate sets. Start a stationary **Palm entrance-sampled** episode at `A^c → A`, with entry-state law `μ_in / J_A`. Record `S0`, the exact original partner set *in the state immediately after entry*. Use the same canonical, permutation-covariant observer as E5B; the dynamical rules cannot inspect `S0`.

Define **renewed states relative to S0**:

```
R(S0) = {x in A : participants(x) ∩ S0 = empty}
```

`τ_R` is the **first** time such a state is attained after entry and before exit from `A`. Cases where R is structurally impossible are `STRUCTURALLY_IMPOSSIBLE`. An episode which exits before R contributes zero to unconditional durability but is **not** treated as a failed numerical solve.

### 4.2 Post-renewal survival at local-event horizons

For an episode whose first renewal occurs at a state `y in R(S0)`, inspect the *next local events* at the frozen anchor. Define `D_h` as the event that the next **h** anchor-local pair toggles all leave the trajectory in `R(S0)` immediately after each local event, with no intervening exit from A or reappearance of any original partner. Nonlocal events do not count toward h but remain part of the full Markov evolution and can alter future rates. `D_0` holds as soon as first renewal is reached.

Choose pre-frozen horizons **h = 0, 1, 2, 4, 8** unless synthetic feasibility/work-budget checks *before freeze* demand a smaller, documented fixed set. Prefer retaining 1, 2, 4 as the minimum informative comparison if resources allow. Record any adapted horizon set and its scientific rationale before observing E5C cells.

Report, for target and **each** comparator:

- `R0 = P_Palm(τ_R < τ_exit)` — reproduce E5B's first-renewal probability (replication check, not new evidence).
- `U_h = P_Palm(τ_R < τ_exit AND D_h)` — **unconditional** durable-renewal probability, with `U_0 = R0`.
- `C_h = P_Palm(D_h | τ_R < τ_exit)` — **conditional** post-renewal survival, if `R0 > 0`; `C_0 = 1`, `U_h = R0 C_h` (frozen consistency identity).
- `P(return before exit | first renewal)` and `P(exit before return | first renewal)` as separate causes when feasible, with conventions for episodes that neither event occurs within the finite observation horizon. Do not assign both outcomes to the same event.
- The distribution of `|S0|` under **Palm entry** and the feasibility fraction. Report values within each entry-size class and their weights; do not confuse a pooled change with within-class improvement.
- `immediate_original_return`: whether the very next anchor-local event restores an original participant. Distinguish this from E5B's `reversal_next_event`, which toggles exactly the previously changed edge back.

The indicators must be constructed from actual finite-chain transitions and states, not inferred from stationary prevalence alone. Use conditional-on-renewal `C_h` beside unconditional `U_h`: conditional survival can improve merely because the difficult episodes never renew, and unconditional survival can improve merely because entry composition changes. Neither may replace the other.

### 4.3 What happens between local events?

**Be rigorous here.** Skipping a nonlocal event in the *count* is not a licence to discard its effect on transition rates. The effective transition law between successive local events must sum over arbitrarily many nonlocal jumps, with correct absorption if the predicate can fail and with a proof/independent fixture check. For the specific flexible anchored predicates, prove that only local events can change membership in A or reintroduce an original partner; that does not mean nonlocal events can be removed from the stochastic process.

Possible mathematical implementation: partition `P = P_local + P_remote` (substochastic pieces on the relevant killed state domain). A local-event kernel may be obtained via a convergent resolvent of `P_remote` (or a direct killed-chain linear solve), then propagated for horizons h. Justify invertibility, positivity, boundary handling and the treatment of the local exit event. Do **not** substitute `P_local / q_local` without showing its validity: remote transitions can change rates before the next local event.

When first renewal is reached, the process must be weighted by the **first-hitting distribution**, not by `π` restricted to R. Preserve the conditional first-renewal state distribution, including the original S0 observer register. A stationary average over renewed configurations would cause selection bias and fail the question.

### 4.4 Additional clock reading and traps

Primary horizon is **anchor-local event count**. Optionally include a small, independently predeclared continuous-time post-renewal survival channel if bounded fixture timing supports it, but keep it separate from event counts and do not invent physical units. For interpretability, report the anchor-local event hazard or rate dispersion as an explanation, not as a fitted correction. Avoid claiming “time-independent identity” because the observer anchor is fixed.

If a model has an exactly zero/degenerate renewal probability or an extremely rare entry class, clearly distinguish structural impossibility, mathematically possible but very small probability, numerical unresolved, and genuine zero. Predefine rare-bin and solver conditioning policy before freezing; do not smooth or retune based on cell results.

### 4.5 Theorem and sanity-check requirements

Derive and test at least:

1. **Feasibility T10 inherited.** At an entry size s and threshold k, complete renewal is possible iff the number of non-original candidates is at least k. `F_STAR` at N≤6 remains excluded from full renewal.
2. **Monotonicity:** `U_0 ≥ U_1 ≥ U_2 ≥ U_4 ≥ U_8 ≥ 0` and similarly for defined `C_h`.
3. **Consistency:** `U_h = R0 C_h`; `U_0` independently replicates the published E5B renewal on all 12 laws within the frozen numerical tolerance.
4. **Relabelling covariance:** permuted anchor/entry sets have identical results.
5. **NULL fixed point:** N0/ND/NK are identical on NULL, within tolerance. Independently verify any simple closed forms you can derive on small systems.
6. **Rate rescaling:** uniform rate scaling leaves local-event horizon probabilities unchanged; a nonuniform *remote* rate change may alter them via the effective local kernel. Test both on synthetic finite systems.
7. **No rule-level history:** `S0` is purely a **read-only observer** carried in the hitting problem, not a new state axis in the grammar. The 8G rule compiler is untouched.
8. **First-hitting versus stationary sampling:** on a synthetic model whose first-renewal distribution differs from stationary-conditional mass on R, check that the implemented result follows the former, not the latter.
9. **Hazard decomposition:** if computing return versus exit causes, ensure they are mutually exclusive first-hitting events with a declared treatment of all other transitions.

A separately implemented small-chain enumeration or Monte Carlo trajectory fixture is an excellent independent check; use N≤4 and synthetic cases before freezing. If N=5 fixture is needed for an entry-size issue, **use an explicitly synthetic non-GT law** and disclose it. The N=5 and N=6 E5C science cells are off-limits until the freeze.
