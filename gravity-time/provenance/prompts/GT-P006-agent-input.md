## 0. Governing intent: two complementary lines of inquiry

The human researcher has clarified the programme's orientation. Do not equate a productive foundational detour with a failure to investigate gravity. The programme moves **recursively between a high-level phenomenon of interest and the lower-level relational mechanisms that might make such a phenomenon possible**.

Preserve two distinct but communicating lenses throughout GT-P006.

### Lens A — Ontological and explanatory depth: what might have to exist in between?

Start with the proposed ontological picture of **stochastic relational actualisation under constraints** and the distant motivating question of whether phenomena resembling gravity, temporality, spatiality and motion could arise. Ask:

- What intermediate conceptual or mathematical capabilities might be required to get from this substrate proposal to those phenomena?
- Which capabilities have we already constructed in explicit models, which have been proved mathematically, which have only been observed within a small grammar, and which are still missing?
- Which links are logically necessary under clearly stated definitions, which are one possible construction, and which are merely suggestive analogies?
- Is the most informative next step toward a gravitational model actually a step deeper into admissibility, recoverability, persistence, emergence of reference frames, or something unforeseen?

Treat the proposed substrate as a **research-level ontological starting point**, not as an empirically established physical substrate. The intuition that such a substrate would sit beneath many physical phenomena is a motivating ontological proposal, **not yet a demonstrated theorem of nature**. Do not convert it into an experimental premise or assert that the current models must describe fundamental physics.

### Lens B — Mathematical possibility: what can the specified relational rules generate?

Independently investigate whether relational configurations and local stochastic constraints can mathematically give rise to well-defined emergent behaviours **without naming them in the primitives**. Examples include:

- asymmetric succession;
- admissibility-based release and recovery;
- structural persistence and relational reference formation;
- conditional direction or directed drift;
- and, **in a later suitable model**, an operationally meaningful change in drift/velocity that might deserve the mathematical name *acceleration*.

Be exact about the distinction between (i) constructing an example, (ii) showing it robustly across a family, (iii) proving a general theorem within stated assumptions, and (iv) claiming relevance to physical gravity. An example of a directed event sequence is **not** yet an example of spatial motion or acceleration. A graph-theoretic distance is **not** automatically a physical distance. A jump count is **not** a physical clock.

**The two lenses must constrain and inform one another without being collapsed.** Lens A identifies missing bridges and interprets their relative foundational depth; Lens B establishes or rejects concrete mathematical capabilities. Neither can validate the other's physical interpretation by assertion.

> **Research compass:** Follow whichever foundational mechanism has the greatest likely information value for clarifying the gap, even when it temporarily moves away from gravity. Preserve the gravity–temporality question as the long-range orientation; do not force gravity-like output into a rule.

## 3. E3A — bounded open inquiry into admissibility, one-way release and recoverability

The research neighbourhood is **how local prohibitions and conditional transition rates reshape the space of possible histories**. Select a small, complementary group of cases from existing grammar capabilities to illuminate several live possibilities without optimising for an attractive outcome.

### Questions to make investigable, not binary targets

1. What actually generates and orients the one-way release mode? Is it the endpoint degree, the formation exclusion's incidence, the asymmetry of the reachable-state graph, the stationary weighting of release opportunities, or a combination? Which distinctions survive a change of reference frame or graph labelling?
2. Once a relation has been released and direct reformation is prohibited, **how can it be recovered**? Which other transitions remove the prohibition? What is the chance of recovery, by what routes, and after how many events/units of model time?
3. Which conditions distinguish *temporary non-reformability* from arrest, absorption, transient loss, persistent absence, or a recurrent cycle with alternative return paths?
4. Does hard admissibility produce new forms of conditional **persistence** (of a relation, an absence, a motif, a local reference, or a configuration class)? When is apparent persistence merely holding due to slow rates or statistical selection?
5. Can one-way release exist with minimal or reversed aggregate directional statistics; can the aggregate order be dominated by soft circulation even when local hard one-way events exist? Avoid assuming a positive partner-frame index.
6. Does the system's organisation depend on whether a reference is imposed, emergent, or merely an observer's projection? Include a source-free contrast and, where informative, a carefully identified held-source comparator; do not smuggle a centre.
7. Which observations illuminate the mathematical preconditions for eventual relational **direction, displacement or acceleration** without claiming that the current experiment already measures those quantities?
8. What unexpected support boundaries, forbidden motifs, metastabilities, recoveries, branchings, equivalences, null findings or counterexamples arise beyond the cases we know?

### Recommended first-round coverage, subject to a written coverage audit

- Prefer **FREE N=4/5** where one-way release is already nontrivial; use HELD N=5 only where it serves as an explicit scaffold/witness control. Reserve N=6 for a sharply justified boundary that is unobservable at smaller N. **Do not use FREE N=7** in this prompt.
- Include hard path-formation prohibition (the GT-E2 one-way mode) **but not merely reruns** of every existing E2 grade/cell. Add contrasts that answer **new recovery, support-graph or first-return questions** on published kernels, plus a handful of genuinely new cells where essential.
- Include *at least two* distinct controls where the proposed one-way mechanism is absent or altered: reversible hard exclusion, all-positive-rate soft template, zero-\(\sigma\) composition, and/or an inert path term under matching exclusion, chosen for information value.
- Examine variants that keep a prohibition hard versus vary a **pre-existing** soft factor, being explicit that a weak soft suppression and a hard zero are qualitatively different; do not invent a continuous-parameter limit or new alphabet without a separate methodological proposal.
- Pair structural controls on equivalent state sets, density/edge count, active witness counts, source participation and reachable classes wherever meaningful. Counterbalance cases that arrest or have multiple recurrent classes; analyse support topology before claiming a unique stationary law.
- Only introduce new code or observables after checking whether the same question is already resolved by a simple graph-theoretic argument, existing transition graph, or previously published stationary law.

### Measurement menu (select and freeze a bounded set)

- **Direct support asymmetry:** counts and stationary flux share of directed edges lacking direct reverse; direction by event type, local topology, slot and partner classes. Explicitly distinguish a one-way transition from an absorbing configuration.
- **Recovery geometry:** shortest permitted return path after each one-way release; existence of return paths; lengths, intermediary support changes, and reformation gate-clearing events; strong connected components and transient/recurrent structure.
- **Recovery probabilities/times:** a clearly declared first-passage or competing-event probability to regain the released relation before a specified alternative event or structural transition, finite horizons H; expected return time where mathematically well-defined. Report unconditional and conditional versions separately, including cases with no finite expectation.
- **Kinetic competition:** matched-state rate, jump-probability normalisation/opportunity, strict enabling, observed precedence and race committors (reuse E2 definitions where possible).
- **Direction and persistence:** event-order asymmetries, partner/source/hub frames, full rate current versus jump event order; motif/autocorrelation or holding times; compare with nulls and role-permutation controls.
- **Structure:** density and conditional composition, degree distribution, witnesses engaged, local motifs, support reachability, weak/strong connectivity, source effects and saturation.
- **Interpretive safeguards:** physical/ontological status, rare-mass flags, clock and projection dependence, counterexamples and surprising nulls.

Use exact finite-state computations when feasible, but do not force the word "exact" to mean arbitrary-precision arithmetic where validated numerical linear algebra is what is used. Independently referee key claims with exact enumeration, rational rates, a second kernel, combinatorial proof, or explicit finite-state path checks, as appropriate. Measure costs before choosing scope.

### Limit the scale

Target approximately **12–40 informative E3A analyses**, counting already published kernels with genuinely new frozen observables where required; a different count is acceptable if justified in advance. Avoid repeated equivalent cells. Prefer a single ordinary working session with reasonable memory bounds; set an explicit wall-clock and per-cell cap in the frozen plan based on measured feasibility. Do not enlarge N solely to accumulate evidence for the preferred mechanism.

- **Runtime:** Keep experiments within explicit declared budgets and bounded workers/memory; write cells atomically and resume safely. Do not redo huge E1/E2 analyses without a clear reason. Preserve failed cases and fixes in provenance.
- **Provenance:** All new diagrams, reports, code, tests, scripts and outputs must reproduce from committed inputs; no untracked essential result. Run the relevant test suite and check GitHub Actions. Record any incomplete CI results as such.
