# Open-Thetical Research (OTR)

**Status:** Working methodological definition, authored for reuse across the Relational Substrate research programmes.  
**Version:** 1.1 — 31 October 2026 (additive; v1.0 of 14 October 2026 is unchanged in §§1–8, and §9 records the additions)  
**Scope:** General research method; not a physical theory, experimental result, or change to the canonical RS ontology.

## 1. Definition

**Open-thetical research** is a *coherence-guided, recursively adaptive method of inquiry*. It starts with a meaningful domain of interest and several incomplete candidate explanations, intuitions, or open questions. It chooses a bounded family of experiments **because they could illuminate relationships among those possibilities**, without requiring any particular hypothesis to be confirmed or any predetermined phenomenon to appear. It observes broadly, distinguishes genuine mechanisms from artefacts, updates a multidimensional coherence map, and uses what was learned to select the next experiment.

The term *open-thetical* is a project-coined methodological label, **not an established category of scientific method**. It has affinities with exploratory modelling, abductive inference, active learning and adaptive experimental design, but adds an explicit concern for recursive global coherence across explanatory levels.

Its objective is **convergence in understanding**, which may mean narrowing, splitting, revising or expanding the space of possibilities; it is *not* necessarily convergence toward confirmation of an initially favoured answer.

## 2. The distinction

| Mode | Main question | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirmatory hypothesis testing | Is this specified prediction borne out? | Essential for later discrimination and validation |
| Unbounded exploration | What happens anywhere in this possibility space? | Can reveal novelty, but may spend effort far from the motivating problem |
| **Open-thetical research** | What bounded inquiry is likely to clarify this cluster of possibilities, including unknown possibilities? | Directed discovery without outcome-fitting |

Open-thetical questions are typically **open**: “What happens when persistent relational structures interact with locally conditional transition rules?” rather than “Does rule R produce a gravitational basin?”

An inchoate candidate need not already be operationally falsifiable to motivate exploration. As claims mature, they should acquire precise meanings and discriminating tests; openness is **not** exemption from evidence.

## 3. Two different senses of bias

**Admissible research-direction bias:** Choose domains, starting conditions, observables and rule families that are plausibly *informative about the subject*. For gravity–temporality, the neighbourhood might include persistent configurations, conditional attachment/dissolution, constrained stochastic succession, relational distance and recurrent events.

**Inadmissible result bias:** Encode the desired basin, force, direction, clock law or other answer into primitive rules, discard unpromising results, change thresholds after inspecting data, or treat resemblance to the intended phenomenon as independent corroboration.

A held structure, a free system, an imposed boundary or a clock convention may be used as an **explicit experimental instrument**; their consequences must be separated from what actually emerges.

## 4. The recursive inquiry cycle

1. **Orient:** State the domain, its motivating observations, multiple candidate explanations and unresolved concepts. Include incomplete and competing ideas without presenting them as settled hypotheses.
2. **Choose for information value:** Select a small, complementary set of experiments whose possible outcomes could clarify *several* candidate accounts. Make meaningful contrasts (e.g. conditional formation, conditional retention, hard exclusion, mixed constraints; held and free systems).
3. **Predeclare and freeze:** Specify the grammar cell, selection and sampling process, initial states, observables, controls, clock conventions, computational budget, provenance and stopping rules **before** running the actual scientific cells.
4. **Explore and observe:** Record structural, dynamical and temporal behaviour comprehensively, including null, weak, fragile, confounded, unexpected and ordinary observations. Preserve representative and atypical trajectories.
5. **Explain and challenge:** Investigate which mechanisms produce each observation and whether density, symmetry, boundary, projection, estimator bias, rate normalisation or representation explain it instead.
6. **Update global coherence:** Record findings, their strength, limitations, dependencies, contradictions and conceptual implications without collapsing them to one score. Keep observations, mathematical results, inferences, ontological interpretations and theological premises in their appropriate categories.
7. **Reflect recursively:** Ask what became clearer; what was contradicted; what surprising questions appeared; and which minimal change in rule selection, conditions, or observation would now yield the most information.
8. **Freeze the next round:** Write a new independent plan reflecting the preceding result; preserve the distinction between exploratory observations, post-hoc inferences and separately frozen confirmation tests. Repeat only while the expected information value justifies the cost.

An exploratory programme should normally include **more than one informed round**, not merely a one-off atlas followed by an arbitrary expansion. An honest early stop is appropriate when the domain is exhausted, infeasible or no further informative experiment is justified.

## 5. Coherence standards

- **Direction without foreclosure:** Let the research question constrain *where to look*, not *what must be found*.
- **Plural explanations:** Preserve distinct models and partially formed possibilities; a useful observation may illuminate several at once.
- **Emergence without smuggling:** Declare every external scaffold, named source, spatial metric, clock, target observable and rule condition. Do not report imposed structure as discovered structure.
- **Layer distinction:** A result proven for a finite stochastic model is not by itself evidence that the physical world uses that mechanism.
- **Negative results remain live:** Small effects, failed predictions and fragility can illuminate conditions under which structures do and do not emerge.
- **Comparative rigor:** Use nulls, matched controls, symmetry/equivalence checks and distinct clock/projection conventions when relevant.
- **Scientific honesty under adaptation:** Each new empirical round must be frozen independently. When an experiment is chosen because of a previous surprising result, record the provenance of that choice.
- **Shared method, independent programmes:** Constraint Laboratory and Gravity–Temporality may use the same methodology and exchange findings while keeping their own objectives, plans, results and provenance.

## 6. How to select the next experiment

Do **not** ask only “Which experiment is most likely to confirm our best hypothesis?”

Instead ask:

> “Among feasible experiments, which would most clarify the relations among our live possibilities, expose hidden assumptions, or reveal a new kind of behaviour—regardless of whether the immediate result is positive or negative?”

Factors to compare *separately*: likely information value, novelty of mechanism coverage, contrast with prior results, ability to distinguish artefacts, interpretability, cost, robustness and cross-programme coherence. These factors need not be compressed into an opaque single numerical ranking.

A next round may (a) adjust constraint combinations; (b) remove or add an openly declared scaffold; (c) study a surprising transition or failure; (d) change an observable; (e) enlarge a system only after demonstrating the smaller one cannot answer the question; or (f) switch to a confirmatory test when a specific prediction finally merits one.

## 7. Shared use across the two programmes

**Constraint Laboratory:** Explore the expressive capacity and relational consequences of primitive constraint grammars, including unexpected behaviour with no immediate physical interpretation. Choose successive cells according to discoveries, the formal expressivity boundary and the coherence register.

**Gravity, Temporality and Emergent Spatiality (GT):** Remain **gravity-motivated** when selecting informative configurations—persistent structures, relational basins, conditional formation and dissolution, emergent asymmetry, recurrence, direction and distance—but observe without requiring any gravity-like outcome. Maintain the freedom to follow unexpected mechanisms and compare them with known physical measurements later.

## 8. Concise memory-ready statement

> **Open-Thetical Research (OTR):** My preferred research method is coherence-guided, recursively adaptive exploration. Begin with a meaningful inquiry direction and multiple incomplete ideas rather than a single prediction. Select bounded, complementary constraints and experiments for their potential to clarify several possibilities, not to confirm a chosen answer. Observe broadly—including weak, null, unexpected and fragile behaviour—then assess mechanisms, artefacts and global coherence. Update a connected evidence/coherence map and adapt the next predeclared experiment according to what was learned. The aim is convergence in understanding, not necessarily confirmation. Apply this shared method to both the Constraint Laboratory and the Gravity–Temporality research programme, preserving their independent results and provenance.

### 9.1 Methodological plurality

Investigate with whatever method the question requires: logical analysis, computation and experiment, mathematical modelling, historical evidence, theological reasoning, or disciplined reflection on experience. Do not require every question to submit to one method. Record which method supplied which warrant, because warrant differs in kind (formal, empirical, historical, philosophical, theological) and must not be silently converted from one kind to another.

### 9.2 Relational error anatomy (extends §4 step 5)

When a candidate fails, do not record only "rejected". Record:

- which asserted relation failed, and at which inferential step;
- which component relations remain valid and reusable;
- which previously assumed relations are now excluded or constrained;
- whether the failure corrects the question or its assumptions as well as the answer.

Intelligible error depends on genuine coherence, so a failed candidate usually contains sound parts. This does not license treating a false relation as true because its surroundings are intelligible.

### 9.3 Independent-convergence test (extends §4 step 6)

Before counting agreement among lines of inquiry as support, test their independence:

- independence of originating premises;
- independence of evidential sources;
- independence of inferential routes;
- shared assumptions or methodological dependencies;
- whether the agreement survives an alternative representation;
- whether it could have been produced by feedback, summarisation or common training.

Separate AI models, sessions or agents are **not** independent evidence by default: they share training material and may have seen one another's output. Agreement produced by repeated merging of summaries is the "false attractor" pattern and is recorded as such.

### 9.4 Admission of coherence factors (new step between §4 steps 6 and 7)

A relationship may be admitted for reuse in later reasoning, as a **coherence factor**, only with the following recorded:

- its statement and scope (where it is warranted to apply, and where not);
- its provenance (rounds, sources, methods) and the kind of warrant it has;
- its dependencies on other factors and assumptions;
- its known uncertainties and live alternatives;
- its review and retraction triggers.

Admission is judged on a **multidimensional vector**: warrant, coherence, provenance, discrimination, scope and dependencies, each assessed separately, with an explicit written rationale. **No universal numerical threshold or single scalar score is set**, and none should be inferred from a vector's values. A factor is reusable only within the scope its evidence and assumptions warrant. Scores produced by AI analysis are triage aids, not evidence of truth.

### 9.5 Memory-ready addendum

> **OTR 1.1:** Use the method the question needs; analyse failures into the relation that failed and the parts that survive; test agreement for real independence before counting it (separate AIs are not independent by default); and admit a coherence factor only with its scope, provenance, dependencies, uncertainties and review triggers recorded, judged on several dimensions together, with no universal numerical threshold.
