
This is a discriminating experiment (Mode A). It is not a test of whether a higher-order relation is ontologically basic. A fit on two aggregate numbers is not an identity of models.

## V. Inferences

The one-parameter pair-face gap from Generation 7 is closed by embedding the unconditional scalar inside a two-level law, not by a pair-conditioned split. The complement does independent work only as a second copy of the same multiplier.

Two diagonal roots can match the same aggregate vector and disagree about `I_C2`. Composition is then a property of the selected representation, and the selection rule is nominal distance, which was frozen before the margins were read.

State shape and triad selectivity do not follow the aggregate match. Extra lower-order parameter slots, set equal at the solution, buy the two passage numbers Generation 7's erasure law already bought. They do not buy the internal transition structure.

Rho moves the required multiplier. The representation at the primary rho is sensitive to that change unless the control is recalibrated. On the six probed cells, recalibration preserves the selected `M_I_2` sign.

## W. RS / global-coherence interpretation

The higher-order representation is more compact on the tested observable family: one predicate of order 3, one rule, one named weight, and a selectivity the pairwise control does not carry. The lower-order representation requires two rule slots and two parameter slots to emulate the aggregate effect, and then uses those slots as one unconditional weight.

That compactness is an observation about this observable family. It does not prove a higher-order ontology. It does not show that the higher-order relation is reducible. Representation on the two aggregate passage numbers is not ontological identity. The state-shape error and the triad selectivity are the differences that remain after the aggregate emulation.

## Y. Future open-exploration fork

Mode B remains available after this control sequence stops earning explanatory compression. A future branch can ask what happens if a relation or constraint is added, rather than whether a control matches a predeclared vector.

That experiment would start from a minimal state and constraint grammar, allow bounded additions and removals, run replayable stochastic trajectories, record every change and every random choice, and compare accumulated patterns for coherence. The absence of an expected phenomenon would be an observation. Multiple causes would be part of the structure to disentangle later, if useful. This generation documents that requirement. It does not implement the engine.
