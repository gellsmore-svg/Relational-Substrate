## 1. Domain

The Relational Substrate programmes study constrained stochastic relational actualisation. RRC asks a narrower mathematical question:

Can complex relational structures be understood as recursively composed systems of smaller relational structures, and are there discoverable limits on the relational information needed to preserve their consequential behaviour?

This is an exploratory investigation of finite relational models. It is not an attempt to prove a physical constant, a preferred integer, or an ontological claim.

## 2. What this charter refuses in advance

Six is one candidate size inside the inquiry. It is not a hypothesis the programme is organised to confirm.

Four different "six" claims stay separate, and none is assumed:

1. Six constituent nodes inside one structure.
2. Six edges along a path, which needs at least seven nodes on a simple path.
3. Six levels of recursive composition.
4. Six units of consequentially relevant context.

A result about one of these is not a result about the others.

The programme also refuses to treat a small local grammar as, by itself, a bounded description of the systems that grammar generates.

## 3. Cycle

Orient, select, freeze, calculate, observe, challenge, integrate, reflect, continue.

Each scientific round is frozen, committed and pushed before its cells run. A later round is chosen only after the earlier round is recorded. An early stop is allowed when further cells would not repay their cost.

Execution is a second cycle: plan, one short item, verify, persist, checkpoint, resume. A round may contain many items. No item is an indivisible computation expected to exceed five minutes. The ordinary watchdog is 120 seconds. Completed items survive a crash. Failed and timed-out items stay failed or unresolved. Their parameters are not edited so that a different experiment can wear the same name.

## 4. Layers

Every written result keeps these apart:

1. A mathematical fact inside the declared model, including an exact identity.
2. An observation produced by a frozen computation.
3. An inference about that model.
4. A representation effect or numerical artefact.
5. An RS or global-coherence interpretation.
6. An ontological or theological possibility.

Layers 5 and 6 are not evidence. Design, if it appears in the wider repository, is an upstream premise and is not tested here.

Pass and fail are reserved for software, validation and execution contracts. No model is ranked by a single score.

## 5. Independence

RRC keeps its own charter, questions, plans, engine, results, provenance and coherence ledger under `relational-complexity/`.

It may read the Constraint Laboratory and Gravity–Temporality documents at the pins in `provenance/base-commits.md`. It does not edit those programmes, their frozen plans, or their ledgers. It does not edit `books/`.

Shared synthesis lives on `infra/shared-research-coherence-v1` and is not on this branch. Intake of `RRC::` is a proposal (`docs/synthesis-proposal.md`), not a silent edit of the federation.

## 6. Compilation gate

Before RRC calls anything a new expressive mechanism, it applies the laboratory gate recorded in `constraint-lab/docs/scientific-plan-discipline.md` at commit `1b9d93d5`:

Can the behaviour be represented by increasing rule complexity, cardinality, or explicit current state inside the existing single-toggle grammar?

If it can, RRC records the trade-off. The opening decision is in `docs/compilation-gate.md`. Structural composition, interface reduction and recursive grammar questions in the first round are questions about reductions of that grammar, not new dynamical primitives.
